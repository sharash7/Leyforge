# Document Purpose

Document 20E defines the civilisation-capability layer that turns knowledge, resources, named specialists, machines and magic into settlement production, protection, research, travel and regional influence. It explains what makes a magical or industrial facility functional, how energy and resources move, how specialists and constructed workers cooperate, how powerful systems remain fair and readable, and how settlements escalate from simple mechanisms and a village mage to portals, regional grids and dimensional containment.

The document separates function from art and runtime state. 20E defines what a mana store, ward relay, power house, factory, ritual hall or portal station must accomplish. Blueprint Forge defines the official editable voxel source, semantic graph and state deltas. Document 19 provides restricted player-compatible authoring. The runtime owns live energy, inventories, recipes, research, staff, destination links, faults, contamination, damage and history.

The goal is depth without a spreadsheet-only factory game or menu-only magic system. Machines and magic exist physically in the voxel world and expose clear causes such as **No Input**, **No Power**, **Low Mana**, **Blocked Output**, **Missing Specialist**, **Unsafe Heat**, **Impure Charge**, **Ward Breach** or **Destination Unstable**. NPCs perform routine operation and maintenance; players intervene at bottlenecks, projects, discoveries, laws, accidents and strategic choices.

# 1. Locked 20E Identity

20E is the practical magic, industrial escalation and regional-infrastructure layer of the universal building registry. It provides physical structures that turn resources, knowledge, specialists and controlled energy into larger production, protection, travel and civilisation-scale capability. It does not replace the core settlement economy; it expands what that economy can do.

> **Locked Rule**
>
> A 20E facility provides only the production, protection, energy, research, labour or travel capacity validated from its source binding, semantic elements, typed networks, staff, knowledge, inventories, ownership, safety and condition. Visual scale, animation, particles or glowing materials never grant functional output by themselves.

| **Identity Layer** | **Design Meaning** | **Player-Facing Result** |
|---|---|---|
| Practical magic | Mana, runes, wards, rituals, healing, portals and leylines become buildable and maintainable infrastructure. | Magic supports settlement life rather than existing only in combat menus. |
| Conserved production | Machines and industry transform exact inputs into exact outputs and by-products. | Automation scales work without duplicating resources. |
| Visible energy | Sources, transmission, storage, controls and consumers have physical states and feedback. | Players can diagnose why a network works or fails. |
| Named expertise | Mages, engineers, alchemists, operators and maintainers remain persistent NPCs with jobs and succession. | Advanced capability creates social roles instead of anonymous bonuses. |
| Risk and choice | Heat, pressure, pollution, corruption, overload, breach, labour displacement and forbidden paths have counterplay. | Powerful growth creates meaningful decisions and consequences. |
| Realm connection | Portals and dimensional facilities bind to persistent destinations, hazards, permissions and supplies. | Other realms become part of civilisation and story rather than disposable levels. |
| Scalable simulation | Nearby systems operate visibly; distant systems preserve bounded authoritative summaries and history. | Cities and multiple realms remain feasible without false production. |

# 2. Scope Boundaries and Anti-Micromanagement Rules

- The seven needs remain the only top-level needs.
- Power and mana are network resources and service capacities, not happiness-style needs.
- Research and magical schools are progression systems, not mandatory bars for every settlement.
- NPCs perform routine operation, refuelling, maintenance, tuning and safe shutdown according to jobs and policy. The player intervenes at bottlenecks, projects, discoveries, accidents, laws and strategic choices.
- Nearby item and energy flow may be represented visually, but authoritative totals use bounded data transactions.
- Machines do not require real-world engineering simulation. They require readable inputs, outputs, energy, buffers, speed, safety, condition and recipe state.
- Mechanical, industrial and magical branches coexist. Mana does not automatically replace water, wind, fuel, tools or NPC labour.
- Not every settlement needs magic, steam, portals, golems or factories. Culture, geography, specialists, law, research, threat and player choice determine development.
- Catastrophic failures are limited to ignored warnings, harsh settings, unstable materials, forbidden systems, attacks or major events. Normal equipment warns, throttles and shuts down.
- Distant settlements do not simulate every shaft rotation, spell particle or golem step. They preserve energy, resources, work, risk and consequences through summaries.
- Player-made sources receive no capacity from appearance or visual size. Functional elements, real components and valid networks define service.
- Full continuous fluid, heat, pressure, electromagnetic or mana-particle simulation remains outside Version 0.2.

| **Explicit Non-Goals** |
|---|
| 20E does not require real electrical engineering, continuous mana particles, manual shift scheduling, individual machine programming, mandatory factory ratios, automatic replacement of villagers, one building per spell school, or portals that ignore destination state and ownership. |

## 3.1 Separation Rules

- A universal definition describes function, requirements and planner meaning; it does not contain a finished voxel layout.
- Blueprint Forge source contains editable art and semantic intent; it does not own live world resources, research progress or energy totals.
- Runtime bake products are disposable and reproducible from approved source and registry versions.
- Construction projects record real consumed resources and labour; completion creates or changes persistent instances.
- Persistent instances own live energy, items, workers, connected graphs, condition, risk, damage and history.
- Player-authored sources cannot change stable universal IDs, deposit reserves, energy units, recipe outputs, portal destination authority, migration aliases or server rules.

# 5. Shared Magic, Power, Machine and Realm Model

## 5.1 Capability Chain

```text
Site or Source
    -> Extraction / Generation
    -> Transmission / Routing
    -> Storage / Buffer
    -> Control / Permission
    -> Consumer / Recipe / Ritual
    -> Output / Service / Travel
    -> Waste / Risk / Maintenance
```

Examples:

- River flow -> waterwheel -> shaft -> mill -> processed grain.
- Fuel and water -> steam plant -> grid -> factory -> machine components and heat/waste.
- Mana crystal -> refinement -> battery -> conduit -> ward -> bounded protection and drain.
- Leyline -> relay -> regional control -> portal station -> travel capacity and stabilisation demand.

## 5.2 Common Capacity Calculations

| **Capability** | **Calculation Direction** | **Player-Facing Example** |
|---|---|---|
| Production throughput | Recipe base x machine/module x energy x staffing x condition x input/output availability. | Mana furnace: six iron per minute; output blocked. |
| Power service | Minimum of generation, transmission, storage discharge and control allocation compared with active demand. | Mechanical network strained: 82/90 units. |
| Mana service | Accepted quantity and purity through valid storage/conduits, modified by stabilisation and risk. | Ward reserve: nine hours at current threat. |
| Ward coverage | Anchor profile x charge x rune quality x relay links x tuning, reduced by interference and damage. | Northern ward: 74% coverage; relay damaged. |
| Research capacity | Specialist time x valid stations x knowledge/material access x safety. | Portal research paused: missing stabilised shard. |
| Portal throughput | Frame/anchor x energy x stability x operator x destination availability x cooldown. | Destination closed: storm in target realm. |
| Golem labour | Valid constructs x charge/fuel x task profile x route/access x maintenance and law. | Three construction golems; one charging. |
| Risk | Base hazard x load x impurity x damage x environment x forbidden modifiers, reduced by safety and staff. | Foundry heat risk strained; cooling low. |

## 5.3 Capacity Does Not Equal Free Output

A facility can have high theoretical capacity and produce nothing when input, energy, staffing, knowledge, permission, destination or output space is missing. The UI shows the current limiting cause first. Capacity is not credited twice when one shared source or buffer serves multiple consumers.

## 6.5 Contract Rules

- Every semantic element has a stable local ID and contract version.
- Sockets declare type, direction, capacity, ownership, accepted tags and connection rules.
- Graph elements do not create energy or resources; they route, transform, store or consume authoritative state.
- A converter declares input type, output type, efficiency, capacity, by-products and risk.
- Coverage and line-of-effect are baked from approved geometry and semantic elements, then modified by persistent runtime state.
- Critical facilities require safe maintenance and shutdown access.
- Missing optional semantics produce warnings; missing required semantics block approval or commissioning.
- Entity Forge body envelopes and equipment clearances are considered for operators, mounts, golems and emergency routes.

# 7. Mana, Purity, Storage, Conduits and Wards

## 7.1 Physical Mana Chain

Raw Mana Crystal -> Mana Shard -> Mana Dust or Refined Charge -> Rune/Core/Battery -> Conduit/Device -> Service

Mana can also come from approved realm resources, leyline infrastructure or culture-specific methods. All sources declare purity, stability, capacity, risk and accepted uses.

## 7.2 Mana Quality

- Stable/Clean.
- Mixed.
- Impure.
- Unstable.
- Corrupted.
- Forbidden or realm-specific.

Quality can influence efficiency, ritual stability, output quality, corruption risk, maintenance and faction response. The player sees quality bands and causes rather than hidden random failure.

## 7.3 Mana Storage and Transmission

- Storage capacity derives from valid vessels or batteries.
- Charge/discharge rate is separate from total capacity.
- Conduits have throughput, range/loss, ownership and damage.
- Incompatible mana types require separation, purification or stabilisation.
- Networks can prioritise wards, health, industry, portals or emergency reserve.
- Normal overload warns, throttles and shuts down. Unstable/forbidden systems may breach under ignored warnings.

## 7.4 Wards

Ward profiles may protect against creature classes, corruption, weather, intrusion, portal effects or specific magic. Coverage is not universal armour. Each ward declares area, accepted threats, mana cost, counterplay, relay rules and failure state.

## 8.2 Common Power Contract

Every source declares:

- Power type and output.
- Fuel/source condition.
- Minimum and maximum safe load.
- Transmission type.
- Storage compatibility.
- Heat, waste, noise or magical side effects.
- Shutdown and overload response.
- Maintenance and operator requirements.
- Ownership and priority permissions.

## 8.3 Mixed Networks

Mechanical, industrial and mana networks may interact through converter or hybrid devices, but one energy type should not silently power every consumer. Conversion has efficiency, cost, capacity and risk.

# 9. Machines, Recipes, Throughput and Automation

- Machines use the shared Recipe Registry and add time, batch size, power, buffers, ports, modules, maintenance and risk.
- Simple machines can auto-configure common input/output sides. Advanced machines expose side/port controls.
- A blocked output normally fills an internal buffer and pauses safely.
- Batching is recipe- and machine-specific.
- Quality can be affected by material quality, station quality, specialist skill, maintenance and magic.
- Machine upgrades include speed, efficiency, quality, filter, power, durability, range, safety, stability and rune slots.
- Automation supports NPC labour: it reduces repetitive hauling and processing, creates operators/engineers and stabilises supply. It does not remove builders, farmers, miners, mages, merchants or maintainers.
- Distant production uses the same input/output transaction totals as visible production.

# 10. Industry, Heat, Pressure, Waste and Environment

Industrial facilities can generate:

- Heat and fire risk.
- Pressure and explosion risk.
- Smoke, ash, dust or fumes.
- Slag, scrap, polluted water or hazardous residue.
- Noise and creature attraction.
- Land damage, subsidence or over-extraction.
- Mana leakage, corruption or dimensional residue.
- High strategic value and raid/sabotage pressure.

These effects are configurable and must have counterplay: filters, cooling, maintenance, safer recipes, distance, containment, recycling, wards, specialist staffing and shutdown. Not every machine needs pollution, and normal infrastructure should not constantly fail for flavour.

# 11. Specialists, NPC Labour, Golems and Social Consequences

## 11.1 Specialist Families

- Miner and extraction engineer.
- Metallurgist, foundry worker and smith.
- Machinist, mechanic and power engineer.
- Operator, dispatcher and maintenance worker.
- Mage, rune specialist, warder and mana technician.
- Alchemist, healer and cleansing practitioner.
- Ritualist and culture/religious specialist.
- Magical researcher, leyline scholar and portal keeper.
- Dimensional scholar and containment specialist.
- Golemancer/controller.

## 11.2 Staffing Rules

Skill affects speed, quality, waste, stability, teaching and emergency response. Low skill should usually reduce efficiency rather than cause arbitrary catastrophe. Critical facilities can require minimum specialist coverage.

## 11.3 Golems

Golems use task profiles, inventories, charge/fuel, allowed zones, ownership, priorities and emergency shutdown. They are specialised workers, not universally superior villagers. Culture, law, employment, religion, scarcity and player behaviour affect acceptance.

Forbidden labour branches - necromantic workers, blood engines, corruption constructs or forced labour - require explicit law, morality, fear, rebellion and faction consequences and are mainly culture/faction branches in 20G.

# 12. Knowledge, Research, Laws, Ownership and Forbidden Paths

## 12.1 Knowledge Gates

Buildings and recipes may require:

- NPC teaching.
- Books, ruins or research notes.
- Experimentation.
- Faction trust or trade.
- Boss or event outcomes.
- Culture traditions.
- Realm discoveries.
- Settlement stage and specialist institutions.

Level alone does not grant every magical or industrial capability.

## 12.2 Ownership and Authority

Facilities support personal, household, workplace, settlement, guild, faction, government, shared multiplayer and restricted ownership. Networks and outputs cannot bypass permissions. Public wards and grids may use civic priorities; private facilities may require contracts or fees.

## 12.3 Laws and Forbidden Systems

Settlements can regulate:

- Hazardous industry near homes or water.
- Mana storage and portal operation.
- Summoning and realm contact.
- Blood, necromantic, shadow, corruption or void practices.
- Golem labour and constructed beings.
- Strategic resource extraction.
- Weapon and siege production.
- Environmental damage and waste.

Illegal operation can create witnesses, investigation, fines, closure, confiscation, fear, faction anger, sabotage or rebellion rather than merely a red UI icon.

# 13. Portals, Dimensions, Realm Materials and Containment

- A portal binds to persistent origin and destination anchors. It is not a generic teleport effect.
- Destination state matters: danger, ownership, weather, siege, corruption, unloaded simulation and route closure.
- Portal stations require arrival clearance, permissions, energy, stabilisation, cooldown and emergency shutdown.
- Freight transfers preserve ownership and exact inventory quantities.
- Realm materials may require cleansing, cooling, binding, purification, shielding or quarantine before ordinary use.
- Major dimensions have their own societies, resources, hazards and infrastructure. 20E structures connect them to settlements without replacing the full Dimension System.
- Containment facilities separate intake, study, storage, decontamination and emergency response.
- Full dimensional research and portal networks are late-game conditional systems, not required for ordinary villages.

## 14.1 Risk Families

## 14.2 Fair Failure Rule

## 14.3 Recovery Options

- Shut down and isolate.
- Reroute power/mana or logistics.
- Use stored reserve.
- Repair or replace component.
- Purify or cleanse stock.
- Quarantine workers/materials.
- Retune runes or wards.
- Close destination.
- Evacuate and contain.
- Rebuild, downgrade or convert.
- Investigate sabotage or law breach.

# 18. Construction Stages, Commissioning and Partial Activation

## 18.1 Shared Stage Vocabulary

1. Planning, survey and source/site claim.
2. Site preparation, hazard exclusion and temporary access.
3. Foundation, supports or containment shell.
4. Structural frame and enclosure.
5. Machine, ritual, ward, portal or production installation.
6. Energy, mana, logistics, cooling and waste connections.
7. Controls, safety, storage and maintenance access.
8. Furnishing, tools, reagents and specialist setup.
9. Testing, tuning, attunement and commissioning.
10. Operational completion.

Small facilities merge stages. Large factories, grids and dimensional complexes split them into independently saved subprojects.

## 18.2 Partial Activation

- A mundane furnace may operate before a later mana branch is installed.
- A waterwheel may power one connected mill before a larger shaft network exists.
- A mana store may accept limited charge before full distribution opens.
- A ward sector may protect its local coverage after anchor and control pass testing.
- A magic academy may teach basic courses before advanced research wings open.
- A factory cell may operate while later production wings are unfinished.
- A portal never accepts travellers until both origin and destination safety contracts pass.
- A dimensional facility never activates hazardous containment without redundant critical systems.

## 18.3 Commissioning

Commissioning verifies source/site binding, inventory and energy state, typed networks, controls, staff, knowledge, permissions, safety, shutdown, maintenance, initial destination or coverage, and runtime bake version. Failed checks create explicit blockers rather than silently reducing output.

# 20. Damage, Corruption, Occupation, Repair and Restoration

## 20.1 Damage and Hazard Families

- Structural, machine, control or transmission damage.
- Heat, fire, pressure, rupture, moving-part or electrical-like fault.
- Fuel, reagent, mana, cooling or output loss.
- Impurity, contamination, corruption or dimensional residue.
- Ward interference, depletion or breach.
- Portal misalignment, destination hazard or quarantine failure.
- Sabotage, theft, hostile occupation or unlawful operation.
- Golem damage, control fault, impoundment or revolt/personhood dispute.
- Environmental pollution, subsidence, over-extraction or realm instability.

## 20.2 Functional Consequence

Damage invalidates only affected elements where possible. A factory can lose one cell without deleting other production. A ward breach affects explicit coverage links. A portal may close while its station and records remain. A mana store isolates one failed cell. Contamination propagates only through defined connections, transfers and events.

## 20.3 Corruption and Cleansing

Corruption is a material, mana, environment or state modifier, not a separate settlement need. It records source, affected elements, spread rules, accepted countermeasures, law/faction consequences and residual history. Cleansing consumes valid labour, items, mana, time and safe disposal capacity; it does not simply toggle a visual overlay.

## 20.4 Occupation and Authority Change

Occupation can change access, production priority, research permission, grid allocation, portal destinations, construct control and strategic stock without rewriting the source blueprint. Origin culture, builder, former owners, legal history and incidents remain preserved.

## 20.5 Repair and Restoration

Repair projects reference stable source elements, exact resources, specialists, safe access and shutdown state. Temporary patches may restore reduced capacity. Restoration can return the original source, apply an approved child source, cleanse corruption, convert the facility, preserve a ruin or expose a new story state.

# 28. Balancing, Diagnostics and Anti-Exploit Rules

## 28.1 Balance Principles

- Manual and early mechanical systems remain useful through low cost, flexibility, repairability and independence.
- Advanced systems improve capacity, consistency or capability but require specialists, resources, networks, maintenance, safety and political acceptance.
- Automation reduces repetitive labour; it does not erase meaningful NPC work or social consequences.
- Magic is powerful but physical, scarce, learnable, regulated and exposed to counterplay.
- Catastrophic failures are unusual outcomes of ignored warnings, forbidden systems, sabotage, major events or harsh settings.
- Exact throughput, energy, mana, risk, research and travel values remain external balance data.
- Culture, biome and realm solutions may differ without one universal best technology path.

## 28.2 Anti-Exploit Rules

- Decorative machines, runes, crystals, portals and animated parts provide no capacity.
- Hidden or unreachable markers do not count.
- Energy, charge and items cannot exist in source and destination simultaneously during transfer.
- Converters cannot output more authoritative value than allowed by input, efficiency and recipe.
- One source cannot be connected repeatedly to multiply output or coverage.
- Overlapping ward zones do not stack beyond explicit profile rules.
- Copying a portal frame does not copy a destination anchor or permission.
- Player sources cannot declare unsupported deposit yield, energy generation, mana storage, research or construct labour.
- Distant and local simulation reconcile at promotion/demotion boundaries.
- Save/load cannot reroll recipe, ritual, research, portal or failure outcomes after authoritative commitment.

## 28.3 Diagnostic Standard

Diagnostics identify severity, source element, graph or inventory involved, affected capability, suggested correction and whether the issue blocks source approval, construction, commissioning or runtime operation.

Example messages:

- `D20E_POWER_SOURCE_UNBOUND - Renewable generator has no authoritative water-flow or wind-exposure binding.`
- `D20E_MANA_TYPE_REJECTED - Storage vessel does not accept the connected mana profile.`
- `D20E_PORTAL_DESTINATION_INVALID - Destination anchor is missing, closed or incompatible.`
- `D20E_CONTAINMENT_REDUNDANCY_FAIL - Critical containment wing has no independent shutdown path.`

- rune execution;

An automation/industrial facility may provide:

- machine floor capacity;
- machine sockets;
- operator/service access;
- input/output buffers;
- safety zones;
- maintenance access;
- utility/power endpoints;
- logistics interfaces.

Document 08 owns the machine/network execution.

Set 29 owns biological work/hazard consequences.

## C. Power Infrastructure

Power facilities must preserve conservation.

Conceptually:

```text
authoritative fuel/source
→ valid generator/converter
→ owned power-network execution
→ transmission/distribution
→ compatible consumer
→ losses/by-products
```

20E cannot create abstract free power because a generator building exists.

Where power is magical, Document 09 owns magical energy rules.

Where power is machine/technical, Document 08 owns execution/network behaviour.

## D. Dimensional Infrastructure

A portal/dimensional building can provide a valid physical anchor/service only.

- valid destination/realm from Document 14;

- required resources/fuel;

- safe containment;
- permission/progression;

## E. Economy and Industry

Set 27 owns:

- enterprise/business state;
- industrial profitability;
- wages;
- procurement;
- energy/fuel prices;
- sale value;
- contracts;
- finance.

20E owns facility capacity, not profitability.

## F. Knowledge and Social State

A mage academy, arcane laboratory or dimensional observatory may expose instruction/research opportunity.

Document 02 owns progression outcomes.

Set 28C owns individual knowledge/belief/information state.

Set 28 owns social willingness and acceptance.

20E may expose:

- heat;
- pressure;

- toxic by-products;
- explosive risk;
