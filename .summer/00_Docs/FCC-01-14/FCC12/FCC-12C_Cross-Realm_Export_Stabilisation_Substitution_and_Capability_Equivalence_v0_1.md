# 1. Purpose

FCC-12C defines how canonical materials behave when they leave their realm of origin, enter another realm, depend on specialist environmental or magical conditions, require stabilisation, become hazardous, or substitute for another material in a functional role.

The governing principle is:

> **Physical matter persists across realms. Specialist behaviour depends on actual physical, magical, legal or environmental requirements — not on realm coordinates alone.**

FCC-12C establishes:

- identity persistence after export;
- ordinary physical-property persistence;
- specialist-property persistence;
- dormancy;
- stabilisation;
- managed environmental support;
- hazardous containment;
- living-material export;
- cross-realm processing;
- provider tags;
- exact-material versus capability-provider requirements;
- substitution thresholds;
- portal-material provider boundaries;
- no-wrong-realm lockout;
- legitimate progression sequence breaking;
- FCC-13 handoff.

FCC-12C does not define exact portal recipes, recipe quantities, stable IDs, stabiliser machine designs, hazard numbers, container capacities, prices or final progression order.

---

# 2. Identity Persists Across Realm Boundaries

A material does not stop being itself because it crosses a portal.

Examples:

- Iron remains Iron.
- Basalt remains Basalt.
- Soul Silver remains Soul Silver.
- Contract Iron remains Contract Iron.
- Living Heartwood remains Living Heartwood.
- Stormglass remains Stormglass.
- Pressure Pearl remains Pressure Pearl.
- Dreamwater remains Dreamwater.

Realm transition does not erase provenance, collapse specialist matter into ordinary matter, promote ordinary matter into specialist realm matter, or assign a universal realm tier.

---

# 3. Ordinary Physical Properties Persist

Ordinary physical properties generally remain valid outside a material's home realm.

These include:

- mass;
- density;
- hardness;
- tensile strength;
- compressive strength;
- thermal conductivity;
- electrical conductivity;
- brittleness;
- flexibility;
- corrosion behaviour;
- transparency;
- melting behaviour.

If the destination environment changes a material, the cause must be real: temperature, pressure, atmosphere, chemistry, corrosion, magical exposure, living-environment failure, or another authored environmental process.

---

# 4. Specialist Intrinsic Behaviour Persists in Identity

A specialist material retains the identity and intrinsic potential that make it special.

Examples:

- Soul Silver remains spirit-active material.
- Contract Iron remains contract-bound material.
- Living Heartwood remains living-specialist material unless its living structure is destroyed.
- Stormglass remains Stormglass.
- Resonance Wood remains Resonance Wood.
- Pressure Pearl remains Pressure Pearl.

Identity persistence does not guarantee full specialist functionality in every environment.

---

# 5. Functional Support States

Where specialist behaviour depends on environment, the material may be:

1. Active
2. Reduced / Weakened
3. Dormant
4. Unstable
5. Unsupported but physically intact
6. Degrading
7. Permanently transformed

These are available semantic outcomes, not mandatory states for every material.

---

# 6. Dormancy

Dormancy applies when:

- material identity remains intact;
- specialist potential remains intrinsic;
- required support is absent;
- specialist behaviour becomes inactive or limited;
- restoring support can reactivate it.

Dormancy is not deletion, identity loss or automatic conversion into an ordinary equivalent.

---

# 7. Weakening

A material can retain partial specialist function outside optimal conditions.

Possible causes include:

- insufficient ambient mana;
- reduced pressure;
- unsuitable coherence;
- absent sacred-law context;
- insufficient seasonal resonance;
- weakened spirit field;
- unsuitable temperature.

Weakening must follow explicit dependencies, never a blanket “foreign realm” penalty.

---

# 8. Instability

A material may become unstable when removed from required support.

Possible results include:

- energy leakage;
- loss of charge;
- structural stress;
- dangerous emissions;
- coherence drift;
- pressure failure;
- thermal runaway;
- magical feedback;
- degradation.

Instability is a material/environment interaction, not a generic export debuff.

---

# 9. Stabilisation

Stabilisation is a process, support relationship or maintained state used to preserve desired behaviour outside natural conditions.

Possible support includes:

- pressure;
- temperature;
- atmosphere;
- mana;
- coherence;
- seasonal field;
- spirit-safe conditions;
- sacred-law context;
- shielding;
- cooling;
- charge maintenance;
- containment.

A stabilised material normally remains the same material identity.

---

# 10. Temporary and Persistent Stabilisation

Stabilisation may be:

- continuously powered;
- periodically refreshed;
- passively maintained;
- structurally encoded;
- chemically achieved;
- magically anchored.

If removing support returns the material to unsupported behaviour, the relationship was stabilisation/state rather than new material identity.

---

# 11. Managed Environmental Support

Players, settlements and machines may reproduce specialist conditions where systems permit.

Examples:

- pressure chamber;
- controlled atmosphere;
- cooling loop;
- mana field;
- coherence stabiliser;
- spirit-safe room;
- seasonal greenhouse;
- high-aether processing chamber;
- contract-authorised worksite;
- heat-resistant containment cell.

This enables cross-realm engineering without faking whole-realm identity.

---

# 12. Environmental Support Is Capability-Based

Support systems provide capabilities such as:

- high pressure;
- controlled humidity;
- mana saturation;
- coherence stabilisation;
- spirit-safe field;
- sacred-law environment;
- cooling;
- shielding;
- inert atmosphere.

A material should query the required condition, not the current realm name.

---

# 13. No Realm Simulation by Name

An Overworld pressure chamber can satisfy a pressure requirement without becoming “Impossible Deep terrain”.

Likewise:

- a coherence field does not become Somnolent terrain;
- a mana field does not become Verdant terrain;
- a sacred-law structure does not automatically become Ascendant territory.

Systems reproduce conditions, not realm identity.

---

# 14. Partial Support

A system may satisfy only some requirements.

If a material requires:

- high pressure;
- low oxygen;
- mana shielding;

a chamber providing only high pressure is insufficient.

This prevents one universal “realm stabiliser” from solving every imported-material dependency.

---

# 15. No Universal Realm Stabiliser

FCC-12 rejects one generic machine that perfectly stabilises every realm material everywhere.

Different materials may require different:

- physical support;
- magical support;
- legal/contract context;
- safety conditions.

Stabilisation is requirement-driven.

---

# 16. Charge Maintenance

Charge is separate from material identity.

A charged material may:

- discharge naturally;
- leak;
- be consumed;
- transfer charge;
- require replenishment;
- require field maintenance.

Charge loss does not automatically change base material identity.

---

# 17. Pressure-Dependent Behaviour

Impossible Deep materials retain physical identity outside the Deep.

Pressure-dependent specialist behaviour may:

- weaken;
- become dormant;
- destabilise;
- require pressurisation.

Never implement “Deep item disabled outside Impossible Deep” when the actual requirement is pressure.

---

# 18. Gravity and Orientation Dependence

Gravity/orientation-sensitive materials may respond to:

- gravity vector;
- orientation;
- pressure;
- engineered fields.

Examples include Seismic Crystal states and gravity-conditioned Abyssal Iron.

Their specialist behaviour may remain functional off-realm if suitable physical conditions are recreated.

---

# 19. Coherence-Dependent Behaviour

Somnolent materials may depend on coherence, dream resonance or stabilisation.

Outside Somnolent:

- the matter remains;
- specialist effects may weaken or become dormant;
- stabilisation can preserve function where canon permits.

No material is deleted merely because it leaves through the Dreamgate.

---

# 20. Dreamwater Export

Dreamwater remains Dreamwater outside the Somnolent Expanse.

It does not become ordinary Water by export.

Its specialist behaviour may require containment or coherence support, but its material identity remains distinct.

---

# 21. Sacred-Law-Dependent Behaviour

Ascendant law-attuned materials/products may depend on:

- active sacred-law context;
- authorised domain;
- compatible law state;
- ritual/structure.

Outside that context, the material remains itself while law-dependent behaviour may become inactive.

---

# 22. Sacred Law Cannot Strand Matter

A law-dependent material must not vanish, become permanently unrecoverable or corrupt a save merely because its current realm lacks the required law.

Specialist function may be unavailable until support is restored.

---

# 23. Contract-Dependent Behaviour

Contract Iron and related bound materials remain physically valid outside Ashen.

Their binding behaviour follows:

- universal contract framework;
- valid contract state;
- relevant authority/consent;
- material binding semantics.

Realm coordinates are not the source of contract validity.

---

# 24. Contract Iron Off-Realm

Contract Iron remains Contract Iron outside Ashen.

Its binding can remain active, become inactive if its governing contract ends, or change according to universal contract rules.

It does not revert to ordinary Ember Iron merely by leaving the realm.

---

# 25. Living Material Export

Living specialist materials may leave their home realm.

Survival depends on actual biological/environmental needs such as:

- moisture;
- nutrients;
- temperature;
- mana;
- light;
- symbiosis;
- seasonal support.

If those needs are met, living behaviour can continue.

---

# 26. Living Heartwood Export

Living Heartwood remains Living Heartwood after export while its living structure survives.

Poor handling may cause:

- stress;
- dormancy;
- degradation;
- death;
- loss of living behaviour.

If living structure is destroyed, the resulting material follows the actual transformation rather than simply disappearing.

---

# 27. Harvested Matter Versus Organism

Harvested matter and living organisms are separate concerns.

A Cinderwood beam may remain stable off-realm while a living Cinderwood Tree may require suitable climate, soil, chemistry, mana and ecology.

Material exportability does not guarantee organism transplant success.

---

# 28. Exported Organisms

Exported organisms follow environmental suitability.

Possible states include:

- thriving;
- surviving;
- stressed;
- dormant;
- failing;
- dead.

Naturalisation requires sustained ecological success.

---

# 29. Naturalised Does Not Mean Native

An organism can become naturalised outside its origin without provenance being rewritten.

Naturalisation changes ecological status, not historical origin.

---

# 30. Invasive Status

Foreign does not automatically mean invasive.

The system distinguishes:

- imported;
- cultivated;
- contained;
- naturalised;
- invasive.

Invasive status requires actual harmful/uncontrolled ecological spread.

---

# 31. Hazardous Export

Hazardous materials remain hazardous outside their origin.

Examples include:

- Black Ember;
- Cold Fire;
- Furnace Heart;
- toxic gases;
- Pressure Oils;
- unstable magical matter.

Export does not neutralise danger.

---

# 32. Hazard Requirements

Hazardous material may require:

- specialised container;
- cooling;
- shielding;
- pressure;
- ventilation;
- legal handling;
- magical warding;
- corrosion resistance;
- monitoring.

These are real support conditions.

---

# 33. Container Eligibility

A container qualifies only when it satisfies requirements such as:

- pressure tolerance;
- heat tolerance;
- chemical resistance;
- impermeability;
- magical shielding;
- structural strength;
- seal quality.

This allows capability-based container substitution.

---

# 34. Containment Failure

If containment fails, the material behaves according to its actual properties.

Possible outcomes:

- leak;
- vent;
- ignition;
- explosion;
- contamination;
- environmental reaction;
- pressure release;
- magical discharge.

Do not silently destroy dangerous matter merely because simulation is inconvenient unless a deliberately abstracted process owns that result.

---

# 35. Cross-Realm Processing

A material may be processed outside its origin realm if all real requirements are satisfied:

- process capability;
- material eligibility;
- specialist support;
- operator knowledge;
- safety;
- contract/law context where applicable;
- energy.

No realm-origin check invalidates an otherwise valid process.

---

# 36. Foreign Station Use

Examples:

- an Overworld forge may work Abyssal Iron if it meets required conditions;
- a Deep workshop may process ordinary Iron;
- an Ashen line may process Copper;
- an Ascendant glassworks may work ordinary glass;
- a Somnolent workshop may shape imported timber.

Foreign processing does not erase provenance.

---

# 37. Native Station Advantage

Native/specialist stations may still be:

- easier to obtain locally;
- more efficient;
- safer;
- culturally integrated;
- naturally supported by local conditions;
- better suited to native materials.

Realm identity remains meaningful without arbitrary exclusivity.

---

# 38. Capability Equivalence

A provider is equivalent for a requirement when it satisfies the required capabilities to the required threshold.

Functional equivalence does not mean identity equivalence.

Two different materials may satisfy the same structural, conductive or optical role while remaining distinct materials.

---

# 39. Provider Tags

Systems may use provider roles such as:

- structural stone;
- structural timber;
- conductive metal;
- corrosion-resistant metal;
- heat-resistant lining;
- optical glass;
- transparent crystal;
- binding fibre;
- sealing material;
- pressure-vessel material;
- magical conductor;
- spirit-compatible material;
- coherence-compatible material.

Exact tag names remain implementation-owned.

---

# 40. Compound Provider Requirements

A provider requirement can combine multiple constraints.

Example: a pressure window may require:

- transparency;
- pressure resistance;
- chemical resistance;
- minimum form/size.

Passing one property is insufficient.

---

# 41. Exact-Material Requirements

An exact material must be required where identity is genuinely the mechanic.

Valid reasons include:

- unique chemistry;
- unique magical structure;
- exact historical provenance;
- exact contract material;
- biological compatibility;
- signature ritual;
- specific portal canon.

Exact-material requirements must not exist solely to manufacture fake progression.

---

# 42. Capability-Provider Requirements

Use provider requirements where any compatible material may satisfy the role.

Examples:

- structural beam;
- conductive wire;
- transparent pane;
- heat-resistant lining;
- cordage;
- seal;
- fastener.

This supports player improvisation and engineering.

---

# 43. Structural Substitution

A structural requirement may specify:

- strength;
- stiffness;
- mass;
- durability;
- rot resistance;
- heat resistance;
- dimensions.

Any material meeting those requirements may substitute.

---

# 44. Structural Trade-Offs

Different valid substitutes can alter:

- weight;
- durability;
- heat tolerance;
- cost;
- construction time;
- maintenance;
- appearance;
- magical behaviour.

Provider equivalence means “meets requirement”, not “produces identical performance”.

---

# 45. Conductive Substitution

A conductive requirement may accept Copper, Silver or another valid material if it satisfies:

- conductivity;
- temperature;
- corrosion;
- form;
- magical compatibility.

Performance can vary by chosen conductor.

---

# 46. Thermal Substitution

A heat-resistant requirement can accept any material meeting:

- maximum temperature;
- thermal shock;
- corrosion;
- structural constraints.

Possible providers include refractory ceramic, specialist stone, specialist metal or magical material.

---

# 47. Sealing and Container Substitution

A sealing/container requirement can evaluate:

- permeability;
- chemical compatibility;
- pressure;
- heat;
- flexibility;
- magical shielding.

Different materials may qualify.

---

# 48. Fibre and Binding Substitution

A binding requirement can accept fibres/cords/strips satisfying:

- tensile strength;
- flexibility;
- environmental resistance;
- fastening compatibility.

A ritual specifically requiring Oath-thread or Grace Thread remains an exact-material requirement.

---

# 49. Optical Substitution

Optical requirements may evaluate:

- transparency;
- refractive behaviour;
- clarity;
- magical affinity;
- pressure resistance;
- spectral properties.

Ordinary optics may accept ordinary glass.

Specialist optics may require exact Lucid Glass, Stormglass, Memory Glass or another authored material.

---

# 50. Lens Substitution

A generic magnifying lens can accept compatible optical materials.

A ritual lens requiring Pollen Amber's specialist behaviour can require exact Pollen Amber.

This distinction is mandatory.

---

# 51. Metal Fastener Substitution

A generic fastener may accept Iron, Steel, Bronze or another suitable material subject to:

- strength;
- corrosion;
- heat;
- compatibility.

---

# 52. Wood Substitution

A generic timber requirement may accept Oak Heartwood, Ironbark, Spruce, Cinderwood or another capable timber.

Specialist living or magical requirements may narrow eligibility.

---

# 53. Stone Substitution

Masonry requirements may accept multiple stones where:

- compressive strength;
- weathering;
- heat resistance;
- workability;

are sufficient.

Decorative or ritual requirements can specify exact stone.

---

# 54. Alloy Substitution

An alloy may substitute for another material only because its actual properties satisfy the requirement, never because it is labelled “higher tier”.

---

# 55. Quality Thresholds

Provider requirements may specify:

- minimum purity;
- minimum structural grade;
- minimum crystal stability;
- maximum contamination;
- minimum tensile strength.

Quality thresholds do not create new material identity.

---

# 56. Grade Thresholds

Ore/feedstock requirements may specify:

- concentration;
- impurity;
- composition.

Richer ore is not another metal.

---

# 57. State Requirements

A provider requirement may include state.

Examples:

- charged crystal;
- dry timber;
- stabilised Dreamwater;
- pressurised gas;
- season-attuned Bloomstone.

State remains separate from identity.

---

# 58. Provenance Requirements

A recipe/ritual may require provenance only when provenance is genuinely part of the mechanic.

Examples:

- material from a specific ruin;
- certified lawful Contract Iron;
- material from a named Titan;
- archaeologically authenticated alloy;
- material from a specific site.

---

# 59. Portal Material Boundary

Portal recipes may contain:

- exact canonical materials;
- provider-role materials;
- crafted components;
- provenanced materials.

FCC-12C defines the semantics.

FCC-13 decides exact recipes and quantities.

---

# 60. Portal Provider Substitution

Substitution is permitted only where the canonical portal recipe explicitly uses a provider role.

A frame may accept a compatible structural/magical provider while a keystone can remain an exact component.

---

# 61. Provider Substitution Cannot Create Generic Realm Access

Substitution must never create:

- generic universal portal;
- seventh portal family;
- portal bypass;
- realm-independent Dimension Gate.

Each current major realm retains its canonical portal family.

---

# 62. No “Any Magic Crystal Opens Portal” Rule

A broad magical-crystal provider cannot silently satisfy an exact portal requirement.

Exact:

- keystone;
- attunement;
- provenance;
- material;

remain exact where canon requires them.

---

# 63. No Wrong-Realm Disabling

A valid material, tool or machine must never fail solely because:

> origin realm != current realm

Invalid conditions must be expressed through real requirements:

- insufficient pressure;
- missing law context;
- insufficient mana;
- inadequate strength;
- wrong exact reagent;
- invalid contract.

---

# 64. Cross-Realm Tools

A tool from one realm may function in another if:

- its material survives;
- its capability is sufficient;
- environment is compatible;
- required support exists.

A stronger valid tool can satisfy an earlier capability requirement.

---

# 65. Cross-Realm Machines

Machines may operate outside their origin realm if:

- power exists;
- materials survive;
- environment is suitable;
- process requirements are met.

Specialist machines may still require pressure, mana, cooling, law, contract or another explicit support.

---

# 66. Cross-Realm Construction

Structures may use foreign materials if:

- geometry allows;
- material capabilities satisfy the design;
- environmental durability is sufficient;
- specialist effects are supported where necessary.

This is intended.

---

# 67. Repair and Substitution

Repairs may use:

- exact matching material;
- approved equivalent;
- emergency substitute;

depending on design rules.

Substitution can change performance and appearance.

---

# 68. Upgrading Existing Infrastructure

Later-acquired materials may legitimately improve earlier infrastructure.

Examples:

- stronger beam;
- better conductor;
- more heat-resistant lining;
- more stable optical component.

This is intended capability progression.

---

# 69. Legitimate Sequence Breaking

A stronger capability acquired elsewhere may satisfy an earlier capability requirement.

Examples:

- stronger tool mines an earlier barrier;
- imported machine supplies a needed process;
- superior support material replaces a local option.

This is allowed when the requirement is capability-based.

---

# 70. Sequence Breaking Remains Bounded

Capability freedom cannot bypass:

- exact portal families;
- exact quest outcomes;
- unique authority events;
- exact rituals;
- explicit provenance requirements;
- law/contract requirements.

Alternative capability applies only where the requirement itself is capability-based.

---

# 71. No Universal Realm Tier Ladder

FCC-12C prohibits universal ranking such as:

> Ashen > Deep > Ascendant > Overworld

Each material has a role-specific capability profile.

A common material can outperform a rare magical material in a role for which it is better suited.

---

# 72. Specialist Versus Generalist Materials

Materials may be:

- general-purpose;
- highly specialised;
- fragile but powerful;
- heavy but durable;
- magically useful but structurally poor;
- cheap but maintenance-heavy.

Substitution preserves trade-offs.

---

# 73. Provider Query Resolution

A provider query conceptually evaluates:

1. exact/category eligibility;
2. required form;
3. required state;
4. capability thresholds;
5. quality/purity thresholds;
6. environmental compatibility;
7. provenance requirement where applicable.

A material that passes can satisfy the requirement.

---

# 74. Exact Requirement Priority

If a recipe explicitly requires Contract Iron, generic structural metal may not substitute simply because it is stronger.

Exact identity is part of the authored mechanic.

---

# 75. Provider Requirement Priority

If a recipe requires conductive metal, the system should not arbitrarily demand Copper when another material satisfies the requirement.

---

# 76. Material-Specific Advantages

A valid substitute may outperform the minimum requirement.

Examples:

- higher conductivity;
- lower mass;
- better heat resistance;
- improved durability.

This lets material choice matter after eligibility succeeds.

---

# 77. Material-Specific Disadvantages

A valid substitute may introduce:

- weight;
- brittleness;
- corrosion;
- cost;
- repair difficulty;
- magical interference.

Passing a provider requirement does not guarantee identical outcome.

---

# 78. Substitution UI

Future UI should distinguish:

- exact material;
- provider class;
- valid alternatives;
- unmet capability;
- state/quality requirements.

Players should understand why a substitute is valid or invalid.

---

# 79. Automation Uses the Same Rules

Automation must obey the same provider rules as manual crafting.

It must not:

- consume invalid material;
- violate exact-material requirements;
- ignore reservations.

Automation may use filters and substitution policies.

---

# 80. Player Substitution Preferences

Systems may support preferences such as:

- preferred material;
- forbidden material;
- minimum quality;
- cheapest valid;
- nearest valid;
- preserve rare material.

FCC-12C requires semantic support, not final UI design.

---

# 81. NPC and Settlement Substitution

NPCs and settlements may choose local substitutes when:

- capability is sufficient;
- knowledge exists;
- culture permits;
- economy permits.

This supports regional industrial variation without duplicate recipes.

---

# 82. Cultural Material Preference

Culture can influence material preference without changing physical truth.

A culture may prefer traditional stone, sacred timber or a specific alloy even where alternatives are physically valid.

---

# 83. Legal Restrictions

A material can be physically valid but legally restricted.

Examples:

- controlled Contract Iron;
- hazardous energetic resource;
- protected biological matter;
- sacred material.

Legality is separate from physical capability.

---

# 84. Contractual Restrictions

Contracts can constrain:

- ownership;
- transfer;
- processing;
- authorised use.

These are legal/system states unless the material itself has bound behaviour.

---

# 85. Research Restrictions

A player may physically possess a material while lacking knowledge to process or stabilise it.

Research can gate knowledge without altering material truth.

---

# 86. Certification

Some activities may require certification for:

- pressure vessel work;
- hazardous energy handling;
- sacred-law processing;
- contract-bound production.

Certification is governance/knowledge, not identity.

---

# 87. Identification

Foreign material may initially be:

- unidentified;
- partially identified;
- misclassified.

Research can reveal identity, capability, hazard and provenance.

Underlying properties remain objective.

---

# 88. Unknown Does Not Mean Random

An unidentified material does not gain arbitrary behaviour.

The canonical properties still exist; the player simply lacks knowledge.

---

# 89. Storage Compatibility

Storage may require:

- temperature;
- humidity;
- pressure;
- shielding;
- segregation;
- ventilation;
- containment.

Warehouses can therefore specialise without creating duplicate material identities.

---

# 90. Bulk Storage

Stable ordinary matter can use generic bulk storage where appropriate.

Specialist matter may require dedicated facilities.

---

# 91. Hazard Segregation

Incompatible hazards may require separate storage.

This follows actual material hazard metadata.

# 92. Transport Compatibility

Transport can evaluate:

- mass;

- pressure;
- containment;

Realm origin alone does not determine transport compatibility.

# 93. Portal Transport

Canonical Realm Access portals can transport physical matter according to portal rules.

- identity;

- ownership;

- neutralise hazards;

# 95. Imported Material Economy

Foreign materials may participate in trade, industry, construction, research, ritual and infrastructure.

Provenance can affect value and legality without changing identity.

# 96. Local Substitute Economy

Civilisations may define standards such as:

# 99. Imported Material Recycling

Imported material remains recyclable according to its actual physical rules.

Foreign provenance can remain meaningful after salvage/reprocessing.

---

# 100. Mixed Scrap

Mixed scrap requires valid sorting, separation or refining.

The system must not magically resolve mixed scrap into arbitrary pure inputs.

---

# 101. Stabilisation Cost Is Not a Tier Tax

Support costs arise only from real needs such as:

- pressure;
- cooling;
- mana;
- coherence;
- containment;
- law.

No generic foreign-material surcharge is imposed by FCC-12.

---

# 102. Foreign Material Advantage Is Allowed

A foreign material may be extremely useful in another realm.

This is intended cross-realm systemic gameplay, not an exploit.

---

# 103. Native Material Advantage Is Allowed

Local matter can have advantages through:

- abundance;
- natural environmental support;
- specialist knowledge;
- lower transport cost.

Realm economies remain meaningful.

---

# 104. No Forced Local Superiority

Native material is not automatically stronger in its home realm than a valid foreign alternative.

Actual properties determine performance.

---

# 105. No Foreign Nerf by Origin

Foreign material is not automatically weaker because it is foreign.

Only explicit support dependencies can weaken specialist behaviour.

# 106. Explicit Support Dependencies

If specialist behaviour depends on environment, the material/system must declare the requirement.

Examples:

- requires pressure;
- requires coherence;
- requires living state;
- requires sacred-law field;
- requires contract context.

Undocumented realm assumptions are not acceptable.

---

# 107. Multiple Support Dependencies

A material may require several conditions simultaneously.

All required conditions must be satisfied for full function.

---

# 108. Form-Specific Support

Support needs can vary by form.

Examples:

- powder may be more volatile;
- large crystal may require orientation;
- contained fluid may require pressure.

# 195. Export Doctrine

> **A material retains its canonical identity after export. Ordinary physical properties persist unless actually altered. Specialist behaviour remains intrinsic but may become weakened, dormant, unstable or support-dependent according to explicit material/system requirements. Stabilisation reproduces required conditions and does not normally create new material identity.**

---

# 196. Substitution Doctrine

> **Use exact-material requirements when identity, chemistry, provenance or unique magical function is the authored mechanic. Use provider/capability requirements when any physically compatible material may satisfy the role. Provider equivalence permits substitution without merging material identities and without guaranteeing identical performance.**

---

# 197. No-Wrong-Realm Doctrine

> **No material, tool, process or machine may be invalidated solely because its origin realm differs from its current realm. Compatibility follows actual physical, magical, environmental, legal, knowledge and capability requirements.**

---

# 198. Sequence-Breaking Doctrine

> **A legitimately acquired stronger or compatible capability may satisfy an earlier capability requirement. This is intended non-linear progression, provided exact canonical access, ritual, provenance, legal or contract requirements are not bypassed.**

---

# 199. Hazard Doctrine

> **Export does not neutralise danger. Materials retain real hazards, and valid transport/processing requires appropriate containment, support and safety capabilities.**

---

# 200. Living-Material Doctrine

> **Living materials and organisms are exportable when physical transfer is valid, but continued living function depends on actual environmental needs. Material identity persists through stress/dormancy until real transformation, death or degradation changes the matter.**

---

# 201. Portal Boundary Doctrine

> **Material provider substitution may operate inside a canonical portal recipe only where the recipe explicitly uses provider roles. It cannot create a generic portal family, bypass canonical Realm Access or introduce additional normal realm-access methods.**
