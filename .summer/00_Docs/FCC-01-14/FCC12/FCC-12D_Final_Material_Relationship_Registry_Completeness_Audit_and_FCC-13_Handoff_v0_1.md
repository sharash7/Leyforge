# 2. Final Universal Relationship Grammar

Every material-related registry entry must resolve through one or more of these semantic roles:

1. **Exact Material Identity** — canonical underlying substance/material.
2. **Material Superclass / Family** — composable taxonomy such as stone, metal, glass, fibre.
3. **Physical Form** — slab, wire, beam, pane, powder, thread, etc.
4. **State / Condition** — hot, wet, damaged, charged, corrupted, dormant, stabilised, etc.
5. **Facies** — geological/environmental expression of a parent material.
6. **Provenance** — origin/history metadata.
7. **Quality / Grade / Purity / Composition Profile** — multidimensional variation.
8. **Transformed Material** — new material created after the FCC-12A transformation threshold is crossed.
9. **Manufactured Component** — designed object made from one or more materials.
10. **Provider / Capability Class** — functional requirement that one or more compatible materials can satisfy.
11. **Alias / Cultural / Legacy Term** — non-canonical display or migration name.
12. **Rejected Generic Identity** — prohibited filler/tier/placeholder material concept.

The registry must never collapse these roles merely because an implementation prefers one row per noun.

---

# 3. Universal Identity Rule

> **A canonical material is a persistent underlying substance or materially distinct physical, magical or biological medium. One material identity may possess many physical forms, states, conditions, qualities, provenances, locations, owners and aliases. Those dimensions do not create new material identities unless composition, persistent structure or intrinsic behaviour changes enough to constitute genuinely different matter.**

This rule governs FCC-13.

---

> **Physically and intrinsically equivalent matter uses one canonical material identity regardless of realm, region, owner or current location.**

Provenance remains separate.

# 21. Final Export Doctrine

> **A material retains its canonical identity after export. Ordinary physical properties persist unless actually altered. Specialist behaviour remains intrinsic but may become weakened, dormant, unstable or support-dependent according to explicit requirements. Stabilisation reproduces required conditions and does not normally create new material identity.**

There is no universal realm stabiliser.

- atmosphere;

# 29. Final State-to-New-Material Threshold

A state becomes a new material only when materially significant change affects one or more of:

- composition;
- persistent structure;
- intrinsic physical behaviour;
- intrinsic magical behaviour;
- intrinsic biological organisation;
- stable energetic/material architecture.

Reversibility strongly favours state.

Irreversibility alone is not enough.
