# 2. U01 — Universal Material Ontology

## Q1 — Canonical material identity

**Decision:** A canonical material is a persistent underlying substance or materially distinct physical, magical or biological medium whose materially significant composition, persistent structure and intrinsic behaviour remain coherent across forms and ordinary states.

A new material identity is justified when the matter itself becomes genuinely different, not merely because its name, shape, location, owner, quality or ordinary condition changes.

## Q2 — Material identity vs physical form

**Decision:** Independent axes. Granite remains Granite as rubble, cut block, brick or stair. Copper remains Copper as ingot, plate, rod or wire.

## Q3 — Material identity vs provenance

**Decision:** Independent axes. Origin/history may remain mechanically meaningful without creating a duplicate substance.

## Q4 — Material identity vs location/ownership

**Decision:** Location or ownership never redefines material identity by itself.

## Q5 — Superclass vs exact identity

**Decision:** Separate layers. `metal`, `stone`, `glass`, `wood`, etc. are taxonomic classes; Iron, Soul Silver, Stormglass and Soulwood remain exact identities beneath them.

## Q6 — Multiple material classes

**Decision:** Allowed. A material may simultaneously be conductive, forgeable, structural, magical, heat-resistant, optical, biological, brittle, etc.

## Q7 — Role of composition

**Decision:** Composition is a major criterion but not the only criterion. Persistent microstructure, intrinsic magical structure and biological/material behaviour may also justify distinct identity.

## Q8 — Persistent magical structure

**Decision:** Persistent intrinsic magical structure may justify distinct material identity. Temporary charge/environmental influence/reversible attunement remains state unless the transformation threshold is crossed.

## Q9 — Biological materials

**Decision:** Wood, fibre, resin, bone, shell, hide, chitin, pearls and similar matter use the same universal material framework. Species/ecology remains owned by biological-content systems.

## Q10 — Energetic resources

**Decision:** Use the broader material/resource framework while distinguishing physical energetic materials, energy charge, fields, captured phenomena and manufactured storage components.

## Q11 — Fluids and gases

**Decision:** Use the same core material-identity concept. Phase, containment and world representation are separate from underlying substance identity.

## Q12 — Components

**Decision:** Components are designed object/product identities made from materials, not material families merely because they are craft inputs.

## Q13 — Physical form

**Decision:** First-class independent axis.

## Q14 — State/condition

**Decision:** First-class independent axis. Wet, hot, charged, damaged, corrupted, pressure-conditioned, polished, etc. do not automatically create new materials.

## Q15 — Provenance

**Decision:** First-class independent axis. Origin realm, deposit/source, biological source, workshop/process and relevant historical chain may be retained.

## Q16 — Quality, purity and grade

**Decision:** Separate from identity and from each other where they mean different things. Do not collapse ore concentration, chemical purity, structural quality, crystal stability, craftsmanship, affinity and contamination into one rarity number.

## Q17 — Taxonomy/tags

**Decision:** Hierarchical and composable. Broad material classes coexist with property/capability tags.

## Q18 — Cultural names

**Decision:** Cultural terms are aliases/display vocabulary on one canonical identity unless the culture actually creates a transformed material.

## Q19 — Regional/local names

**Decision:** Local terminology may display contextually while the underlying canonical material remains one identity.

## Q20 — Universal material-definition principle

> **A canonical material is a persistent underlying substance or materially distinct physical, magical or biological medium. One material identity may possess many physical forms, states, conditions, qualities, provenances, locations, owners and aliases. Those dimensions do not create new material identities unless composition, persistent structure or intrinsic behaviour changes enough to constitute genuinely different matter.**

**U01 Result:** 20/20 resolved autonomously; no creative escalation required.

# 3. U02 — Shared Matter & Cross-Realm Identity

## Q21 — Shared-identity standard

**Decision:** Matter uses one shared canonical identity wherever materially significant composition, persistent structure and intrinsic behaviour are genuinely the same. Realm boundary alone never creates a duplicate.

## Q22 — Natural compositional tolerance

**Decision:** Exact atomic sameness is not required. Natural compositional ranges may remain one material where differences do not cause materially significant behaviour. Significant differences become composition profile, grade, variant or distinct material according to the transformation threshold.

## Q23 — Magical contamination

**Decision:** Reversible/external magical influence is state or contamination metadata. Persistent intrinsic transformation may create distinct identity.

## Q24 — Environmental conditioning

**Decision:** Pressure, gravity, heat exposure, moisture and ordinary weathering remain state/facies while underlying matter remains continuous. Permanent transformation may create a new identity.

## Q25 — Provenance on shared matter

**Decision:** Shared material identity and provenance coexist. Example: Basalt with Ashen origin remains Basalt.

## Q26 — Realm-prefixed duplicates

**Decision:** Rejected by default for physically identical matter. Do not create separate Overworld Basalt, Ashen Basalt, Verdant Iron or Deep Copper merely because of origin/location.

## Q27 — Imported materials

**Decision:** Imported matter retains its canonical identity unless actual transformation occurs.

## Q28 — Ordinary Water

**Decision:** Ordinary physical Water is one shared universal material identity wherever the source means genuinely ordinary water. Temperature, salinity, contamination, magical charge and provenance are state/composition/context unless transformation occurs.

**Non-merge:** Dreamwater remains distinct.

## Q29 — Lava / Magma

**Decision:** Use a universal magma/lava material-family architecture based on molten geological composition. Where molten-rock composition is materially the same, identity can be shared. Basaltic/silica-rich/gas-rich/magically altered compositions remain composition profiles, states or distinct transformed matter as appropriate.

`Lava` is primarily a molten-rock form/state/class, not automatically one exact universal substance and not automatically Ashen.

## Q30 — Sand

**Decision:** `Sand` is primarily a particle-size/material-form class, not one universal exact substance. Exact identity follows underlying composition where relevant.

**Non-merge:** Dream Sand remains distinct.

## Q31 — Gravel

**Decision:** Same principle as Sand. `Gravel` is a coarse aggregate/particle-size class. Exact identity follows underlying rock/mineral composition where gameplay requires it. A generic mixed gravel product may exist where useful.

## Q32 — Clay

**Decision:** `Clay` is a universal mineral/feedstock class with composition-bearing identities rather than one realm-specific material per world. Physically equivalent clays may share identities. White Kaolin remains a specialist clay/mineral source; River Clay remains a source/deposit relationship.

## Q33 — Ordinary manufactured glass

**Decision:** Ordinary silica-based manufactured glass uses a shared universal identity where formulation is materially equivalent. Colourants, formulation, tempering, magical structure and specialist transformations may be state, formulation or distinct material depending on significance.

**Distinct specialist glasses remain distinct:** Memory Glass, Lucid Glass, Stormglass, Ventglass, Hellglass and Thunder Glass.

## Q34 — Common metals

**Decision:** Common metals use one universal physical identity across realms: Iron, Copper, Tin, Lead, Nickel, Cobalt, Silver, Gold, etc.

Distinct realm metals remain distinct: Soul Silver, Skyiron, Abyssal Iron, Ember Iron.

## Q35 — Common alloys

**Decision:** The same materially significant alloy formulation remains the same material across realms. Different formulations/heat treatment become grade, state, formulation variant or distinct alloy according to metallurgy rules.

## Q36 — Ordinary Salt

**Decision:** Ordinary Salt uses one shared identity where materially the same ordinary salt is meant. Grave Salt, Blessing Salt, Black Brine Salt and Ember Salt remain distinct.

## Q37 — Imported ordinary timbers

**Decision:** Imported timber retains exact identity. Oak Heartwood remains Oak Heartwood outside the Overworld.

## Q38 — Imported ordinary fibres

**Decision:** Same rule. Sunflax Fibre, Mirehemp Fibre and other physical fibres retain identity after export.

## Q39 — Imported ordinary biological products

**Decision:** Leather, bone, shell, resin, oil and similar goods remain the same physical substances unless transformed. Source-species/provenance may remain metadata.

## Q40 — Shared matter entering specialist realms

**Decision:** It remains the same material unless transformed. Realm environment may add condition, charge, contamination, attunement, damage, suppression or enhancement without automatic identity change.

## Q41 — Specialist matter entering ordinary realms

**Decision:** Specialist identity persists. Soul Silver stays Soul Silver; Stormglass stays Stormglass; Contract Iron stays Contract Iron; Living Heartwood stays Living Heartwood. Later U13 decides whether specialist behaviour weakens, goes dormant or needs support off-realm.

## Q42 — Display naming with provenance

**Decision:** Use canonical material name by default. Optional contextual provenance qualifiers may appear when useful, without creating new identities.

## Q43 — Stacking across provenance

**Decision:** Material identity should not force separate stacks solely because provenance differs. Provenance-sensitive gameplay may require metadata-aware lot handling where mixing would destroy meaningful information. Exact inventory behaviour remains downstream.

## Q44 — Shared-matter doctrine

> **Physically and intrinsically equivalent matter uses one canonical material identity regardless of realm, region, current owner or current location. Provenance and ordinary environmental conditioning remain separate metadata/state. Broad physical terms such as sand, gravel, lava and clay may describe form/class/composition families rather than one monolithic substance. Specialist realm materials remain distinct when persistent composition, structure or intrinsic behaviour is materially different.**

**U02 Result:** 24/24 resolved autonomously; no creative escalation required.

# U03 — Provenance, Location & Ownership

Purpose: preserve meaningful origin/history without multiplying one physical substance into duplicate material identities.

## Q45 — What is provenance in the universal material model?

**Decision:** Provenance is first-class metadata/history attached to matter, lots, objects or products; it is not the underlying material identity.

## Q46 — How is origin realm represented?

**Decision:** Origin realm is retained where known and meaningful. Export never rewrites a material into a destination-realm version.

## Q47 — How is current realm/location represented?

**Decision:** Current location is separate runtime/location data. It never substitutes for origin.

## Q48 — Should source deposit/site be retainable?

**Decision:** Yes where gameplay benefits. A deposit, biome, farm, creature, ruin, workshop or named source may be preserved as provenance without requiring a new material ID.

## Q49 — Should processing provenance be retained?

**Decision:** Yes where relevant. A material may record important processing origin such as a famous forge, spirit-active workshop or contract-binding facility without becoming a new substance solely for that reason.

## Q50 — How is biological-source provenance handled?

**Decision:** Species/source organism can remain provenance when it materially matters; harvested matter still enters universal material ontology. Species identity is not duplicated into every derived material definition.

## Q51 — How is archaeological/historical provenance handled?

**Decision:** Archaeological era, ruin, civilisation and recovered-context data remain provenance/status. Terms like Relic Material never replace actual composition.

## Q52 — Is full chain-of-custody mandatory for every stack?

**Decision:** No. Provenance supports bounded history appropriate to gameplay. Important lots/artifacts may retain richer chains; ordinary bulk matter may retain only material-significant provenance.

## Q53 — What happens to provenance after true transformation?

**Decision:** The output receives its own material identity when transformation warrants it while preserving relevant source provenance/inputs. Contract Iron can remember Ember Iron/Ashen provenance without remaining Ember Iron.

## Q54 — Can provenance-sensitive lots merge freely?

**Decision:** Only when doing so does not destroy information required by gameplay. Material identity alone should not force separate stacks, but provenance-aware inventory/logistics may preserve lots when legality, quality, research or contracts require it.

## Q55 — Can provenance affect trade or law?

**Decision:** Yes. Origin, certification, stolen status, sacred provenance, contract provenance, contamination history or archaeological status may affect value, legality and access without changing substance identity.

## Q56 — Can provenance affect research, quests or archaeology?

**Decision:** Yes. Provenance is explicitly available to discovery, scholarship, authentication and quest systems.

## Q57 — How are forged/counterfeit provenances represented?

**Decision:** Separate claimed provenance from verified provenance. Counterfeiting alters records/claims, not underlying physical history.

## Q58 — How should UI display provenance?

**Decision:** Canonical material name remains primary; concise qualifiers appear only where useful, with deeper history available through inspection/research interfaces.

## Q59 — Can provenance be compressed or discarded?

**Decision:** Only nonessential detail may be aggregated. Information that affects rules, authenticity, contracts, law, research or unique history must survive relevant transformations/saves.

## Q60 — Universal provenance doctrine

**Decision:** One canonical material can carry meaningful origin, source, processing and custody provenance without becoming duplicate matter. Provenance persists only to the resolution gameplay needs and is distinct from owner, location, state and identity.

## U03 Result

**U03: PASS — Q45–60 autonomously resolved; no creative escalation required.**

# U04 — State, Facies, Condition & Transformation

Purpose: define exactly when changing matter remains a state and when it becomes a genuinely new material.

## Q61 — What is a material state?

**Decision:** A state is a persistent or transient modifier to an existing material whose underlying identity remains continuous.

## Q62 — How are reversible changes treated?

**Decision:** Reversible or routinely removable changes default to state/condition, not new material identity.

## Q63 — How are geological facies treated?

**Decision:** Facies are state/profile expressions of one geological material when composition and intrinsic behaviour remain within the parent material envelope.

## Q64 — Temperature state

**Decision:** Hot, cold, molten/frozen where chemically continuous, thermally stressed and similar conditions are state/phase unless permanent transformation occurs.

## Q65 — Moisture state

**Decision:** Wet, dry, soaked, waterlogged and ordinary drying states remain condition metadata.

## Q66 — Damage and wear

**Decision:** Cracked, chipped, worn, fatigued and damaged conditions remain state unless the object/material physically becomes a different product such as rubble.

## Q67 — Charge state

**Decision:** Electrical, mana or other stored charge remains state/energy data unless charge causes permanent structural transformation.

## Q68 — Attunement state

**Decision:** Reversible/bounded magical attunement is state. Persistent intrinsic restructuring can cross into transformed material.

## Q69 — Pressure conditioning

**Decision:** Pressure-conditioned variants remain state/facies when material continuity is preserved; this explains Pressure Stone facies and related Deep behaviour.

## Q70 — Gravity/orientation conditioning

**Decision:** Gravity-conditioned/oriented expressions remain state/form where locked, including Inverted/Floating Pressure Stone and Seismic Crystal orientation forms.

## Q71 — Coherence conditioning

**Decision:** Somnolent coherence/lucidity/nightmare conditioning defaults to state unless the source canon identifies a genuinely different material.

## Q72 — Sacred-law conditioning

**Decision:** Law/domain exposure is state/context by default. A crafted law-attuned product may be a processed product where its intrinsic behaviour persists.

## Q73 — Contract binding

**Decision:** Ordinary contractual status is external state/legal context. Deliberate material binding that permanently creates Contract Iron is a transformed-material lineage.

## Q74 — Seasonal attunement

**Decision:** Seasonal charge/attunement remains state unless a separately authored irreversible transformation is proven; Seasonal Crystal therefore remains charged Bloomstone.

## Q75 — Blight and natural decay

**Decision:** Blight/rot default to biological/material condition; Rotwood remains state unless irreversible self-propagating transformation creates genuinely different matter.

## Q76 — Void corruption

**Decision:** Void corruption is state-first. It never automatically creates generic corrupted material identities.

## Q77 — When can corruption become new matter?

**Decision:** Only when transformation is irreversible or materially persistent enough that composition/structure/intrinsic behaviour becomes distinct, with explicit provenance to the transformation.

## Q78 — Polish, finish and weathering

**Decision:** Polished, roughened, oxidised surface, patina and ordinary weathering are finish/condition unless chemistry changes enough to affect material identity.

## Q79 — Permanent microstructure change

**Decision:** Permanent structural/microstructural change can justify a transformed material when it substantially changes capability/behaviour and is not merely a grade or heat-treatment profile.

## Q80 — Permanent composition change

**Decision:** A chemically/compositionally new reusable substance is normally a new material identity.

## Q81 — Irreversible magical transformation

**Decision:** A stable magical restructuring that becomes intrinsic and changes behaviour can create a new material identity even if mundane chemistry remains related.

## Q82 — Do states survive form changes?

**Decision:** Relevant states carry through processing only when physically meaningful. Cutting a charged crystal may distribute/preserve charge according to the process; sanding away a surface coating may remove that state.

## Q83 — Do states survive realm export?

**Decision:** Yes if the physical/magical condition itself persists. Realm-dependent effects may become dormant or require support rather than being erased.

## Q84 — Universal transformation test

**Decision:** Create a new material only when materially significant composition, persistent structure or intrinsic behaviour changes enough to make the output a distinct reusable substance. Otherwise use state, facies, quality, provenance or form.

## U04 Result

**U04: PASS — Q61–84 resolved. The state→new-material threshold is now explicit and consistent with all locked realm lineages.**

# U05 — Quality, Grade, Purity & Composition

Purpose: retain meaningful material variation without turning quality adjectives into hundreds of separate substances.

## Q85 — Should quality dimensions be separate?

**Decision:** Yes. Grade, purity, structural quality, workmanship, stability, affinity, contamination and condition are independent dimensions where they represent different behaviour.

## Q86 — Ore grade/concentration

**Decision:** Ore grade records concentration/richness of valuable material in source rock/feedstock; it is not a new metal.

## Q87 — Purity

**Decision:** Purity records unwanted inclusions/contaminants relative to the intended material or formulation.

## Q88 — Structural quality

**Decision:** Structural quality records defects, grain/fibre condition, cracking and usable mechanical integrity.

## Q89 — Workmanship quality

**Decision:** Workmanship belongs primarily to the crafted object/form, not the base material identity. A poorly forged Iron sword does not create Poor Iron as an element.

## Q90 — Crystal stability

**Decision:** Stability is a dedicated property/state dimension for crystals and magical matter where instability changes handling/behaviour.

## Q91 — Affinity/resonance

**Decision:** Affinity/resonance is separate from purity and charge; a crystal can be pure yet poorly aligned to a given magical use.

## Q92 — Contamination

**Decision:** Contamination records foreign matter, toxins, corruption or other impurities and may require treatment without redefining the base material.

## Q93 — Moisture

**Decision:** Moisture content is condition data for wood, soils, fibres and other hygroscopic materials.

## Q94 — Age/weathering

**Decision:** Age and weathering are provenance/condition dimensions; they create new identity only if transformation criteria are crossed.

## Q95 — Biological maturity

**Decision:** Growth/maturity can affect harvested material quality/properties but does not automatically create separate material families.

## Q96 — Alloy composition

**Decision:** Alloy composition/formulation is explicit. Small controlled ranges can remain one alloy with composition/grade metadata; materially different formulations become variants or distinct alloys.

## Q97 — Heat treatment

**Decision:** Heat treatment is usually material state/processing history where the substance remains the same alloy, with capability changes recorded separately.

## Q98 — Charge is not quality

**Decision:** Charge/energy content is its own dimension and must not be conflated with material quality.

## Q99 — Rarity is not quality

**Decision:** Rarity/provenance scarcity never functions as a universal quality/tier ladder.

## Q100 — Stacking implications

**Decision:** Stacks may merge only where required metadata can be preserved/aggregated correctly. Exact inventory lot logic remains FCC-13/implementation-owned.

## Q101 — UI representation

**Decision:** UI should expose only dimensions meaningful to the current task rather than one overloaded rarity colour or universal star rating.

## Q102 — Universal quality doctrine

**Decision:** Material variation is multidimensional: composition/grade, purity, structural quality, condition, charge, affinity/attunement, contamination and provenance remain separate unless a specific system intentionally derives a summary metric.

## U05 Result

**U05: PASS — Q85–102 resolved; no universal rarity/quality ladder introduced.**

# U06 — Stone, Soil, Sediment & Ceramic Architecture

Purpose: reconcile mineral/earth materials, common geology, ceramics and the final Deepstone disposition.

## Q103 — Universal stone superclass

**Decision:** Use a broad stone/rock superclass with exact material identities beneath it. Ordinary and magical stones can share masonry capabilities without being merged.

## Q104 — When are ordinary stones shared?

**Decision:** A physically equivalent ordinary stone remains one material across realms. Realm location/provenance does not create a duplicate.

## Q105 — Raw stone versus masonry

**Decision:** Raw rock and cut masonry are forms/products of the same material unless processing causes real chemical transformation.

## Q106 — Rubble and aggregate

**Decision:** Rubble/aggregate are size/form classes. Exact composition can be retained where gameplay needs it; mixed aggregate may be a deliberate mixture/product.

## Q107 — Stone powder

**Decision:** Powder is a derived particulate form where crushing/grinding is meaningful, not another base stone family.

## Q108 — Sand architecture

**Decision:** Sand is a particle-size/material-form class. Exact identity follows composition; quartz-rich, volcanic and Dream Sand are not automatically one substance.

## Q109 — Gravel architecture

**Decision:** Gravel is likewise a coarse aggregate class whose exact composition can vary; mixed/common gravel may exist as a product/category.

## Q110 — Overworld soil families

**Decision:** The eight locked Overworld soils remain principal substrate identities; fertility, tilling, moisture and similar conditions remain states/profiles.

## Q111 — Universal clay architecture

**Decision:** Clay is a mineral/feedstock superclass. Composition-bearing clays can share universal ceramic processing without becoming one monolithic substance.

## Q112 — River Clay

**Decision:** Retain as clay/mineral-source/deposit terminology tied to compatible clay composition, not a ninth soil family.

## Q113 — White Kaolin

**Decision:** Retain as specialised kaolin clay/mineral feedstock with ceramic/alchemical relevance where later registries use it.

## Q114 — Brick Loam

**Decision:** Retain as clay-rich/alluvial/brickmaking-quality soil profile rather than independent principal soil/material family.

## Q115 — Raw ceramic feedstock

**Decision:** Ceramic feedstock resolves from suitable clay/mineral compositions plus additives, not from every soil automatically.

## Q116 — Fired ceramic identity

**Decision:** Firing that irreversibly vitrifies/sinters clay creates a ceramic product/material lineage distinct from raw clay feedstock.

## Q117 — Brick and tile

**Decision:** Brick and roof tile are forms/products of fired ceramic formulations; form does not create a separate chemical material by itself.

## Q118 — Refractory ceramic

**Decision:** A materially distinct refractory formulation can be a separate ceramic material/formulation where composition/properties justify it.

## Q119 — Glassmaking sand

**Decision:** Only compositionally suitable silica-rich feedstock satisfies ordinary glassmaking; 'sand' alone is not sufficient.

## Q120 — Basalt

**Decision:** Basalt remains the already-locked shared material across Overworld/Ashen contexts where physical identity matches.

## Q121 — Special realm stones

**Decision:** Rootstone, Memory Stone, Dreamstone, Waking Stone, Cloudstone, Hymnstone, Pressure Stone and Bone Stone remain distinct siblings under broad stone/geological taxonomy.

## Q122 — Deepstone — final disposition

**Decision:** Retire **Deepstone as an active exact material identity**. Preserve `deepstone` only as a legacy/local geological category or terrain tag for deep ordinary rock. Old rows map contextually to actual local stone; Impossible Deep geology maps to Pressure Stone/Bone Stone/Emberbone where appropriate.

## Q123 — Deep Forge Stone migration

**Decision:** Keep retired as an independent natural family; FCC-13 migration maps old usages contextually to Pressure Stone, heat-treated Deep masonry, Bone Stone or Emberbone rather than resurrecting the placeholder.

## Q124 — Mineral/ceramic doctrine

**Decision:** Use composition-aware mineral identities, universal form/processing superclasses and state/profile metadata. Never invent a fantasy rock merely to mark depth, realm or progression.

## U06 Result

**U06: PASS — Q103–124 resolved. Deepstone is retired as an active material and retained only as category/legacy terminology.**

# U07 — Wood, Fibre, Hide & Biological Materials

## Q125 — Universal wood/timber superclass

**Decision:** All wood/timber materials share broad botanical structural taxonomy while exact species/fantasy timbers remain distinct.

## Q126 — Imported timber identity

**Decision:** Compatible woods may inherit log/timber, board/plank, beam, post/pole, rod/dowel, panel, shingle, veneer, peg and other physically valid forms.

## Q128 — Wood-form eligibility

**Decision:** Eligibility follows grain, strength, flexibility, size and specialist behaviour; not every wood automatically supports every structural form.

## Q132 — Reverie Wood processing

**Decision:** Reverie Wood inherits wood forms but specialist dream/coherence properties require coherence-safe processing where relevant.

## Q133 — Resonance Wood processing

## Q134 — Cinderwood processing

**Decision:** Cinderwood inherits wood/timber forms with Ashen heat/ash properties retained where physically intrinsic.

## Q135 — Rotwood

**Decision:** Rotwood remains a condition/state of compatible wood unless irreversible transformation produces genuinely distinct matter.

## Q136 — Plant Fibre — final disposition

## Q144 — Hide and leather

## Q145 — Chitin and shell

**Decision:** Use broad biological-hard-material classes with source-specific exact materials when anatomy/properties matter; cutting/plate/powder forms are capability-based.

## Q146 — Bone and Oathbone

**Decision:** Ordinary bone and Oathbone remain distinct siblings; Oathbone is not merely Ancestral provenance on ordinary bone.

## Q147 — Cinderhide and Titan Bone

**Decision:** Cinderhide remains distinct Ashen biological material. Titan Bone is provenance-sensitive special material only from legitimate Titan remains/shed history, never generic boss currency.
