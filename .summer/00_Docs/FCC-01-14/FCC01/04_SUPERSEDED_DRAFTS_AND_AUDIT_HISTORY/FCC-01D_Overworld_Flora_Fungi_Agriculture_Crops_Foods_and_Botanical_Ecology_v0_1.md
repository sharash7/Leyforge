# 1. Purpose

FCC-01D defines the authoritative **Overworld flora, fungi, agriculture, crop, food-foundation and botanical-ecology canon**.

It owns the content-level answers to questions such as:

- what ecological roles wild plants, fungi, mosses, lichens, reeds, shrubs, grasses, flowers and other producers must fill;
- how named gameplay-bearing flora differs from broad ambient botanical diversity;
- how decomposition, fungal ecology and soil renewal connect to world-state change;
- how forage, medicines, poisons, dyes, fibres, resins, saps, oils and other botanical outputs gain legitimate biological provenance;
- which wild botanical content is edible, medicinal, dangerous, industrially useful or ecologically significant;
- what the Overworld's principal cultivated food crops are;
- how crop species, cultivars, strains, domestication states and wild ancestors remain separate concepts;
- how orchards, fields, paddies, dryland farms, wetland agriculture, fungal farms, gardens, specialist plantations and controlled-environment agriculture fit the same world;
- how soil, water, climate, season, labour, pollination, fertility and infrastructure constrain production;
- how agriculture supports settlements without creating one universal staple crop or one mandatory farming biome;
- how wild foods, animal-derived food, fisheries and imported foods coexist with cultivated crops;
- how botanical material providers established in FCC-01C connect to actual living producers;
- how cross-realm plants and crops may be imported, cultivated, naturalised, stressed, quarantined or become invasive while preserving provenance;
- how agriculture and botanical ecology can be simulated persistently at Minecraft-scale without requiring every plant to run full local AI;
- which exact decisions must be handed to FCC-12, FCC-13, universal biology/nutrition systems, later balancing and art governance.

FCC-01D does **not** own:

- final timber/material identity or global material equivalence — FCC-01C / FCC-12;
- exact Block and Item rows — FCC-13;
- exact seed, cutting, spore, produce or processed-food registry rows — FCC-13;
- exact recipes, quantities, cooking times, crop-day values, yields, fertiliser rates or machine throughput — FCC-13 / balancing;
- universal hunger, nutrition, digestion, spoilage or biological-effect runtime — Set 29 / universal survival systems;
- exact medical treatment effects — Set 29;
- creature species, pollinator AI, livestock, fisheries or creature-derived products — FCC-01E;
- settlement economy, prices, labour markets or trade execution — FCC-01F / Set 27;
- exact farm-building definitions — settlement/building owners, especially Set 20;
- final colours, textures, plant models, animation, particles, growth VFX or sound — later art governance;
- technical crop tick rates, voxel representation, scheduler budgets or LOD distances — later technical canon.

The governing rule is:

> **FCC-01D defines what living botanical content exists or must be representable in the Overworld, what ecological/agricultural role it plays, how it relates to climate and civilisation, and what provenance constraints apply. It does not turn every flower into a registry object or every crop into a hard-coded numeric simulation.**

# 2. Canon Statement

The Overworld is biologically broad enough to support continents, climates, wild ecosystems and many civilisations.

That breadth does **not** require a unique gameplay object for every visible grass, mushroom or flower.

The final botanical philosophy is:

1. **Ecology before catalogue inflation.** Plants and fungi exist because they fill habitat, producer, decomposer, forage, medicinal, industrial, agricultural or narrative roles—not because every biome needs a new collectible.
2. **Named gameplay-bearing flora is bounded.** Distinct names are reserved for organisms or lineages whose identity matters to gameplay, ecology, materials, agriculture, medicine, culture, history, sites or progression.
3. **Ambient biodiversity may be broad.** Visual/procedural flora can vary by biome, region, season, altitude, moisture, soil and history without each variant becoming a separate Block/Item family.
4. **A plant is not its product.** Fibre, resin, sap, dye, bark, oil, food and medicine may be outputs of plants without requiring the output and producer to be the same registry identity.
5. **A crop is not a universal staple.** Different regions and cultures may depend on different grains, roots, legumes, fungi, orchards, fisheries and livestock.
6. **Cultivar is not species.** Climate adaptation, flavour, colour, yield, disease resistance, harvest timing and cultural breeding may create cultivars/strains without multiplying core crop species.
7. **Domestication is historical state.** A cultivated lineage can have wild relatives or ancestors without implying a duplicate material or crop definition.
8. **Agriculture remains physical.** Food comes from land, water, seed/stock, labour, infrastructure, time and ecological conditions; farms do not generate provisions because a building named “Farm” exists.
9. **Automation scales production, not reality away.** Automated farms, greenhouses, irrigation and magical support still require valid inputs, maintenance and environmental compatibility.
10. **Fungi are first-class ecology.** They may be decomposers, food, medicines, symbionts, hazards, crop foundations or subterranean producers.
11. **Wild gathering remains viable.** Forage, fungi, fruit, roots, aquatic plants and other wild foods can support exploration and survival without making farming unnecessary.
12. **Cross-realm ecology preserves provenance.** A foreign plant can grow in the Overworld only through legitimate transfer and suitable conditions; successful naturalisation does not rewrite its origin.
13. **Strange biology is not automatically realm leakage.** Magical, luminous, dark, dreamlike, root-heavy, volcanic or subterranean flora may be native Overworld ecology unless evidence establishes foreign provenance.
14. **Damage and recovery persist.** Deforestation, fire, drainage, overharvest, soil depletion, blight, pollution and invasive spread can produce persistent ecological change and restoration work.
15. **Food content and hunger runtime remain separate.** FCC-01D guarantees coherent ingredients and production pathways; universal systems decide biological consumption effects.

# 6. Named Gameplay-Bearing Flora Versus Ambient Biodiversity

A named plant/fungus should normally satisfy at least one substantial role:

- principal crop;
- distinct material producer;
- medicine or poison source with unique gameplay;
- food source;
- magical reagent source;
- critical creature-host relationship;
- dungeon/site-specific organism;
- culture or historical role;
- ecological indicator;
- restoration role;
- special hazard;
- distinctive cultivation system.

Cosmetic difference alone is insufficient.

A biome may therefore display many grasses, flowers, shrubs and fungi without the Item registry containing “Purple Flower #7”.

Ambient plant variation may encode:

- region;
- soil;
- moisture;
- season;
- age;
- height;
- colour;
- bloom;
- snow cover;
- drought;
- grazing;
- disease;
- disturbance;
- mana exposure;
- pollution;
- blight;
- restoration.

Those are normally states/variants/placement differences rather than new species rows.

# 7. Wild Flora Role Families

S16 requires every major environment to possess enough botanical foundation to support believable producer/decomposer/forage and resource relationships.

The principal **role families** are:

1. **Canopy / woody producers** — tree and shrub ecology.
2. **Grassland / meadow producers** — grazer support, thatch/fibre possibilities, seed food.
3. **Wetland / reed producers** — filtration, bank stability, fibre, nesting and wetland food.
4. **Dryland / drought-adapted flora** — browse, roots, seeds, medicines and erosion control.
5. **Cold / alpine / boreal flora** — short-season forage, moss/lichen systems and cold-adapted herbs.
6. **River / riparian flora** — bank stabilisation, forage, medicinal and fibre interfaces.
7. **Coastal / salt-tolerant flora** — dunes, marsh, estuary and maritime food/material roles.
8. **Aquatic producers** — submerged/floating vegetation and algae/kelp-like systems.
9. **Cave / subterranean producers and fungi** — fungal, chemosymbiotic or mana-supported systems where appropriate.
10. **Magical / ley-responsive flora** — native plants reacting to mana without automatic corruption or realm provenance.
11. **Disturbance / pioneer flora** — recolonisation after fire, flood, landslide, mining or abandoned agriculture.
12. **Decomposer and detrital flora/fungi** — nutrient cycling, deadwood breakdown and soil formation.

These role families are ecological requirements, not necessarily one species each.

# 8. Fungi and Decomposition

## 8.1 Fungi Are Not Merely Cave Decorations

Overworld fungi may contribute to:

- decomposition;
- nutrient recycling;
- soil fertility;
- root symbiosis;
- food;
- fermentation/culturing;
- medicine;
- poison;
- alchemy;
- cave ecology;
- settlement agriculture;
- decay indicators;
- habitat creation.

## 8.2 Natural Rot Is Not Void Corruption

Ordinary decomposition is healthy world ecology.

The following remain distinct:

- normal decay;
- disease;
- ecological blight;
- magical blight;
- curse;
- Void corruption.

A rotting log, fungus-covered forest floor or decaying crop is not evidence of the Void.

## 8.3 Deepcap Mushroom

**Deepcap Mushroom** is both:

- a fungal organism/cultivation lineage;
- one of the seven principal Overworld food crops.

It anchors genuine underground agriculture and allows subterranean settlements to produce food without requiring ordinary sunlight-dependent fields.

Its exact environmental requirements, growth timing, yields and processed foods remain downstream/balance work.

## 8.4 Wild and Cultivated Fungal Relationships

A wild fungus may become cultivated through:

- spore selection;
- substrate management;
- controlled humidity;
- cave farming;
- timber inoculation;
- compost beds;
- magical cultivation where appropriate.

Cultivation does not automatically create a second species identity.

# 40. Realm-Specific Botanical Boundaries

## 40.1 Verdant Covenant

Verdant crops/flora may be cultivated where compatible.

However:

- Greatheart/Living Heartwood provenance remains Verdant;
- a Greatroot in the Overworld does not become a Greatheart Tree;
- wild Overworld root magic does not create a Covenant Portal bypass;
- naturalised Verdant plants still retain historical origin.

## 40.2 Ancestral Veil

Spirit/memory-associated vegetation in the Overworld does not automatically originate in the Veil.

Imported Veil botanical content, where it exists, requires real provenance.

## 40.3 Somnolent Expanse

Dreamlike herbs, sleep-associated plants and hallucinogenic fungi can be native Overworld biology.

They do not create physical access to Somnolent Expanse.

Imported Somnolent living producers remain provenance-bearing.

## 40.4 Ascendant Reach

High-altitude plants, storm-adapted plants and floating-island flora can be native Overworld ecology.

Ascendant crops/plants require legitimate import.

Overworld skylands do not establish Ascendant provenance.

## 40.5 Impossible Deep

Overworld cave fungi—including Deepcap Mushroom—are not automatically Impossible Deep life.

Imported pressure/brine/gravity specialist organisms may require controlled habitats.

Mining deeper in the Overworld cannot convert local plants into Deep-native biology or bypass the Deepgate.

## 40.6 Ashen Lower Realms

Heat-, ash- and volcanic-adapted Overworld plants can exist natively.

Ashen crops and flora require real Ashgate/trade/migration provenance.

Volcanic similarity alone does not establish Ashen origin.
