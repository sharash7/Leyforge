# 1. Purpose

FCC-01C defines the authoritative **Overworld-facing geology, material, resource, processing-foundation and provenance canon**.

It owns the content-level answer to questions such as:

- what ordinary Overworld soils, rocks, sands, clays, salts and geological construction materials exist;
- which geological settings can produce those materials;
- how ordinary surface geology relates to deep, volcanic, hydrothermal, sedimentary, metamorphic and impact contexts;
- which timber, fibre, reed, vine, resin, sap and other botanical construction-resource families are genuinely Overworld-native;
- which ordinary, precious, alloy-capable and magical metals exist in the Overworld;
- which ore/deposit relationships and prospecting evidence support those metals;
- which gems, crystals, mana-bearing materials and exceptional mineral products exist;
- which fuel and strategic-resource roles the Overworld can support;
- which broad processing families can transform raw resources without defining final recipes;
- how grade, purity, quality, condition, provenance, contamination and state remain distinguishable;
- how extraction, depletion, renewability, restoration, recycling and historical salvage affect resource ecology;
- which genuine Overworld material capabilities can later satisfy portal, building, machine, magic and civilisation requirements;
- which old Atlas/prototype material concepts survive only as source candidates, migration evidence or downstream reconciliation items.

FCC-01C does **not** own:

- the final global determination that two cross-realm materials are physically identical, variants, states or derived forms — **FCC-12**;
- final material-equivalence tags or recipe substitution rules — **FCC-12**;
- exact Block rows, Item rows, processed-form rows or stable registry IDs — **FCC-13**;
- exact recipe inputs, quantities, times, energy, heat, station requirements or by-products — **FCC-13** and the universal recipe system;
- exact portal recipes or the mandatory portal dependency graph — Realm Access / FCC-13 / FCC-14;
- plant biology, crop ecology or wild-flora species definitions — FCC-01D;
- creature-resource anatomy and ethical harvesting — FCC-01E;
- economy, prices, labour and trade execution — FCC-01F / Set 27;
- final colours, textures, PBR/material-response values, models, particles or sound — later art governance;
- technical voxel deposit generation, chunk representation, extraction algorithms or performance budgets — the later Leyforge technical canon.

The governing rule is:

> **FCC-01C decides what material and resource content genuinely exists in the Overworld, where it comes from, what broad physical or magical role it has, and what provenance constraints later systems must respect. It does not turn those decisions into the final universal material database or recipe registry.**

# 2. Canon Statement

The Overworld is materially rich because it possesses a coherent world history, geology, climate, ecology and civilisation—not because every gameplay role receives a dedicated fantasy ore.

Resources must remain connected to the world that produces them.

A useful material should have an understandable relationship to at least one of:

- geology;
- climate;
- hydrology;
- ecology;
- cultivation;
- creature biology;
- magical geography;
- historical production;
- salvage;
- trade;
- realm contact.

The final material philosophy is therefore:

1. **Resources have origins.** Stone comes from geology, fibre from biology, ores from deposits, Mana Crystal from magical geology, and foreign matter from legitimate transfer.
2. **Materials are not a single vertical tier ladder.** Some providers improve particular capabilities, but later materials do not automatically replace earlier ones.
3. **Old materials remain useful.** Wood, stone, clay, glass, copper, iron, steel, silver and other common materials continue to matter in construction, repair, machines, settlement supply, culture, trade and specialised recipes.
4. **Capability precedes named tier.** A recipe, project or interaction should care about the required physical or magical property where substitution is appropriate rather than arbitrarily demanding a realm or progression label.
5. **Rarity is not strength.** Rare resources may be valuable because of a unique property, provenance, scarcity, ritual role or cultural demand without being universal upgrades.
6. **State is not automatically a new material.** Rich ore, polished stone, wet clay, charged crystal, rusted iron or corrupted material should normally remain an underlying material plus grade/condition/state unless the transformation produces a genuinely distinct substance.
7. **Provenance survives processing.** A material can be cut, smelted, forged, repaired, recycled, traded and reused while retaining meaningful source and custody history where gameplay requires it.
8. **Extraction changes places.** Mining, quarrying, forestry, peat cutting, salt extraction and industrial processing can alter terrain, ecology, water, labour demand, politics and settlement history.
9. **No arbitrary portal filler.** The Overworld already contains sufficient structural, botanical, conductive, vessel-grade and magical providers for later portal reconciliation. New materials must not be invented solely because a recipe needs another colour-coded ingredient.
10. **Foreign resemblance is not foreign provenance.** Basalt does not become Ashen Basalt because it forms near lava; strange crystal does not become Ascendant or Void material because it glows.
11. **Physical matter remains physical.** Cross-realm export may affect specialist magical behaviour, but ordinary physical properties are not erased merely by crossing a portal.
12. **Final global deduplication comes later.** FCC-12 decides shared identity, material equivalence, variants, derived forms and substitution semantics after all realm and Overworld rosters are available.

# 5. Material and Resource Architecture

## 5.1 Resource Is Not Automatically a Block or Item

A **resource foundation** describes a source/material relationship.

It does not automatically require:

- one terrain Block;
- one dropped Item;
- one ore Block plus duplicate ore Item;
- one processed Item for every visual state.

The definitive physical representation is chosen later by FCC-12/FCC-13 and universal registries.

## 5.2 Material Identity Versus Resource Source

A material can have several legitimate sources without becoming several materials.

For example:

- iron may come from several ore/deposit contexts;
- sand suitable for glass may occur on coasts, in rivers and deserts;
- timber families may have regional growth states;
- Salt may occur in evaporites, brines or managed extraction systems;
- mana-bearing material may occur in different geological grades or states.

Likewise, one source can yield several outputs through processing.

## 5.3 Provenance Categories

FCC-01C consumes the Overworld provenance architecture established by FCC-01A:

- Native;
- Universal / Common;
- Imported;
- Diaspora-associated;
- Historical-contact;
- Anomaly;
- Realm-derived.

Material records may additionally track useful source detail such as:

- region;
- deposit/site;
- geological formation;
- producer organism;
- mine/quarry/forest/farm;
- culture or workshop;
- crafter/processor;
- former owner;
- salvage site;
- realm of origin;
- contamination history;
- restoration/recycling history.

## 5.4 Conservation

Materials and resources are conserved through normal gameplay transactions.

Mining, processing, crafting, construction, trade, automation, storage and salvage do not create or destroy stock merely because a UI operation occurs.

Legitimate loss or transformation includes:

- consumed fuel;
- waste;
- slag;
- ash;
- breakage;
- corrosion;
- spoilage where biological;
- irreversible chemical or magical transformation;
- terrain depletion;
- disposal.

## 5.5 Provider Diversity

A content requirement may later admit:

- one exact identity;
- one family;
- one property requirement;
- a minimum grade/quality;
- a realm-compatibility requirement;
- a no-substitute identity-critical component.

FCC-12/FCC-13 own the final provider contracts.

The Overworld canon deliberately contains enough variety to make provider choice meaningful without turning every recipe into arbitrary scavenger-hunt gating.

# 6. Geological World Relationship

FCC-01B established the Overworld as a coherent geological world with continent-scale landforms, sedimentary basins, mountain belts, volcanism, erosion, rivers, glaciation and deep subterranean regions.

FCC-01C converts that world logic into resource context.

## 6.1 Principal Geological Contexts

Overworld material generation may use broad geological relationships such as:

- sedimentary basins and layered deposits;
- intrusive/plutonic geology;
- metamorphic belts;
- volcanic provinces;
- lava fields and cooled volcanic formations;
- hydrothermal systems;
- weathered uplands;
- alluvial/placer systems;
- floodplains and river terraces;
- coastal deposition;
- evaporite basins and brines;
- glacial deposits;
- karst/limestone systems;
- deep mantle/pipe-like rare-deposit contexts where worldgen supports them;
- impact structures;
- geothermal zones;
- leyline/mana geology;
- ancient artificial strata and machine-salvage contexts.

These categories guide world placement; they are not technical worldgen algorithms.

## 6.2 Geological Coherence

A major deposit should have a plausible content-level relationship to:

- host rock;
- regional geology;
- formation process;
- erosion/exposure;
- depth;
- water;
- thermal state;
- magical geography where relevant.

This prevents ore placement from becoming a globally uniform random sprinkle.

## 6.3 World Variation

No seed is required to place every deposit near every settlement.

Regional scarcity is desirable because it supports:

- prospecting;
- trade;
- migration;
- infrastructure;
- specialist industries;
- contested resources;
- exploration.

Mandatory progression must still remain reachable through globally validated alternatives, substitution, trade, travel or provider diversity.

## 8.3 Basalt

**Basalt is ordinary Overworld volcanic stone.**

It may occur in:

- volcanic provinces;
- basaltic plateaus;
- lava-field margins;
- cooled flows;
- intrusive/dyke-like expressions where worldgen supports them;
- quarry contexts.

Basalt is also present in Ashen Lower Realms canon.

FCC-01C therefore locks only the Overworld content truth:

> **Basalt genuinely exists as ordinary Overworld geology.**

FCC-12 later determines the final shared universal material identity and prevents unnecessary `Overworld Basalt` / `Ashen Basalt` duplication where the substance is physically the same.

## 8.4 Obsidian

**Obsidian is volcanic glass**, not merely another generic stone skin.

It forms in compatible volcanic-glass contexts and may support:

- sharp tools/edges where recipes allow;
- glass-like or magical applications;
- construction/decorative forms;
- alchemical or ritual vessels/components where physically justified.

- ordinary;
- rich;

where appropriate.

**Purity** describes unwanted inclusions/contamination or desired material concentration.

It matters especially for:

- ores;
- refined metals;
- glass;
- crystals;
- mana materials;
- alchemical resources.

## 23.3 Quality

**Quality** can describe workmanship, refinement, cut, structural consistency or crafted output quality.

- damaged;
- weathered;
- rusted;
- wet;
- cracked;
- polished;
- charged;
- depleted;
- contaminated;
- repaired.

**Provenance** records where something came from and what happened to it.

A material can be chemically identical to another piece while possessing different:

- historical value;
- ownership;
- legality;
- cultural meaning;
- ritual significance;
- contamination risk;
- trade value.

## 23.6 No One-Dimensional Rarity Ladder

Grade, purity, quality, condition, magical affinity and provenance must not be collapsed into a single colour-coded `rarity` stat.
