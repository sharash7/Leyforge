# 1. Purpose

FCC-01E Part 1 defines the authoritative Overworld content canon for ordinary terrestrial, freshwater, marine, aerial, amphibious, subterranean and volcanic fauna.

It owns:
- principal ordinary creature-family identity;
- food-web roles;
- habitat relationships;
- population, migration, territory and lifecycle content;
- predator/prey/scavenger/pest relationships;
- husbandry, livestock, work-animal and mount eligibility;
- fisheries/aquaculture content;
- creature-derived material provenance;
- settlement/agriculture/ecology interaction;
- ecological degradation and recovery;
- imported-organism suitability and provenance.

It does **not** finalise exact health, damage, armour, spawn rates, breeding timers, taming percentages, catch chances, drop quantities, nutrition, prices, movement equations, disease rules, final IDs, models, palettes, animation, audio or VFX.

Part 2 owns S20 magical fauna, undead, blight, foreign-provenance hostile ecology, constructs and the personhood/classification boundary.

---

# 2. Why FCC-01E Is Split

S18–S20 collectively contain **82 principal foundations**: 31 S18, 25 S19 and 26 S20.

A single physical file would be unnecessarily difficult to navigate. The split is editorial:

> **Part 1 = ordinary ecology and civilisation use. Part 2 = magical/undead/blight/construct ecology and classification/material boundaries.**

No new canon owner is created by the split.

---

# 3. Canon Statement

Creatures are living world actors, not interchangeable combat spawns.

A production creature should meaningfully participate in one or more relationships such as:
- grazing/browsing;
- predation;
- pollination;
- decomposition;
- scavenging;
- pest pressure;
- migration;
- habitat engineering;
- husbandry;
- transport/labour;
- material production;
- environmental clues;
- settlement pressure;
- conservation/restoration.

Combat is one role, not the entire identity.

Universal aggression is rejected. Predators may stalk, warn, retreat, scavenge, defend young, avoid settlements or attack livestock depending on ecology and state.

# 4. Authority Boundaries

**FCC-01B** owns biome, climate, hydrology, ocean/sky/cave region and environmental state.  
**FCC-01D** owns plants, fungi, crops and forage.  
**FCC-01E** owns the creature side of those relationships.  
**Set 29** owns physiology, injury, disease and treatment runtime.  
**Set 30** owns physical movement, riding, swimming, flight and traversal execution.  
**Combat** owns hit/damage resolution.  
**FCC-12** later owns global creature-material relationships where needed.  
**FCC-13** owns definitive harvested Items, exact recipes and quantities.

A creature name never overrides environmental truth or creates resources by itself.

# 7. Ecology Graph

Food webs are assembled from compatible authored relationships, not fixed one-creature-per-biome lists.

Useful ecological guilds include:
- grazer;
- browser;
- omnivore;
- mesopredator;
- apex predator;
- scavenger;
- decomposer;
- pollinator;
- seed disperser;
- pest;
- parasite;
- habitat engineer;
- filter feeder;
- schooling prey;
- domestic/work animal.

Population changes can propagate:
- apex removal may increase prey or mesopredators;
- grazer increase may increase crop pressure;
- wetland restoration may restore prey then predators;
- settlement waste may increase scavengers;
- overfishing may displace marine predators;
- habitat recovery may enable recolonisation.

These effects are causal and persistent, not arbitrary punishment rolls.

---

# 8. Population, Territory and Persistence

Populations may preserve:
- abundance;
- recruitment/age bands;
- habitat quality;
- range;
- food availability;
- predation;
- harvest pressure;
- migration state;
- nests/nurseries;
- disturbance;
- important individual identity where required.

Nearby animals may be full actors. Distant wildlife may be cohorts, territory records, school/herd summaries, nest records or regional populations.

A local presentation actor disappearing must not refill the population. Killing one representative cannot remove or generate more animals than the authoritative population contains.

Prefer recruitment through breeding, migration, recolonisation, nests, husbandry and valid events over unexplained infinite point spawning.

# 10. S18 Ecological Guilds

## 10.1 Ambient and Decomposer Ecology
Lantern Gnat, Threadwing Moth, Pebbleback, Hush Cricket, Ember Ant and Carrion Beetle provide environmental clues, pollination, decomposition and low-cost ecological activity.

Ambient life may be expressed through simplified agents, swarms, decals, calls or regional abundance while preserving underlying ecological truth.

## 10.2 Grazers and Browsers
Meadowhorn, Briarback Deer, Stonewool Ram, Dune Sailer, Mire Grazer, Frostmane Elk, Canopy Strider and Cliffback Ibex collectively cover major temperate, forest-edge, highland, desert, wetland, cold, rainforest and cliff/karst roles.

The game does not need recoloured “deer” for every biome.

## 10.3 Predators
Gloamcat, Silverfang Packhound, Sandglass Stalker, Rootmaw Bear and Stormclaw form the principal terrestrial predator set.

Predator danger should be readable through tracks, kills, calls, missing livestock, territory marks and NPC knowledge before combat.

## 10.4 Scavengers and Pests
Scrap Jackal, Crop Gnawer, Rotfly Swarm and Tunnel Snatcher connect ecology to camps, industry, farms, warehouses, battlefields and underground settlements.

## 10.5 Domestic / Work Fauna
Hearthhen, Woolhorn, Burdenback, Mossyak, Reedrunner and Tunnel Mole are principal terrestrial domestication/work foundations.

Domestic animals remain living actors with food, shelter, welfare, injury, social and ownership requirements.

## 10.6 Specialist Ordinary Fauna
Cavern Weaver and Magma Shell lock the rule that strange habitat/adaptation does **not** automatically mean magical, monstrous or realm-derived.

# 11. Predator Territory and Consequence

Predator territory may use:
- dens;
- tracks;
- scratch/mark sites;
- kill remains;
- calls;
- prey displacement;
- livestock incidents.

Removing a predator may make an area safer, but may also:
- free a territory for another predator;
- increase prey;
- alter pests;
- change migration.

Wildlife clearing is therefore persistent ecology, not a temporary spawn cooldown.

# 13. Freshwater Ecology

Freshwater habitats include rivers, streams, floodplains, lakes, ponds, wetlands, springs and subterranean waters.

Freshwater populations can respond to:
- flow;
- temperature;
- water quality;
- vegetation;
- flood/drought;
- dams;
- pollution;
- fishing;
- introduced species;
- settlement extraction.

A fish market does not spawn fish. Supply must come from a real population, aquaculture, inventory or trade route.

---

# 14. Marine and Coastal Ecology

Marine fauna consumes real environmental context:
- salinity;
- depth/pressure;
- temperature;
- oxygen;
- currents;
- tides;
- substrate;
- reef/kelp habitat;
- storm state;
- port/industry pressure.

Population forms can include schools, pods, colonies, benthic beds, nursery sites, migration corridors and habitat carriers.

Fishing consumes real stock. Overfishing can cause persistent decline. Recovery may involve reduced pressure, habitat restoration, spawning protection, aquaculture, migration or reintroduction.

---

# 15. Aerial Ecology

Native Overworld aerial fauna can use mountains, cliffs, rare skylands, thermals, wind corridors and storms.

**Overworld sky ecology is not Ascendant Reach.**

Flying higher, following a Cloudray or using a Skybridle Glider does not bypass the Ascension Gate.

Aerial fauna may interact with towers, airships, skyports, storms, seed dispersal and migration routes.

---

# 16. Amphibious and Intertidal Ecology

Tides and shore state can change:
- harvest access;
- nesting sites;
- predator routes;
- stranding risk;
- amphibious crossings;
- road/dock conflict.

Possible persistent events include nesting seasons, strandings, rescue, fish runs and shoreline migration.

---

# 17. Husbandry and Domestication

Useful relationship states include:
- wild;
- habituated;
- tamed individual;
- trained;
- managed herd;
- domesticated lineage;
- regional breed;
- feral;
- protected/sacred;
- work animal;
- companion;
- mount.

Not every family supports every state.

Husbandry requires real feed, water, shelter, space, handling, breeding stock, health, labour/automation and route access.

A building named Stable, Fishery or Ranch does not generate animals or outputs merely by existing.

---

# 18. Breed and Ecotype Rule

Differences in colour, coat, size, horn form, temperament, endurance, climate tolerance or local adaptation should normally be data/variant state within the family.

Create a new creature family only when identity is genuinely distinct.

This prevents registry explosion and supports the Forge's reusable body/animation architecture.

---

# 19. Mount and Work Roles

Mount, pack animal, war beast, plough animal, courier, quarry animal and ferry animal are use roles.

FCC-01E decides whether a family can plausibly support that role.

Set 30 owns actual ridden/hauling movement.

Skybridle Glider remains a glide-dominant aerial mount candidate; it does not become an air vehicle. Tideback Tortoise remains living coastal transport; it does not become a boat definition.

---

# 20. Fisheries and Aquaculture

Civilisations may fish, trap, net, hand-harvest, manage spawning grounds, cultivate shellfish, operate aquaculture and protect nursery/reef habitat.

Extraction remains conserved.

A viable fishery must connect:
**population → method → landing/storage → processing/trade/food use**.

Exact catch rates, Items, recipes and market values are deferred.

---

# 21. Creature Materials

A creature does not need a unique named material just because it exists.

Ordinary outputs can include meat, hide, bone, horn, antler, feather, egg, milk, shell, chitin, fibre, fat/oil, cocoon, manure or shed plate where biologically valid.

A separate named material is justified only when physical/magical properties or gameplay meaning genuinely require it.

FCC-01E owns biological provenance. FCC-12/FCC-13 own final material/Item identity and recipes.

---

# 22. Non-Lethal Resource Paths

Legitimate resource paths may include:
- eggs;
- milk;
- wool/fibre;
- shed antler;
- moulted feathers;
- shell/plate sheds;
- cocoons;
- manure;
- non-lethal samples;
- husbandry;
- trade;
- naturally dead carcasses.

Progression should not make every useful animal a loot pinata.

---

# 23. Hunting, Decline and Recovery

Hunting may support food, protection, culture, trade, research or population management.

Overhunting can cause scarcity, altered food webs, migration changes, legal conflict or local extinction.

Recovery can use reproduction, immigration, protection, habitat repair, reintroduction and managed breeding.

---

# 24. Agriculture Interface

FCC-01D and FCC-01E form one causal ecology:
- Briarback Deer may pressure orchards;
- Crop Gnawers may pressure fields/stores;
- predators may regulate pests or threaten livestock;
- pollinators may support compatible plants;
- Mire Grazers may reshape wetland channels;
- livestock grazing may fertilise or overgraze.

Default play should be meaningful without punitive micro-ecology. Deeper simulation settings may expose stronger ecological coupling where performance and player settings permit.

---

# 25. Settlement, Roads and Industry

Civilisation changes wildlife through clearing, farms, fences, roads, bridges, mines, lighting, waste, water diversion, ports, shipping and industry.

Wildlife may avoid, exploit, adapt to or conflict with those changes.

Settlement growth must not simply delete ecology without persistent habitat/population consequence.

---

# 26. Vessels and Wildlife

Marine/aerial fauna can respond to vessel wake, noise, collisions, fishing, ballast/cargo transfer, hull fouling, harbour lighting, spills and wrecks.

Attached organisms or cargo water can become invasive-transfer vectors.

The maritime runtime owns vessel physics; FCC-01E owns the populations affected.

---

# 27. Hazards, Events and Recovery

Fauna can respond to fire, flood, drought, storm, volcanic activity, pollution, habitat loss and magical environmental states through migration, dormancy, mortality, breeding change or settlement incursion.

Creature-event families may include herd migration, spawning run, nesting season, predator shift, population boom, infestation, stranding, invasive spread and conservation return.

S31 owns event lifecycle; FCC-01E supplies ecological content.

---

# 28. Tracking and Knowledge

Creature knowledge can come from tracks, calls, nests, remains, observation, local experts, books, instruments and repeated encounters.

Knowledge may progress from unknown → observed → studied → expert, while contested folklore can remain visible.

In-world beliefs do not overwrite developer truth.

---

# 29. Cross-Realm Fauna

Foreign organisms require legitimate physical provenance: portal transfer, historical contact, trade, deliberate introduction or valid cross-realm event.

After transfer they face suitability states such as:
- compatible;
- managed habitat;
- stressed;
- dormant;
- loses specialist capability;
- naturalised;
- invasive;
- cannot survive.

Naturalisation changes ecology, not origin history.

---

# 30. Anti-Leak Boundary

The following do **not** prove realm provenance:
- giant roots → Verdant;
- spirit/memory behaviour → Ancestral;
- dreamlike appearance → Somnolent;
- flight/storm adaptation → Ascendant;
- deep/cave adaptation → Impossible Deep;
- volcanic/ash adaptation → Ashen;
- strange/dark/blight appearance → Void.

Provenance must be explicit.

---

# 31. Simulation Scalability

The canon requires ecological truth, not millions of fully active actors.

Implementation may use full actors, lightweight agents, cohorts, region summaries, nests/nurseries and migration records.

Promotion/demotion must conserve population and any persistent individual identity, ownership/training, injury or breeding state that matters.

Exact algorithms are later technical-canon work.

---

# 32. Art / Forge Handoff

Future creature assets must communicate:
- body/movement family;

- juvenile/adult distinction where needed;
- husbandry equipment compatibility;
- material-producing anatomy where relevant;
- environmental adaptation.

Final colours, models, rigs, animation, sound and VFX are not locked here.
