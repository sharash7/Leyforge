# 1. Purpose

Step 2A extracted the Overworld-facing obligations and deliberate downstream deferrals from all six completed realm FCC sets.

Step 2B compared those obligations against:
- the other completed realms;
- the reconciled Foundation/source corpus;
- World Content Atlas content;
- production-governance systems;
- existing runtime/prototype registry content;
- legacy realm identities and portal assumptions.

Step 2C now assigns **one primary decision owner to every surviving issue**.

The objective is to prevent three failure modes before the FCC-01 questionnaire begins:

1. **FCC-01 absorbs global work it does not own.**
2. **FCC-01 fails to answer something the completed realms genuinely require from the Overworld.**
3. **A legacy/runtime migration issue is mistaken for a new content-design question.**

This is therefore a **routing and scope-control document**, not a new realm-content design pass.

# 3. Decomposition Rule

Several Step-2B issues contained multiple kinds of work.

For example:

> “What is the Dreamgate external prerequisite?”

actually contains at least three separate questions:

1. Does the **Overworld need content that can support Dreamgate discovery/contact?**
2. Does the **global realm-access graph require an earlier-realm dependency?**
3. What are the **final recipe rows and quantities?**

Those do not share one owner.

Step 2C therefore decomposes composite B-COL entries into subrows where necessary.

This prevents a misleading assignment such as “all portal work belongs to FCC-01.”
