# 1. Purpose

FCC-01B defines the authoritative environmental and world-generation content of the **Overworld**.

It owns the Overworld-facing canon for:

- continental and macro-landform structure;
- mountains, plateaus, basins, valleys, canyons and other major terrain grammar;
- watersheds, rivers, lakes, springs, wetlands, aquifers and inland-water relationships;
- climate, seasons, weather and natural-disaster context;
- ordinary natural surface-biome architecture and final family scope;
- biome mosaics, ecotones, sub-biomes, variants and transition rules;
- oceans, seas, coasts, island systems and marine environmental structure;
- the Overworld sky, rare skylands and high-altitude environmental content;
- underground topology, cave systems, cave provinces and subterranean regions;
- the finite Deep Overworld and its strict boundary with Impossible Deep;
- special magical, historical, artificial and transformed environmental overlays;
- environmental hazard context and persistent regional-state behaviour;
- environmental readability, settlement suitability, traversal and progression constraints;
- persistent environmental aftermath and world memory.

FCC-01B does **not** duplicate the complete registries for:

- stones, soils, ores, woods and other materials — FCC-01C;
- flora, fungi, crops and food ecology — FCC-01D;
- creatures and creature ecology — FCC-01E;
- peoples, settlements, politics and economy — FCC-01F;
- structures and infrastructure — FCC-01G;
- dungeons and bosses — FCC-01H;
- magic-system implementation, historical chronology, event runtime or cross-realm interfaces — FCC-01I and universal system owners;
- exact Block/Item/Recipe definitions — FCC-12/FCC-13;
- technical terrain-generator, voxel-streaming, fluid-simulation or LOD implementation — the later Leyforge technical canon.

The governing rule is:

> **FCC-01B defines what kinds of Overworld environments exist, how they relate to one another, what conditions they create for life and civilisation, and which environmental boundaries later content must obey.**

# 2. Canon Statement

The Overworld is a **coherent living fantasy planet-scale world**, not a sequence of disconnected terrain skins.

Its landforms, water, climate, ecology, history, civilisation, magic and dynamic state are related through deterministic seed-derived world structure. A mountain range should help explain rain shadows, river headwaters, passes, mines, settlement geography and political routes. A flood basin should explain wetlands, fertile soils, navigation, seasonal danger and settlement engineering. A desert should possess water logic, exposed geology, trade routes and ecological adaptation rather than existing only because a noise field selected a yellow palette.

The final Overworld therefore follows these environmental principles:

1. **Natural readability first.** Ordinary Overworld geography is recognisably natural fantasy. Strong magical or impossible environments are allowed, but unusual conditions require an explainable source.
2. **No empty beauty.** Every major environment must matter to survival, resources, ecology, travel, civilisation, adventure, magic, restoration or world state.
3. **No universal best biome.** Fertile and convenient regions gain different advantages from harsh, remote, mineral-rich, magical or strategically important regions.
4. **Travel has meaning.** Regional variation in resources, climate, terrain, danger, culture and infrastructure creates real reasons to explore and build transport networks.
5. **Older/common regions remain useful.** Common lowland or forest regions do not become obsolete because the player later reaches rare magical environments or other realms.
6. **Difficulty is layered.** Danger is not a simple function of distance from spawn. It can arise from weather, depth, territory, ecology, wealth, events, magic, bosses, structures and world settings.
7. **Terrain remains buildable.** Dramatic terrain must still expose plausible places, methods or engineering responses for habitation and infrastructure.
8. **Ecology is legible.** Habitats, tracks, migration, nests, damage, sounds and food-web relationships should make creature presence understandable.
9. **Disasters are fair.** Severe environmental events communicate warnings where fiction permits, allow preparation and produce recovery gameplay rather than arbitrary punishment.
10. **Procedural does not mean equal.** No seed must contain every environment in the same proportions or near the same starting point, but progression promises require alternatives, travel or substitution.
11. **Culture is layered over environment.** Climate affects how people build and live; it does not biologically assign cultures or ancestries to biomes.
12. **World memory persists.** Fire, flood, extraction, settlement, pollution, war, restoration, magical change and other significant outcomes may permanently alter the environmental record.

# 3. Authority and Ownership Boundaries

## 3.1 FCC-01B Owns Environmental Identity

FCC-01B determines whether an environmental concept is:

- a continent or macro-region feature;
- a terrain province;
- a watershed or hydrological feature;
- an ordinary base biome;
- a sub-biome or local variant;
- an ecotone;
- a marine/aerial/subterranean environmental family;
- a special overlay;
- a regional environmental state;
- a persistent transformed condition.

This document does not automatically create a new gameplay definition merely because an environment has a distinct appearance.

## 3.2 Later Content Consumes Environmental Context

Later FCC-01 documents use FCC-01B to decide where their content can exist.

Examples:

- FCC-01C binds materials to compatible geology and environments.
- FCC-01D binds plants and crops to climate, soil and water contexts.
- FCC-01E binds creatures to habitats, migration corridors and environmental states.
- FCC-01F binds settlements, agriculture, trade and civilisation to geography.
- FCC-01G binds structures and infrastructure to terrain and hazards.
- FCC-01H binds sites, dungeons and major threats to environmental suitability.
- FCC-01I binds magic, history, events and realm interfaces to environmental state without redefining the base world.

## 3.3 Universal Systems Remain Universal

Environmental content must consume rather than duplicate universal runtime systems for:

- movement and navigation;
- fluids and maritime execution;
- fire and damage;
- survival exposure;
- disease/biology;
- event runtime;
- corruption runtime;
- realm access;
- save/persistence;
- simulation LOD.

## 3.4 Realm Boundaries

The Overworld may contain environments that resemble another realm without becoming part of that realm.

The hard rule is:

> **Environmental resemblance is not provenance and is never a portal.**

Therefore:

- unusual forest growth is not automatically Verdant;
- spirits, graves and memory phenomena are not automatically Ancestral;
- dreams and nightmare-like environments are not automatically Somnolent;
- floating land, gravity anomalies and storms are not automatically Ascendant;
- extreme caves and strange depth are not automatically Impossible Deep;
- ash, magma and volcanic terrain are not automatically Ashen;
- darkness, instability and corruption-like presentation are not automatically Void.

# 4. Overworld Environmental Hierarchy

The environmental hierarchy extends the world topology established in FCC-01A.

The principal content-facing chain is:

> **World → Continent / Macro-region → Region → Terrain / Hydrological Province → Biome / Environmental Context → Local Feature / Site / Settlement**

Additional cross-cutting layers include:

- climate fields;
- watershed structure;
- coast and ocean-basin structure;
- altitude and sky-volume context;
- cave and subterranean topology;
- geology;
- historical layers;
- magical overlays;
- dynamic environmental states;
- civilisation influence;
- ownership/territorial overlays.

These layers are not required to map one-to-one onto technical runtime records. The hierarchy is a canon and authoring model.

## 4.1 Regions Are Larger Than Biomes

A region can contain several biomes and transitions while maintaining a coherent identity through:

- landform;
- climate;
- watershed;
- geology;
- resource pattern;
- settlement geography;
- route structure;
- history;
- political relationships;
- magical conditions.

A biome is primarily a local environmental/ecological context, not the sole determinant of regional identity.

## 4.2 Biome Mosaics

Large regions should normally generate as mosaics containing combinations such as:

- dominant base biomes;
- secondary biomes;
- riparian corridors;
- wetlands;
- ridges and altitude bands;
- coastlines;
- clearings;
- cliffs;
- sub-biomes;
- ecotones;
- settlement-altered land;
- rare special overlays.

This prevents kilometre-scale rectangles of one biome identity and allows geography to read as a connected system.

# 5. Continental and Macro-Landform Assembly

## 5.1 Continent-First Geography

The default Overworld is Minecraft-scale in exploration extent and supports multiple enormous landmasses, ocean basins and inland systems.

Large geography is established before local detail.

The generation logic should first determine broad relationships such as:

- major land/ocean distribution;
- continental outlines and shelves;
- long mountain chains;
- broad plateaus and plains;
- major basins;
- drainage divides;
- large river systems;
- glacial or erosional provinces;
- volcanic/geothermal provinces;
- major climatic gradients.

Local hills, ravines, small rivers, caves, vegetation and sites then inherit from this larger context.

## 5.2 Plausible Geological Formation Without Full Earth Simulation

Leyforge does not require a complete geophysical plate-tectonic simulator.

It does require enough causal structure that large landforms feel related.

The Overworld may use simplified influences analogous to:

- tectonic uplift;
- rifting;
- volcanism;
- erosion;
- sedimentation;
- glaciation;
- river incision;
- basin formation;
- coastal deposition;
- karst dissolution;
- magical or historical disturbance where explicitly justified.

The player should be able to understand why broad mountain, river, desert, wetland and coastal patterns exist without needing real-world geology expertise.

## 5.3 Major Landform Families

The Overworld supports broad landform families including:

- mountain chains;
- isolated mountains and massifs;
- foothills;
- rolling uplands;
- high plateaus;
- low plateaus;
- plains;
- prairies/steppes;
- valleys;
- glacial valleys;
- river valleys;
- canyons and gorges;
- escarpments;
- mesas/badlands;
- closed basins;
- salt basins;
- volcanic calderas;
- lava fields;
- karst uplands;
- sinkhole provinces;
- coastal cliffs;
- deltas;
- flood basins;
- dune fields;
- glacial and polar surfaces.

These are terrain/geographic families, not automatically separate biomes.

## 5.4 Continuous Elevation

Elevation should behave as a coherent world field rather than a set of biome-specific height presets.

Biome suitability may respond to elevation, but biome identity does not directly manufacture disconnected mountains.

Elevation influences:

- temperature;
- snow and ice;
- vegetation;
- river gradients;
- erosion;
- settlement suitability;
- route costs;
- wind exposure;
- cloud/fog conditions;
- mining/geological exposure.

## 5.5 Buildability

Dramatic terrain must preserve practical sandbox value.

Mountain and canyon regions should contain combinations of:

- valleys;
- shelves;
- terraces;
- passes;
- plateaus;
- caverns;
- cliff ledges;
- engineering routes;
- terraformable spaces.

The game should create construction challenges, not simply deny construction through terrain spectacle.

# 6. Watersheds and Inland Hydrology

## 6.1 Water Follows World Structure

Surface water is derived from terrain and climate relationships rather than being scattered as decorative ponds.

The Overworld supports:

- drainage divides;
- watersheds;
- major rivers;
- tributary networks;
- braided channels;
- seasonal channels;
- deltas;
- floodplains;
- lakes;
- glacial lakes;
- oxbows/backwaters;
- springs;
- wetlands;
- closed basins;
- aquifer-linked environments;
- geothermal water systems;
- underground water connections where appropriate.

## 6.2 Rivers

Major rivers are regional infrastructure and ecological systems.

They may influence:

- settlement location;
- fertile land;
- freshwater supply;
- transport;
- political borders;
- flood risk;
- fishing;
- mills/industry;
- ports;
- bridges and ferries;
- erosion;
- wetland formation;
- historical routes.

A large river should have an explainable headwater and downstream destination unless an explicit closed-basin, magical or engineered exception exists.

## 6.3 Lakes and Inland Seas

Lakes may arise from compatible terrain histories including:

- glacial basins;
- tectonic or rift basins;
- volcanic calderas;
- river processes;
- sink/karst systems;
- dammed valleys;
- artificial works;
- justified magical events.

Large inland seas can function as regional marine-like systems while remaining part of the Overworld's inland geography.

## 6.4 Wetlands

Wetlands emerge from hydrology, substrate, flood frequency, drainage, vegetation and water chemistry.

Wetland identity is not a synonym for “swamp.”

The final surface-biome roster includes several distinct wetland bases and retains additional wetland concepts as local expressions, transitions or states where appropriate.

## 6.5 Springs and Groundwater

Springs may become important for:

- settlement viability;
- pilgrimage;
- agriculture;
- caves;
- geothermal environments;
- magical research;
- desert survival;
- historical sites.

The world may contain groundwater/aquifer logic at the content level even if the technical implementation later uses abstraction rather than a full volumetric aquifer simulation.

## 6.6 Flooding and River Change

Rivers and floodplains may change through events and persistent state.

Potential consequences include:

- temporary inundation;
- erosion/deposition;
- damaged roads/bridges;
- altered navigation;
- fertile sediment;
- wetland expansion/contraction;
- abandoned or rebuilt settlements;
- changed river channels where the world-event contract supports it.

Large hydrological change must be attributable and persistent rather than an unexplained chunk reload difference.

# 7. Climate Foundation

## 7.1 Multi-Field Climate

Overworld climate is generated from overlapping deterministic fields rather than direct biome lookup.

Principal influences include:

- latitude / broad solar-climate band;
- altitude;
- moisture;
- ocean moderation;
- continentality;
- prevailing exposure;
- topographic barriers;
- orographic rainfall;
- rain shadows;
- hydrology;
- seasonality;
- storm pressure / storm-track tendency;
- justified persistent magical or historical modifiers.

These fields combine to create climate suitability before final biome selection.

## 7.2 Latitude Is Important but Not Absolute

Latitude helps establish broad climate expectations but does not single-handedly determine biome.

Mountain elevation, coastlines, currents, interior continentality, rain shadows and magical/historical states can create substantial regional departures.

## 7.3 Ocean Moderation

Large water bodies moderate nearby temperatures and influence:

- humidity;
- storm formation;
- seasonal range;
- fog;
- snow/rain patterns;
- coastal ecology;
- agriculture.

Interior regions may experience stronger seasonal extremes and drier conditions where geography supports them.

## 7.4 Altitude Cooling

Altitude broadly lowers temperature and changes exposure.

This can create:

- montane forests;
- alpine meadows;
- snowlines;
- glaciers;
- cold plateaus;
- high-altitude storm regions

within otherwise warmer latitude bands.

## 7.5 Orographic Rainfall and Rain Shadows

Mountain systems may force moist air upward, increasing precipitation on exposed slopes and decreasing it in leeward regions.

This gives mountain placement consequences for:

- forests;
- grasslands;
- deserts;
- rivers;
- agriculture;
- settlement and trade.

## 7.6 Magical Climate Influence

Native Overworld magic may modify local or regional climate where a valid source exists.

A magical modifier is not permission to ignore geography without explanation.

Likewise, foreign realm influence must have explicit provenance and interface rather than being inferred from unusual weather.

# 8. Seasons

## 8.1 Seasonal World Layer

The Overworld supports meaningful seasonal variation.

Season can affect:

- temperature;
- precipitation;
- snow and ice;
- river levels;
- wetland extent;
- plant growth;
- crop suitability;
- animal migration;
- breeding;
- fire risk;
- road conditions;
- maritime conditions;
- settlement provisioning;
- festivals and cultural activity;
- labour and trade;
- event eligibility.

## 8.2 Regional Seasonal Expression

The same global period need not look identical everywhere.

Examples include:

- strong winter/summer contrast in continental climates;
- wet/dry seasonal cycles in tropical/savanna regions;
- mild maritime seasons;
- short growing seasons in polar/alpine regions;
- flood seasons in monsoon or snowmelt basins.

## 8.3 Seasonal State Is Not a New Biome

Snow cover, flowering, drought stress, leaf colour, flooding and other recurring seasonal presentations normally modify a base biome rather than creating separate permanent biome definitions.

A genuinely transformed long-duration ecology may later qualify for a different identity, but seasonal presentation alone does not.

## 8.4 Verdant Boundary

The Overworld's seasons are its own environmental system.

Verdant Covenant seasonal states, Courts or events do not automatically replace or globally control Overworld seasons.

Any cross-realm seasonal influence requires an explicit supported interface and provenance.

# 9. Weather and Natural Disasters

## 9.1 Weather Families

The Overworld supports ordinary and severe weather appropriate to climate and terrain, including broad phenomena such as:

- rain;
- drizzle;
- thunderstorms;
- windstorms;
- fog;
- snow;
- blizzards;
- hail where supported;
- heatwaves;
- cold snaps;
- drought conditions;
- coastal storms;
- dust/sand storms;
- magical weather where a legitimate native or foreign source exists.

This list describes weather content classes rather than a final technical weather enum.

## 9.2 Storm Tracks

Severe weather is influenced by regional climate and storm tendency rather than being uniformly random across the map.

Players can therefore learn which coastlines, plains, mountain barriers or seasonal periods are commonly dangerous.

## 9.3 Natural Disasters

Environmental disasters are severe event/state relationships, not a separate type of terrain.

Potential Overworld disasters include:

- flood;
- drought;
- wildfire;
- severe storm;
- blizzard;
- landslide/rockfall;
- avalanche;
- volcanic eruption or awakening;
- cave or terrain collapse where the owning systems support it;
- ecological collapse;
- severe magical/environmental crisis with valid provenance.

Detailed event-family identities and runtime selection belong to FCC-01I / the event system.

## 9.4 Fairness and Warning

Major hazards should be communicated through valid evidence such as:

- weather signs;
- environmental change;
- instruments;
- NPC reports;
- animal behaviour;
- smoke;
- tremors;
- rising water;
- visible storm fronts;
- magical sensors where appropriate.

Settings may adjust warning, severity and permanent consequence.

## 9.5 Persistent Aftermath

Disaster recovery does not necessarily restore the exact old world.

Possible persistent aftermath includes:

- changed channels;
- burned forest succession;
- rebuilt infrastructure;
- abandoned districts;
- new flood defences;
- soil change;
- altered migration;
- memorials;
- displaced populations;
- new settlement policies;
- changed resource access.

# 10. Surface-Biome Architecture

## 10.1 Final Biome Identity Layers

FCC-01 locks the following distinctions:

### Biome Family
A broad environmental grouping used for inheritance, authoring and classification.

### Base Biome
A canonical ordinary environmental identity with enough persistent ecological/terrain distinction to justify its own base entry.

### Sub-biome
A meaningful subordinate environmental expression of a base biome or family.

### Variant
A bounded expression caused by geography, season, management, local substrate or other non-identity-changing context.

### Ecotone
A transition zone between environmental identities.

### Overlay
A cross-cutting condition layered over a base environment while its underlying identity remains meaningful.

### Dynamic State
A current condition such as burned, flooded, overharvested or restoring that can change without redefining the entire biome.

The same visual difference must not be registered as a new biome merely because its palette, vegetation density or hazard pressure differs.

## 10.2 Biome Selection Inputs

Ordinary surface biome selection may consider:

- climate;
- elevation;
- slope;
- hydrology;
- geology/substrate;
- exposure;
- adjacency;
- history;
- mana/magic;
- civilisation impact;
- rarity/world-profile constraints.

## 10.3 Biome Mosaics and Ecotones

Biome boundaries should normally transition through understandable environmental change.

Ecotones may alter:

- vegetation density;
- soil;
- moisture;
- snow;
- water behaviour;
- habitat suitability;
- resource clues;
- route cost;
- settlement suitability;
- ambience.

Hard boundaries are permitted where a genuine geological, hydrological, artificial or magical boundary explains them.

## 10.4 No Biome-Culture Determinism

A biome may influence:

- architecture;
- diet;
- transport;
- farming;
- industry;
- settlement layout;
- seasonal practices.

It does not automatically assign:

- ancestry;
- culture;
- faction;
- morality;
- government;
- religion.

# 12. Surface-Biome Design Rules

Every final base biome must support a meaningful subset of the following contracts:

- readable climate/terrain cause;
- water relationship;
- characteristic vegetation/ecology;
- resource opportunities;
- travel/traversal consequence;
- settlement suitability/constraint;
- agriculture or food relationship where appropriate;
- threat/adventure suitability;
- seasonal expression;
- dynamic-state response;
- restoration/exploitation response;
- world-history hooks.

A biome should not exist solely because it has a distinct palette.

## 12.1 Common Biomes Remain Valuable

A common meadow, woodland or river basin can remain strategically important because of:

- agriculture;
- population;
- transport;
- construction materials;
- trade;
- political location;
- large-scale building;
- restoration;
- industry.

Rare environments provide different opportunities rather than invalidating common ones.

## 12.2 Rare Does Not Mean Automatically Harder

Biome rarity and danger are separate relationships.

A rare environment can be peaceful.

A common environment can become extremely dangerous through:

- war;
- weather;
- disease;
- boss pressure;
- occupation;
- magical contamination;
- world events.

# 13. Oceans, Seas and Maritime Geography

## 13.1 Oceans Are Full Regions

Oceans are not world borders or visual emptiness.

They are enormous explorable regions containing:

- depth bands;
- currents;
- climate relationships;
- islands;
- ecosystems;
- resource geography;
- routes;
- hazards;
- structures;
- settlements;
- ruins;
- dungeons;
- major creatures and bosses;
- persistent events and aftermath.

Ocean crossings can be genuinely long at the Minecraft-scale baseline.

## 13.2 Multiple Ocean Basins and Inland Seas

A world may contain:

- several major ocean basins;
- enclosed or semi-enclosed seas;
- island seas;
- narrow straits;
- large gulfs/bays;
- inland seas;
- archipelagic regions.

No single mandatory global ocean topology exists.

## 13.3 Marine Environmental Family Architecture

Marine environmental content is organised through broad contexts such as:

### Coastal Interface
Variables can include substrate, slope, tide range, wave exposure, sediment, vegetation and river input.

### Shallow Marine
Variables can include light, temperature, salinity, substrate, nutrients, currents and shelter.

### Pelagic
Variables can include major currents, temperature, productivity, storm regime and distance from land.

### Deep Marine
Variables can include depth, pressure, slope, vents, oxygen, sediment and other local influences.

### Island Surface
Variables can include origin, age, relief, climate, freshwater, isolation and history.

### Transformed Marine
Special magical, historical, contamination or other persistent overlays may transform ordinary marine environments when a valid source exists.

# 15. Overworld Sky and Skylands

The governing rule is:

- high mountains;
- cloud layers;

- gravity-anomaly sites.

## 15.3 Skyland Rarity

Skylands are **rare provinces**, not the dominant Overworld topology.

- flying high enough;
- climbing a floating island;
- riding an airship;
- following a wind current;
- entering a storm;
- falling from a skyland;
- reaching the technical top of the Overworld sky volume.

Normal physical access remains the **Ascension Gate**.

- high plateaus;

The underground is not a collection of isolated caves placed under surface biomes.

It is a large connected environmental layer supporting:

## 16.2 Canonical Cave Hierarchy

The locked underground hierarchy is:

> **Local Feature → Cave System → Cave Province → Deep Route → Subterranean Region**

### Local Feature
Examples include a chamber, fissure, sinkhole, local tunnel, grotto or small water feature.

### Cave System
A connected local network containing several features and entrances.

### Cave Province
A large regional grouping characterised by geology, water, ecology and access relationships.

### Deep Route
A major traverse, shaft, road, river, mine, tunnel, fault or engineered corridor linking deeper provinces.

### Subterranean Region
A large underground environmental/civilisation region comparable in importance to major surface regions.

## 16.3 Entrances and Connectivity

- sinkholes;
- sea caves;
- river caves;
- lava tubes;

- mines;
- shafts;
- wells;
- engineered tunnels;
- dungeon/site entrances.

Required progression content must not be accidentally unreachable because every possible entrance generated sealed or incompatible.

## 16.4 Underground Civilisation

- ventilation;
- water;

- food systems;
- freight routes;
- structural support;
- evacuation;
- mining/industry;
- social infrastructure.

## 16.5 Player Excavation

The player may create persistent tunnels, mines and connections where physical capability permits.

Cave/dungeon challenge does not justify arbitrary unbreakable geology.

Authored sites may protect indispensable semantic machinery from accidental softlock, but the surrounding world remains governed by real material/capability rules.

# 17. Deep Overworld

## 17.1 Finite Physical Depth

The Overworld has a **finite physical deep layer**.

# 18. Special Overlays and Exceptional Environments

## 18.1 Overlay-First Rule

Strongly magical, historical, corrupted, blessed, artificial, dreamlike, gravity-altered or other exceptional conditions normally use:

> **Base Environment + Overlay / State**

when the underlying biome remains recognisable.

A dedicated environmental identity is justified only when transformation is deep enough to alter the terrain/ecology/environmental contract beyond a normal overlay.

## 18.2 Exceptional Environments Need a Source

A special environment must have a valid cause such as:

- leyline or mana node;
- ancient structure;
- historical catastrophe;
- ritual;
- boss/titan influence;
- ecological process;
- magical contamination;
- machine/industrial complex;
- divine/sacred interaction;
- realm-contact event;
- Void provenance;
- player/civilisation action;
- unknown but explicitly authored anomaly.

It should not appear solely because a generic noise threshold says “make this area weird.”

## 18.3 Overlay Data Contract

Special overlays should expose content-facing information equivalent to:

- **Source** — what created it;
- **Intensity** — how strong it is;
- **Footprint** — point, corridor, patch, watershed, biome, region, vertical column or moving front;
- **Spread** — none, seasonal, resource-driven, creature-carried, waterborne, route-following, event-driven or other supported pathway;
- **Persistence** — temporary, cyclical, recoverable, stabilised, persistent, effectively permanent or irreversible where genuinely justified;
- **Transformation Domains** — terrain, water, weather, light, ecology, resources, structures, behaviour, magic and other supported systems;
- **Counterplay/Response** — avoidance, research, wards, infrastructure, diplomacy, cleansing, adaptation, containment, exploitation, coexistence or major encounter resolution;
- **Outcome** — restored, adapted, contained, exploited, worsened, converted, hybridised or unresolved.

This is a content contract, not a final save schema.

## 18.4 Native Magical Regions

The Overworld can naturally contain:

- mana-rich areas;
- leyline crossings;
- crystal-rich magical environments;
- spirit-active places;
- gravity anomalies;
- bounded time anomalies;
- native dreamlike or memory-sensitive environments;
- magical weather zones;
- ancient magical scars.

None requires another realm merely because it is unusual.

## 18.5 Realm-Touched Environments

A true realm-touched/realm-leak state requires an explicit provenance chain.

Valid causes can include:

- damaged canonical portal infrastructure;
- historical realm-contact event;
- transported realm material/organism causing local transformation;
- authored cross-realm breach;
- other explicitly defined interface.

It is never inferred from theme alone.

- altered routes;
- destroyed structures;
- settlement-altered land;

Presentation may simplify at distance; authoritative truth does not.

The same world seed and world-profile/version should resolve the same authoritative environmental plan where no save delta changes it.

- landmarks;
- routes;
- maps;
- rumours;
- ecological clues;
- geology;
- weather;
- archaeology;
- magical evidence.

## 26.6 Persistence

Player and world changes that are canonically persistent must survive:

- chunk unload;
- save/load;
- LOD transitions;
- long-distance simulation.

## 26.7 Performance Is a Technical Proof Requirement

Minecraft-scale world extent, caves, oceans, persistent edits and dynamic environmental states are design requirements.
