# 4. Infernal

**Infernal** is a broad realm-linked category.

It may refer to:

- native Ashen peoples;
- realm-associated lineages;
- magical beings historically associated with the Lower Realms;
- cultures with long-term Ashen identity;
- realm-derived institutions or aesthetics.

Infernal is **not**:

- one biological species;
- one ancestry;
- one culture;
- one government;
- one morality;
- one faction.

An infernal person may be:

- kind;
- cruel;
- ordinary;
- ambitious;
- devout;
- secular;
- scholarly;
- criminal;
- heroic;
- authoritarian;
- rebellious;
- apolitical.

The term describes realm-linked identity, not alignment.

---

# 5. Demon

**Demon** is retained as a broad cultural, historical or supernatural label.

Depending on speaker and context, it may refer to:

- infernal peoples generally;
- dangerous infernal beings;
- particular magical lineages;
- historical enemies;
- outsider categories;
- religious interpretations.

It is **not** one canonical species record.

This rule prevents a generic `species.demon` from swallowing distinct peoples, magical beings and fauna.

Different cultures may disagree over who or what counts as a demon.

Those disagreements are lore/culture, not developer taxonomy.

The Ashen Lower Realms is not biologically exclusive.

# 8. Mortal Enclave

A **Mortal Enclave** is a settlement/cultural category for populations whose ancestry or historical origin is primarily outside the Lower Realms.

Possible populations include:

- Overworld migrants;
- descendants of traders;
- refugees;
- exiles;
- scholars;
- missionaries;
- merchants;
- diplomatic communities;
- mercenaries;
- mixed descendants;
- long-established diasporas.

“Mortal” is a traditional Ashen term.

It does **not** mean infernal peoples are immortal.

A Mortal Enclave may become deeply Ashen in culture across generations while retaining historical origin.

# 9. Construct Classification

Ashen civilisation distinguishes at least three broad construct states:

1. **Tool / Machine Construct**
2. **Bound Intelligent Construct**
3. **Free / Awakened Construct Person**

These are social and ontological categories, not automatically three biological species.

---

# 10. Tool / Machine Construct

A Tool / Machine Construct is a non-sapient constructed system.

It may perform:

- lifting;
- transport;
- furnace operation;
- mining;
- sorting;
- pumping;
- cooling;
- defence;
- routine logistics.

It may be owned as equipment where universal machine rules permit.

Non-sapient machine ownership must not be confused with sapient-person ownership.

---

# 11. Bound Intelligent Construct

A **Bound Intelligent Construct** is demonstrably intelligent/sapient but subject to a legal, contractual, magical or institutional constraint.

Possible constraints may concern:

- employment;
- movement;
- service;
- duty;
- access;
- maintenance;
- contractual obligation;
- legacy programming.

Its sapience remains meaningful.

The realm may contain political disagreement over:

- whether such binding is legitimate;
- how it can be amended;
- whether historical contracts remain valid;
- what rights the construct possesses.

The game's personhood model must not reduce this category to ordinary inventory property.

---

# 12. Free / Awakened Construct Person

A **Free / Awakened Construct Person** is a sapient construct recognised or self-recognised as a person.

Such persons may:

- own property;
- work;
- form households;
- vote where law permits;
- join factions;
- establish communities;
- travel;
- form contracts;
- resist coercion;
- inherit culture;
- create new traditions.

Exact universal personhood systems belong outside FCC-08.

FCC-08F locks that construction origin does **not** automatically deny personhood.

---

# 13. Sapient Person ≠ Ordinary Inventory Property

No sapient person may be reduced to an ordinary transferable inventory object.

This remains true even where a coercive regime claims:

- ownership;
- debt service;
- forced labour;
- prison control;
- contractual service;
- inherited obligation.

A regime may commit or institutionalise coercion.

The underlying simulation should still represent the affected being as a person/NPC rather than generic property.

This allows slavery, forced labour and emancipation politics to exist as serious world-state content without corrupting personhood architecture.

The Ashen Lower Realms is not ruled by one canonical infernal empire.

It contains many:

- city-states;
- kingdoms;
- republics;
- communes;
- oligarchies;
- war states;
- merchant polities;
- sovereign regimes;
- industrial city powers;
- monasteries;

Exactly eight major civilisation **foundations** are locked for FCC-08.

They are not eight mandatory factions.

A specific civilisation may overlap several.

- coercive bondage;

## 19.2 Emancipated Community

**Emancipated Community** describes a settlement or faction founded substantially by:

# 28. Contract ≠ Law ≠ Ownership ≠ Sovereignty

FCC-08F preserves the core separation:

- **Contract** = explicitly accepted supported obligation.
- **Law** = authority attempting to enforce rules over territory/subjects.
- **Ownership** = recognised claim over property/resource/infrastructure.
- **Sovereignty** = claim to political authority over territory/population.

These can overlap.

They are not automatically identical.

Examples:

- a Contract House may own a warehouse but not govern the city;
- a sovereign may govern land but not own every mine;
- a traveller may enter a territory without accepting a magical contract;
- a trade road may be privately maintained but publicly governed;
- a city may reject a House contract without denying House property.

---

# 29. Contract Acceptance Boundary in Civilisation

No settlement, building, road, flower, sign, market, gate or jurisdiction silently binds a character into a magical contract merely through presence.

Valid contract formation requires:

- readable terms;
- recognised acceptance;
- supported universal contract framework;
- clear parties and obligations where required.

Civilisations may still enforce declared law through ordinary institutions.

A government may:

- fine;
- arrest;
- restrict entry;
- confiscate under law;
- challenge ownership;

without pretending those consequences came from an accepted magical contract.
