# 2. Canon Statement

The Ashen Lower Realms uses exactly **eight top-level biome families** for current FCC purposes:

1. **Ember Marches**
2. **Red Ledger Dominion**
3. **Cinder Seas**
4. **Hunger Pits**
5. **War-Furnace Barrens**
6. **Sootglass Gardens**
7. **Chainwind Heights**
8. **Black Ember Hollows**

These eight biome families are distributed across exactly **nine world-scale Lower Strata**:

1. **Lower Stratum I — Ashen Frontier**
2. **Lower Stratum II — War Marches**
3. **Lower Stratum III — Contract Realms**
4. **Lower Stratum IV — Chain Depths**
5. **Lower Stratum V — Hunger Deeps**
6. **Lower Stratum VI — Ruin Hollows**
7. **Lower Stratum VII — Ambition Realms**
8. **Lower Stratum VIII — Dominion Realms**
9. **Lower Stratum IX — Furnace Crown**

The nine strata are **not** nine biomes.

The eight biomes are **not** eight separate dimensions.

The realm is one persistent world in which:

- each stratum may contain multiple biome families;
- biome families may recur at several depths;
- recurrence becomes depth-conditioned rather than becoming a new top-level biome;
- political borders may cut across biome boundaries;
- one biome family may exist under several different governments;
- one government may control territory across several biome families;
- regional states may overlay any compatible biome/stratum combination;
- settlements and dungeons occupy sites inside those layers rather than replacing them;
- the same biome can be peaceful in one region and catastrophic in another;
- deeper generally raises the realm's environmental and capability envelope without forcing every deeper square metre to be more dangerous than every shallower one.

The Ashen Lower Realms therefore uses a layered environmental model rather than a simple sequence of themed floors.

# 3. Environmental Classification Model

Ashen Lower Realms generation, mapping and simulation must keep five related concepts distinct.

## 3.1 Lower Stratum

A **Lower Stratum** is a world-scale depth region.

It establishes broad environmental and progression context such as:

- depth;
- typical heat envelope;
- geological resistance envelope;
- industrial scale;
- dominant political pressures;
- likely biome weighting;
- settlement engineering expectations;
- material opportunity;
- ordinary route difficulty;
- access to deeper infrastructure.

A stratum is not a single terrain palette.

## 3.2 Biome Family

A **biome family** is an environmental/ecological/civilisation landscape foundation.

It defines recurring environmental grammar such as:

- ash plains and lava canals;
- ledgered territory and contract-built infrastructure;
- Cinder-Sea medium and chained islands;
- appetite-shaped basins;
- industrial forge wastelands;
- cultivated black-glass estates;
- hot-wind mountain/aerial terrain;
- cooled ruin-caverns and sanctuary hollows.

A biome family can occur in multiple strata.

## 3.3 Territorial / Political Overlay

Political control is not a biome.

A territory may be:

- occupied;
- disputed;
- sovereign;
- liberated;
- rebel-held;
- Contract-House controlled;
- war-state controlled;
- neutral;
- abandoned.

Political ownership can change while the underlying biome remains.

## 3.4 Site Occupancy

A site is a local feature placed inside environmental context.

Examples include:

- settlement;
- dungeon;
- fortress;
- farm;
- mine;
- Ashgate complex;
- industrial works;
- prison;
- market;
- refuge;
- ruin.

A site does not automatically redefine the surrounding biome.

## 3.5 Regional State

A **regional state** is a temporary or persistent overlay such as:

- Furnace Surge;
- Ashstorm;
- Warfront;
- Ledger Shift;
- Hunger Pressure;
- Chain Crisis;
- Cinder-Sea Disturbance;
- Void-Corrupted State.

A state may transform gameplay conditions without creating a new biome.

This distinction is authoritative.

# 4. Realm-Wide Environmental Principles

## 4.1 Depth-Conditioned Recurrence

Biome recurrence is intentional.

A biome appearing deeper in the realm may change through:

- higher heat;
- more extreme geology;
- larger industrial scale;
- older infrastructure;
- stronger local powers;
- harsher route conditions;
- different ecological adaptations;
- different material quality;
- historical damage or occupation;
- stronger regional-state expression.

This does not require renaming the biome.

An Ember Marches region in Stratum II and an Ember Marches region in Stratum VIII remain part of the same top-level family while expressing radically different depth conditions.

## 4.2 Safe Territory Exists at Every Depth

Every stratum may contain:

- settlements;
- refuges;
- farms;
- negotiated safe districts;
- protected trade routes;
- controlled industry;
- functioning civic life.

Deeper does not mean constant passive damage.

The deeper trend is expressed through:

- a higher risk envelope;
- stronger capability demands;
- more extreme hazards;
- more expensive infrastructure;
- more powerful regional actors;
- harder-to-maintain safe zones.

A well-engineered Stratum-VIII district may be safer than an active Stratum-II Warfront.

## 4.3 Environmental Danger Is Learnable

Routine environmental danger must be readable through compatible cues.

Possible signals include:

- temperature instruments;
- ash density;
- visible furnace exhaust;
- route warning signs;
- contract/territorial markers;
- water/cooling alarms;
- chain/lift condition;
- smoke;
- lava flow;
- creature migration;
- Cinder-Sea behaviour;
- NPC reports;
- map-state changes;
- settlement notices;
- alarms and sirens;
- vibration;
- environmental sound;
- lighting changes;
- infrastructure shutdowns.

Uncertainty may remain, but severe hazards must not rely primarily on invisible surprise.

## 4.4 World Change Leaves History

Major environmental, political and industrial changes may persist through:

- damaged structures;
- new ruins;
- repaired routes;
- altered borders;
- liberated prisons;
- refugee districts;
- abandoned farms;
- exhausted industrial zones;
- opened mines;
- rerouted lava canals;
- changed Cinder-Sea access;
- new bridges;
- new tunnels;
- changed ownership;
- updated maps;
- NPC memory;
- event history.

The realm must not feel like every crisis disappears when a timer expires.

## 4.5 Native Ashen Conditions Are Not Automatically Void Corruption

The following are not Void corruption by default:

- infernal heat;
- fire;
- ash;
- Cinder Seas;
- Hunger-Pit appetite phenomena;
- Black Ember;
- Cold Fire;
- Contract magic;
- coercive politics;
- oppressive law;
- war;
- industrial pollution;
- unusual infernal creatures;
- sovereign power;
- ancient ruins.

Genuine Void corruption requires actual Void provenance.

Detailed corruption lifecycle is owned by FCC-08I.

## 4.6 No Realm-Wide Generic Exposure Bar

Ashen environmental danger must remain decomposed into meaningful systems.

Heat belongs to temperature/exposure systems.

Smoke and toxic atmosphere belong to breathing/toxin systems.

Scarcity belongs to food, logistics and settlement systems.

Law belongs to legal/faction systems.

Contracts belong to explicit accepted-contract systems.

Industrial risk belongs to machinery, structure and hazard systems.

Void corruption belongs to corruption systems.

The realm does not collapse these into one generic **Ashen Exposure** meter.

# 6. Stratum I — Ashen Frontier

## 6.1 Core Environmental Role

The **Ashen Frontier** is the principal first-arrival stratum.

It establishes the realm's environmental language without requiring immediate exposure to its most extreme forms.

Typical regional identity includes:

- ashland terrain;
- basaltic volcanic geography;
- moderate-to-severe heat zones;
- cooled refuges;
- trade routes;
- frontier industry;
- farms adapted to ash and heat;
- Cinder-Sea margins;
- ruined and reoccupied hollows;
- ordinary infernal settlement life.

## 6.2 Preferred Biome Expression

Strong or common environmental families include:

- **Black Ember Hollows** — primary upper-realm sanctuary/ruin expression;
- **Ember Marches** — common frontier ashland/military expression;
- **Cinder Seas** — common where geography supports them;
- **War-Furnace Barrens** — occasional industrial regions.

Other biome families are not automatically inserted merely to increase variety; distribution follows the locked preference matrix.

## 6.3 Safety Expectations

Stratum I must be capable of generating validated regions suitable for:

- first Ashgate arrival;
- cooling refuge;
- initial trade contact;
- ordinary exploration;
- settlement support.

This does not make the stratum harmless.

Heat, ash, smoke, war, Cinder-Sea hazards and local political danger remain valid.

## 6.4 Progression Function

The Ashen Frontier teaches:

- heat management;
- Ashen atmosphere management;
- legal/territorial readability;
- native transport;
- resource/trade relationships;
- the fact that infernal civilisation is ordinary civilisation rather than a universal hostile mob layer.

- siege corridors;
- lava canals;
- fortified ridges;

- damaged terrain;
- industrial support zones;

## 7.2 Preferred Biome Expression

Primary/strong families include:

- **Ember Marches** — primary;
- **War-Furnace Barrens** — primary;
- **Chainwind Heights** — strong;
- **Cinder Seas** — common.

## 7.3 Dynamic Borders

Borders may move through persistent world state.

A military victory may change:

- checkpoints;
- patrols;
- road access;
- settlement control;
- supply routes;
- siege damage;
- legal jurisdiction.

The biome itself does not move merely because a faction changes control.

## 7.4 Environmental Consequence

Warfront conditions can amplify otherwise manageable environmental problems.

Examples:

- destroyed cooling lines;
- blocked water routes;
- damaged bridges;
- overloaded fuel storage;
- smoke from bombardment;
- abandoned fields;
- collapsed tunnels.

War is therefore a world-state hazard, not just a higher enemy spawn rate.

# 9. Stratum IV — Chain Depths

## 9.1 Core Environmental Role

The **Chain Depths** emphasise extreme vertical geography, suspended infrastructure, transport chains, lifts, prisons, coercive institutions and liberation routes.

Typical terrain may include:

- high cliffs;
- vast shafts;
- suspended fortresses;
- lift networks;
- chained platforms;
- prison roads;
- hot wind corridors;
- narrow passes;
- refuge caverns.

## 9.2 Preferred Biome Expression

Strong families include:

- **Chainwind Heights** — primary;
- **Hunger Pits** — secondary;
- **Black Ember Hollows** — common refuge expression;
- **Red Ledger Dominion** — possible;
- **Cinder Seas** — common where geography permits;
- **War-Furnace Barrens** — occasional.

## 9.3 Jurisdiction and Falling

The older concept that falling may “bind” travellers to lower jurisdictions is reconciled as follows:

- falling can physically relocate a traveller into another controlled territory;
- guards, local law or custody may then apply;
- route rights may change because the traveller has crossed a real boundary;
- no magical contract is accepted merely because the player fell.

This rule is authoritative.

## 9.4 Liberation Geography

Resolved prison or transport sites may become:

- safe routes;
- rebel strongholds;
- refugee corridors;
- liberated settlements;
- contested transport hubs.

Environmental persistence must support these outcomes.

# 14. Stratum IX — Furnace Crown

## 14.1 Core Environmental Role

The **Furnace Crown** is the deepest ordinary Lower Stratum in current FCC scope.

It represents the strongest known ordinary combinations of:

- extreme heat;
- ancient industry;
- deep geology;
- Furnace-Crown works;
- sovereign infrastructure;
- realm-climax political pressure;
- rare energetic systems;
- high-capability excavation and metallurgy.

## 14.2 Biome Expression

Furnace Crown is **not** a ninth top-level biome.

It is a stratum whose regions are formed from deep expressions of compatible existing biome families.

Possible distribution includes:

- **War-Furnace Barrens** — extreme;
- **Hunger Pits** — extreme;
- **Red Ledger Dominion** — possible;
- **Ember Marches** — possible;
- **Sootglass Gardens** — rare;
- **Chainwind Heights** — rare;
- **Black Ember Hollows** — rare ancient;
- **Cinder Seas** — rare.

## 14.3 No Mandatory Constant Lethality

Even Stratum IX may contain:

- fortified settlements;
- Crown Works settlements;
- engineered refuges;
- safe industrial interiors;
- negotiated territories.

Its danger lies in the capability required to create and maintain safety.

## 14.4 Current-Scope Boundary

Stratum IX is the deepest ordinary Lower Stratum in the current FCC.

FCC-08B does not create:

- a hidden tenth stratum;
- a secret micro-realm beneath Furnace Crown;
- an automatic Void layer;
- a new portal-access tier.

Future expansion must explicitly reconcile any such proposal.

# 26. Environmental Difficulty Envelope

Depth creates a broad trend, not a universal monotonic rule.

A deeper region may normally have:

- greater heat;
- stronger infrastructure demands;
- more resistant geology;
- more severe industrial conditions;
- rarer safe routes;
- stronger political powers;
- more expensive recovery.

However:

- a stable deep city can be safe;
- an upper Warfront can be deadly;
- a shallow Ashstorm can exceed a calm deeper district;
- a ruined deep region may be cooler than an active upper furnace zone.

The game must not assign danger based only on stratum number.

# 51. Regional-State Combination Rules

Compatible states may coexist.

Examples include:

- **Warfront + Ashstorm** — reduced visibility during active military conflict;
- **Furnace Surge + Hunger Pressure** — increased water/food stress under extreme heat demand;
- **Ledger Shift + Chain Crisis** — legal/ownership instability during liberation or repression;
- **Cinder-Sea Disturbance + Warfront** — dangerous convoy routes and naval blockade;
- **Furnace Surge + Cinder-Sea Disturbance** — changing thermal sea profiles;
- **Void-Corrupted State + Warfront** — a war occurring inside a genuinely corrupted region.

Not every theoretical combination must be supported.

State combinations must be:

- valid;
- deterministic;
- save-safe;
- readable;
- bounded;
- non-contradictory.

Where two state outcomes conflict, the implementation must resolve ownership/priority explicitly rather than silently overwrite world truth.

# 86. Realm-Local Lock Summary

The Ashen Lower Realms environmental canon is built on **layered diversity rather than nine themed levels**.

Its core environmental rules are:

- exactly nine world-scale Lower Strata;
- exactly eight top-level biome families;
- strata and biomes are separate classifications;
- biomes recur across depth;
- deeper expressions may intensify without becoming new top-level families;
- political control overlays environment rather than defining species/biome;
- every stratum may contain safe engineered civilisation;
- danger depends on actual conditions and regional state, not only floor number;
- Ember Marches preserve mobile warfront geography;
- Red Ledger Dominion preserves readable legal/ownership landscapes without hidden contract acceptance;
- Cinder Seas are real Ashen sea environments with bounded state-driven behaviour;
- Hunger Pits preserve appetite ecology without owning universal Hunger;
- War-Furnace Barrens preserve industrial environmental transformation;
- Sootglass Gardens preserve beauty, surveillance and political risk;
- Chainwind Heights preserve vertical/hot-wind infrastructure;
- Black Ember Hollows preserve ruin, cooling, refuge and sanctuary;
- Furnace Crown is a stratum, not a ninth biome;
- inter-stratum geology remains environmentally real and physically breachable;
- twelve hazards feed appropriate universal systems;
- nine regional states overlay compatible regions;
- twelve event families provide environmental world-change foundations;
- major change is readable, bounded and persistent;
- player and faction engineering can alter routes and terrain;
- near/far simulation may scale computation without changing authoritative history;
- native Ashen danger remains distinct from genuine Void corruption.
