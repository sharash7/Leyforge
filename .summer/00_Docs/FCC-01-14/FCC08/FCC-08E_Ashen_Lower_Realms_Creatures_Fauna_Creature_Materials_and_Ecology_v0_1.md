# 1. Purpose

FCC-08E defines the authoritative creature, fauna, creature-material provenance and animal-ecology canon for the **Ashen Lower Realms**.

It owns the realm-local definition of:

- exactly twelve principal native creature families;
- ecological roles and food-web relationships;
- biome and stratum affinities;
- locomotion and habitat use;
- heat-, ash-, Cinder-Sea-, vertical- and industrial-environment adaptation;
- grazing, browsing, predation, scavenging, filter feeding and detritivory;
- pollination relationships;
- the principal biological provenance of Cinderhide;
- the principal living provenance of Cinder Pearls;
- husbandry, aquaculture, fishing and creature-management compatibility;
- the biological meaning of Devourer;
- the role categories War Beast and Garden Predator;
- exceptional-individual rules;
- Ember Leviathan megafaunal boundaries;
- creature responses to hazards, regional states and realm events;
- cross-realm fauna suitability;
- creature-registry discipline;
- boundaries separating wildlife from sapient peoples, elementals, constructs and civilisation roles.

FCC-08E does **not** finalise:

- exact creature health;
- exact damage;
- exact armour values;
- exact AI-state implementation;
- exact spawn rates;
- exact breeding timers;
- exact taming percentages;
- exact drop quantities;
- exact meat/nutrition values;
- exact husbandry building recipes;
- exact fishing mechanics;
- exact boss statistics;
- final creature models;
- final animation timing;
- final sound design;
- final colour palettes;
- final stable IDs for every new creature family unless already established elsewhere;
- dungeon guardian or Realm Authority encounter implementation.

Those belong to later FCC-08 documents, FCC-12/FCC-13, implementation-facing registries and later art governance.

# 2. Canon Statement

Ashen Lower Realms fauna is a **functioning ecology**, not a list of infernal enemies.

The realm contains creatures that:

- graze;
- browse;
- filter feed;
- scavenge;
- hunt;
- pollinate;
- burrow;
- migrate;
- recycle industrial waste;
- stabilise food webs;
- support agriculture;
- support fisheries;
- produce useful biological materials;
- interact with settlements;
- respond to war;
- respond to Hunger pressure;
- respond to Cinder-Sea disturbance;
- respond to Ashstorms and Furnace Surges.

Many principal creatures can be dangerous.

Danger is not the same as universal hostility.

A creature's behaviour should arise from combinations of:

- ecology;
- territory;
- hunger;
- breeding;
- defence;
- age;
- injury;
- training;
- regional state;
- corruption;
- civilisation interaction.

# 5. Ashmane Kelgrim

## 5.1 Identity

The **Ashmane Kelgrim** is a large ashland predator characterised by:

- a heat-adapted body;
- a visually prominent ember-bright or heat-reactive mane region;
- strong territorial behaviour;
- endurance across ash plains and warm rocky terrain.

It is one of the realm's principal large terrestrial predators.

## 5.2 Ecology

Ashmane Kelgrims may prey on:

- small herd animals;
- juvenile Duraels;
- Voles;
- smaller Striders;
- carrion;
- other suitable fauna.

They may follow:

- herd migration;
- battlefields;
- drought;
- frontier livestock routes.

## 5.3 Behaviour

Typical behaviour can include:

- territorial marking;
- ambush;
- pursuit;
- scavenging;
- defence of young;
- avoidance of heavily fortified settlement centres.

Not every Kelgrim attacks the player on sight.

Threat may depend on:

- distance;
- territory;
- hunger;
- injury;
- presence of young;
- training;
- war conditions.

## 5.4 Civilisation Interface

Kelgrims may be:

- feared predators;
- culturally revered;
- hunted;
- relocated;
- trained by specialist handlers;
- used as guard or War Beast animals where individual temperament permits.

Routine domestication is not assumed.

## 5.5 Material Boundary

FCC-08E does not create a new principal “Kelgrim Hide” material family by default.

Ordinary meat, hide, tooth, claw or bone products may exist where anatomically and culturally justified, but final Item definitions belong to FCC-08G/FCC-13.

# 6. Emberhorn Durael

## 6.1 Identity

The **Emberhorn Durael** is a heat-adapted herd grazer/browser related to the broader Durael lineage where that wider lineage is globally retained.

It is one of the Ashen Lower Realms' most important manageable large animals.

## 6.2 Ecology

Duraels may feed on:

- Cinderthorn browse;
- Ashreed;
- Ember Moss;
- crop residues;
- managed fodder;
- compatible grass/minor flora.

Herd movement can:

- disperse seeds;
- open vegetation;
- create trails;
- support predator ecology.

## 6.3 Civilisation Uses

Where culture permits, Duraels may support:

- pack transport;
- riding;
- draught work;
- herd meat;
- milk;
- horn;
- hide;
- manure;
- trade.

This makes the Durael a major livestock candidate without making every culture use it identically.

## 6.4 Temperament

Wild herds may:

- flee;
- charge when cornered;
- defend calves;
- migrate around heat and food.

Domesticated breeds may differ through:

- size;
- horn form;
- coat/hide;
- temperament;
- heat tolerance.

Breed variation should prefer data/variant systems over duplicate species IDs.

# 7. Moltenhide Strider

## 7.1 Identity

The **Moltenhide Strider** is a large long-legged volcanic and ashland animal adapted to:

- hot terrain;
- rough basalt;
- ash flats;
- furnace-adjacent routes;
- intermittent high-temperature exposure.

It is the principal canonical living producer of **Cinderhide**.

## 7.2 Ecology

Striders may function as:

- grazers;
- browsers;
- opportunistic feeders on fungal/lichen systems;
- long-distance migratory animals.

Their long limbs and thermal adaptation support travel across terrain hostile to many other large animals.

## 7.3 Cinderhide Provenance

Current canon establishes:

> **Moltenhide Strider is the principal Cinderhide source.**

Cinderhide remains a material family owned by FCC-08C.

Other suitable Ashen fauna may yield:

- lesser grades;
- different grades;
- specialist Cinderhide-compatible material;

where future biology justifies it.

Cinderhide must not become a generic drop from all infernal fauna.

## 7.4 Husbandry

Striders may be managed for:

- hides;
- transport;
- pack work;
- difficult-route travel;
- controlled breeding.

Exact domestication difficulty is deferred.

## 7.5 Non-Mandatory Exploitation

Progression should not require exterminating wild Strider populations.

Legitimate Cinderhide supply may come from:

- husbandry;
- hunting;
- trade;
- salvage;
- naturally recovered material where biologically plausible;
- political or commercial supply chains.

# 8. Sunjaw Behemoth

## 8.1 Identity

The **Sunjaw Behemoth** is massive heat-storing reptilian megafauna capable of long dormancy and extremely slow energy cycling.

It is a normal creature family, not a mandatory boss species.

## 8.2 Ecology

Depending on ecotype, Sunjaw Behemoths may be:

- giant grazers;
- browsers;
- omnivores;
- opportunistic scavengers.

Their sheer scale can affect:

- trails;
- vegetation;
- water access;
- settlement routes;
- migration corridors.

## 8.3 Dormancy

A dormant Behemoth may resemble:

- terrain;
- a rocky mound;
- an ash-covered ridge;
- a warm geological feature.

Readable biological cues should exist for experienced players and local cultures.

## 8.4 Landscape Hazard

Waking, feeding or moving Behemoths can create regional hazards through:

- trampling;
- structural damage;
- route blockage;
- panic among other fauna.

These effects arise from size, not arbitrary magical boss rules.

## 8.5 Exceptional Individuals

Ancient or unusually large Sunjaw Behemoths may become:

- named regional encounters;
- settlement crises;
- sacred animals;
- hunting targets;
- migration events.

Exceptional does not automatically mean fixed Realm Authority.

# 10. Furnace-Bellied Toad

## 10.1 Identity

The **Furnace-Bellied Toad** is a warm-pool and volcanic-wetland amphibian adapted to:

- cooling basins;
- geothermal pools;
- artificial water systems;
- condensation gardens;
- warm marsh-like environments.

## 10.2 Ecology

It may feed on:

- insects;
- Voles' smaller competitors;
- larvae;
- worms;
- small aquatic life.

It can support pest control around agriculture and water systems.

## 10.3 Defensive Behaviour

Selected variants may possess bounded defensive adaptations such as:

- short heat discharge;
- steam burst;
- pressure croak;
- volatile gland secretion.

Not every Furnace-Bellied Toad explodes.

Any dangerous discharge must be:

- telegraphed;
- biologically bounded;
- proportionate to creature scale.

## 10.4 Settlement Interface

Toads may thrive around:

- Cooling Monasteries;
- reservoirs;
- greenhouse water systems;
- village ponds;
- industrial cooling channels.

Some cultures may deliberately encourage them for ecological services.

# 11. Blackglass Basilisk

## 11.1 Identity

The **Blackglass Basilisk** is a specialist predator associated with:

- Sootglass Gardens;
- Hellglass-rich terrain;
- reflective ruins;
- dark high-temperature estates.

Its defining encounter identity uses **thermal/reflection perception and gaze-associated status effects**, not unconditional instant-kill petrification.

## 11.2 Hunting Ecology

Basilisks may exploit:

- reflections;
- heat mirage;
- visual fixation;
- sudden movement;
- ambush cover.

Their prey may include:

- small and medium fauna;
- livestock;
- Garden animals;
- isolated travellers.

## 11.3 Gaze / Perception Effect Boundary

Supported effects may include bounded:

- disorientation;
- fixation;
- heat mirage;
- temporary glass-like or thermal status;
- vulnerability;
- sensory disruption.

FCC-08E explicitly rejects a default unavoidable instant-death gaze.

## 11.4 Civilisation Interface

Blackglass Basilisks may be:

- estate hazards;
- deliberately kept Garden Predators;
- hunted animals;
- symbolic creatures;
- security animals under specialist handlers.

Any captive use must preserve believable danger and welfare/containment needs.

# 13. Cinderback Mollusc

## 13.1 Identity

The **Cinderback Mollusc** is a large Cinder-Sea filter-feeding molluscan family adapted to:

- hot mineral-rich fluid/slurry environments;
- chained islands;
- sheltered Cinder-Sea basins;
- cooler mineral currents;
- settlement aquaculture zones.

It is the principal living producer of **Cinder Pearls**.

## 13.2 Cinder Pearl Provenance

Current canon establishes:

> **Cinderback Mollusc is the principal biological source of Cinder Pearls.**

Cinder Pearl material identity remains owned by FCC-08C.

Pearls may be obtained through:

- aquaculture;
- naturally abandoned shells;
- naturally deceased individuals;
- managed harvesting;
- wild collection;
- trade;
- archaeological stores.

## 13.3 Ecology

Cinderback Molluscs may filter:

- suspended microorganisms;
- ash-organic particles;
- mineral-associated producers;
- fine detritus.

They can contribute to:

- local fluid clarity;
- nutrient cycling;
- nursery habitat.

## 13.4 Aquaculture

Cinderback aquaculture may require:

- correct thermal range;
- suitable Cinder-Sea chemistry;
- food availability;
- current flow;
- protection from storms and predators.

This prevents Cinder Pearl from becoming an arbitrary generic mob drop.

# 16. Slag Beast

## 16.1 Identity

The **Slag Beast** is a semi-living mineral/thermal organism family associated with:

- furnace waste;
- molten slag environments;
- industrial ruins;
- War-Furnace Barrens;
- Furnace-Crown Works;
- abandoned foundries.

It is native/realm-generated life, not a factory robot category.

## 16.2 Ecology

Slag Beasts may consume or metabolise:

- metal-rich waste;
- furnace residues;
- mineral detritus;
- slag;
- industrial by-products.

They may:

- reshape slag beds;
- expose inclusions;
- concentrate minerals;
- contaminate work areas;
- recycle otherwise useless waste.

## 16.3 Industrial Interface

Depending on context, Slag Beasts may be treated as:

- pests;
- scavengers;
- useful waste-processors;
- managed industrial organisms;
- hazards.

Civilisations may attempt controlled management without converting them into constructed machines.

## 16.4 Material Boundary

Slag Beast bodies may contain ordinary recoverable mineral inclusions.

This does not justify a generic new “Slag Beast Metal” family.

Any harvested matter must resolve to real anatomical/mineral provenance.

# 23. Labour Construct Boundary

**Labour Constructs** are not wildlife.

They belong to constructed-being and civilisation systems.

Potential states include:

- non-sapient machine/tool construct;
- bound intelligent construct;
- free/awakened construct person;
- escaped construct;
- abandoned construct;
- damaged or malfunctioning construct.

Creation origin does not automatically determine personhood.

FCC-08F owns civilisation/personhood consequences.

FCC-08G owns constructed components.

FCC-08H may use individual constructs as encounters where appropriate.

---

# 24. Imp / Infernal Person Boundary

Imps are sapient infernal people, not fauna.

Therefore:

- Bureaucratic Imp is an NPC/civilisation role;
- old hostile Ash Imp / Ember Imp wildlife treatment is superseded;
- an individual imp can still be hostile;
- hostility arises from person/faction/context rather than wildlife taxonomy.

Likewise “Demon” remains a broad cultural/historical/supernatural label rather than one generic animal species.

---

# 25. Debt Collector Boundary

**Debt Collector** is an occupation, institution, construct role or legal office.

It is not a biological species.

A Debt Collector may be:

- an imp;
- another sapient person;
- an intelligent construct;
- an institutional role.

FCC-08F owns the civilisation role.
