# 1. Purpose

FCC-03I defines the authoritative **dynamic-state and cross-realm interaction layer** of the Ancestral Veil.

It exists to answer one question:

> **When Ancestral content changes, what kind of change is it, what owns that change, how is it stored, how can it be restored, and when does it become a genuinely different canonical thing?**

FCC-03I owns realm-local canon for:

- recognition states;
- memory solidity;
- identity stability;
- route availability and route recognition;
- historical reflection state;
- boundary stability;
- Necropolis activation and instability;
- native deterioration and recovery;
- Void corruption as a distinct external influence;
- cleansing and restoration;
- irreversible transformation gates;
- regional/site state persistence;
- interactions among hazards, settlements, dungeons, authorities, bosses, titans and realm events;
- direct locked cross-realm interfaces;
- low-cost deterministic implementation rules;
- save-safety boundaries.

FCC-03I does **not** own:

- universal death or respawn;
- universal personhood;
- universal save/load;
- universal portal runtime;
- the cosmological origin of Void corruption;
- dream-network simulation;
- Pocket Realm construction;
- exact crafting recipes;
- final material/block/item registries;
- final art profiles.

Those systems are consumed through their owning documents.

# 2. Dynamic-State Canon Statement

The Ancestral Veil is a realm where **recognition, memory, identity, history, routes and embodiment can matter mechanically**, but those concepts must be represented through bounded game state rather than unrestricted metaphysical simulation.

The governing principles are:

1. **Authoritative truth is never silently deleted.**
2. **Presentation may be disrupted without destroying underlying data.**
3. **Recognition is explicit, not inferred from arbitrary language.**
4. **Memory is bounded, not a recording of every thought or event.**
5. **Historical reconstruction may be contested.**
6. **Necropolis is local engineered/political state, not universal afterlife routing.**
7. **Native Veil instability is not Void corruption.**
8. **Corruption is usually a state before it becomes a new definition.**
9. **Restoration may produce a new stable future rather than recreating the past exactly.**
10. **Cross-realm interaction never creates a second normal access method; Ancestral entry remains Veilgate-based.**

# 3. State Architecture

Ancestral content may carry several independent state axes.

| State Axis | What It Represents | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition State** | Whether a name, title, claim, route, destination or authority is mechanically recognised | unrecognised, recognised, disputed, revoked |
| **Memory Solidity** | How strongly a selected place/record/phenomenon is represented and supported | fragmentary, reconstructed, corroborated, restored |
| **Identity Stability** | Reliability of recognition-dependent interaction for an entity/site | stable, strained, unstable, disrupted |
| **Route State** | Availability and reliability of a Processional or relationship-linked route | open, restricted, unstable, lost, restored |
| **Historical Reconstruction State** | Confidence/completeness of a historical representation | unknown, fragmentary, disputed, corroborated, falsified |
| **Boundary Stability** | Stability between physical and spirit-active interaction layers | stable, thin, unstable, breached |
| **Necropolis State** | Presence/condition of local engineered persistence, embodiment or cessation systems | inactive, maintained, unstable, failed, transformed |
| **Corruption State** | Degree/type of Void influence | clean, exposed, contaminated, corrupted, transformed |
| **Restoration State** | Current recovery trajectory | damaged, stabilising, restoring, restored, new-stable-state |
| **Event State** | Whether an authored/procedural major realm event is active | inactive, active, resolving, aftermath |

These axes may interact, but they must not be collapsed into one universal “Veil corruption” meter.

# 4. Recognition State

## 4.1 Definition

Recognition is a bounded system state that establishes mechanical significance for:

- names;
- titles;
- office;
- citizenship;
- claims;
- witnesses;
- archived identities;
- routes;
- settlements;
- historical locations;
- authorised visitors;
- court standing.

Recognition can arise through explicit supported actions such as:

- introduction;
- registration;
- archive confirmation;
- legal decision;
- ritual acknowledgement;
- evidence recovery;
- appointment;
- inheritance;
- diplomacy;
- authored realm interaction.

## 4.2 Recommended State Family

A recognised object may use one or more explicit statuses such as:

- **Unknown / Unrecognised**
- **Known**
- **Recognised**
- **Disputed**
- **Restricted**
- **Revoked**
- **Superseded**

These are not necessarily a linear progression.

## 4.3 Safety Rule

Recognition state may change:

- route access;
- Court standing;
- archive permissions;
- structure responses;
- NPC services;
- claim legitimacy;
- selected creature behaviour;
- dungeon outcomes.

It may **not** erase the canonical identity of the underlying player, NPC, settlement or record.

A Remnant Eater, Devouring Silence event or Predator Dark exposure may temporarily hide or disrupt recognition-dependent presentation while the authoritative data persists underneath.

# 5. Memory Solidity

## 5.1 Definition

Memory Solidity describes how strongly a selected memory-active structure, reflection, record or regional phenomenon is supported by valid evidence and realm state.

It does **not** measure how many people globally remember something.

Valid inputs can include:

- documented evidence;
- archive restoration;
- local remembrance state;
- recognised records;
- settlement maintenance;
- recovered artefacts;
- authority outcomes;
- major realm events;
- historical investigation.

## 5.2 Canonical Descriptive States

The following descriptors may be used where appropriate:

- **Absent / Unmanifested**
- **Fragmentary**
- **Disputed**
- **Reconstructed**
- **Corroborated**
- **Falsified**
- **Restored**
- **Suppressed**

These labels describe different properties and are not all one numeric ladder.

A Forgotten City district might be:

- highly solid but historically disputed;
- fragmentary but accurately corroborated;
- physically restored after a false reconstruction was exposed.

## 5.3 No Universal Memory Simulation

The game does not continuously evaluate the memories of every NPC to decide whether buildings exist.

Memory Solidity uses:

- site records;
- regional summaries;
- evidence flags;
- quest/event state;
- settlement upkeep;
- authored state transitions.

# 11. Necropolis Canon

## 11.1 Definition

**Necropolis** is a cross-biome **domain/state/polity family** in which death, embodiment, persistence, remains, memory, identity storage, undeath, continuation or cessation has been deliberately altered, engineered, ritualised, preserved or institutionalised.

It is not:

- a ninth biome;
- a separate realm;
- the universal destination of the dead;
- a morality tag;
- shorthand for “hostile undead area.”

## 11.2 Compatible Forms

A Necropolis domain may contain one or more of the following:

- undead or altered-body citizens;
- engineered longevity;
- preserved bodies;
- replacement bodies;
- identity-storage systems;
- artificial memory populations;
- tomb-city infrastructure;
- ritual continuation;
- stasis;
- failed immortality systems;
- difficult-to-end persistence states;
- legally controlled cessation systems;
- sovereign persistence institutions.

Not every Necropolis uses all of these.

## 11.3 Domain Inputs

A site becomes Necropolis-active only through explicit authored or generated conditions such as:

- existing Necropolis polity;
- persistence infrastructure;
- identity-storage architecture;
- engineered body-state systems;
- legal/ritual continuation systems;
- dungeon/site history;
- major event aftermath.

An ordinary cemetery does not automatically become a Necropolis domain.

## 11.4 Domain State

Useful bounded domain conditions may include:

- **Inactive / Historical**
- **Operational**
- **Maintained**
- **Strained**
- **Unstable**
- **Failed**
- **Reforming**
- **Decommissioning**
- **Transformed**

The state describes local systems, not whether inhabitants are good or evil.

# 12. Necropolis Embodiment and Persistence

Necropolis content may alter the relationship among:

- body;
- identity;
- memory;
- legal personhood;
- persistence;
- cessation.

However, FCC-03I requires explicit state ownership.

Examples:

### Preserved Body System
A body may remain stored or maintained while a person is elsewhere, inactive or using another supported state.

### Replacement Body System
A person may use an approved constructed or altered body through universal personhood/body systems.

### Identity Storage
A bounded identity record may support a local persistence technology.

It must not become a free duplicate of a full NPC/person unless the universal personhood architecture supports that state.

### Artificial Memory Population
A polity may contain people whose origin is reconstructed or artificial memory.

If universal personhood rules classify them as persons, their origin does not reduce their rights.

### Cessation Restriction
A local system may make ending a persistence state difficult.

That may be:

- political;
- technical;
- magical;
- legal;
- environmental.

It is not universal immortality.

# 13. Necropolis Instability

The canonical **Necropolis Instability** hazard occurs when local persistence/embodiment/identity-storage/cessation systems fail or conflict.

Possible causes include:

- neglected maintenance;
- incompatible body/identity state;
- damaged infrastructure;
- contested legal control;
- conflicting succession;
- resource shortage;
- deliberate sabotage;
- identity-record corruption;
- Void influence;
- authority/event consequences.

Possible symptoms include:

- unstable embodiment;
- failed transfers;
- duplicated recognition claims;
- inaccessible stored identities;
- maintenance crises;
- citizens unable to enter/leave supported states;
- structure malfunction;
- dangerous persistence loops;
- political emergency.

The system must not randomly duplicate or erase authoritative persons.

Where apparent duplication is part of the story, the underlying personhood/identity relationship must be explicitly represented.

# 18. Void Corruption Canon

## 18.1 Source Ownership

The **Void Between** owns the deeper cosmological source and ultimate truth of Void corruption.

FCC-03 owns:

- how corruption manifests in Ancestral content;
- local state changes;
- hazards;
- affected materials;
- affected creatures;
- affected structures;
- affected routes;
- restoration/containment options;
- realm-specific consequences.

## 18.2 Distinction Rule

Void corruption is external to normal Ancestral mechanics.

It is not equivalent to:

- grief;
- darkness;
- mourning;
- forgetting;
- Remnant Eater predation;
- Devouring Silence;
- Necropolis engineering;
- undeath;
- spirit existence;
- disputed history;
- boundary weakness.

A site can be dark, dangerous, undead, unstable and memory-eroded **without being Void-corrupted**.

# 19. Void Corruption State Model

Default corruption should be represented as state first.

Recommended broad states:

1. **Clean**
2. **Exposed**
3. **Contaminated**
4. **Corrupted**
5. **Severely Corrupted**
6. **Irreversibly Transformed** — only where a separate definition is justified.

These states do not have to be a universal numeric meter.

Different content types may use a smaller subset.

## 19.1 Exposed

Contact has occurred but authoritative content remains functionally normal.

Possible outputs:

- warning;
- minor contamination;
- increased cleansing need.

## 19.2 Contaminated

The content carries active corruption risk but largely remains itself.

Possible outputs:

- reduced performance;
- hazard emissions;
- unsafe handling;
- spread risk.

## 19.3 Corrupted

Core behaviour is significantly altered.

Possible outputs:

- hostile state changes;
- structure malfunction;
- distorted route logic;
- altered ecology;
- severe material instability.

## 19.4 Irreversible Transformation

A new definition is allowed only when the original identity no longer adequately represents:

- composition;
- anatomy;
- lifecycle;
- behaviour;
- recipe role;
- ecological role;
- save identity;
- visual/material identity.

This gate prevents `Corrupted X` duplication across every registry.

# 20. Material Corruption Interface

FCC-03C already establishes the material rule:

- early/reversible corruption = state;
- distinct transformed material only when permanent material identity genuinely changes.

Potentially eligible severe transformations include:

- Corrupted Memory Glass;
- Corrupted Soulwood;
- Warped / Corrupted Soul Silver.

FCC-03I does **not automatically lock all three as required final definitions**.

FCC-03J and FCC-12/13 must verify:

- distinct use;
- distinct processing;
- distinct visuals;
- distinct recovery behaviour;
- no duplicate state-only implementation.

Other Veil materials remain state-corrupted unless the same threshold is met.

# 21. Creature Corruption Interface

- aggression;
- movement;

- abilities;

# 24. Corruption Spread

Corruption spread must be bounded and deterministic.

Possible propagation channels include:

- direct Void breach;
- contaminated object;
- corrupted creature;
- corrupted water/terrain site;
- damaged route;
- corrupted structure;
- authored realm event.

The simulation may use:

- regional contamination records;
- adjacency;
- event propagation;
- site links;
- route graphs;
- periodic bounded checks.

It must not require continuous cell-by-cell corruption simulation across the entire world.

Spread may be:

- stopped;
- slowed;
- contained;
- redirected;
- cleansed;
- accepted as permanent transformed region where authored.

---

# 25. Cleansing

**Cleansing** removes or reduces active harmful state.

It is not the same as full restoration.

Cleansing methods may include:

- Grave Salt;
- compatible wards;
- specialist materials;
- Cleansing House services;
- settlement infrastructure;
- authority outcomes;
- ritual/technical procedures;
- environmental repair;
- cross-realm resources where later reconciled.

Cleansing may remove:

- contamination;
- possession/influence;
- active corruption;
- harmful residue.

It may leave behind:

- physical damage;
- lost infrastructure;
- political consequences;
- altered ecology;
- historical change.

Those require restoration or adaptation.

---

# 26. Stabilisation

**Stabilisation** prevents further decline or secures a dangerous state.

It may apply to:

- identity;
- route;
- boundary;
- Necropolis systems;
- damaged structures;
- corrupted sites;
- Memory Sea conditions.

Stabilisation is often an intermediate outcome.

A site may be **stable but not restored**.

Examples:

- a damaged archive sealed safely;
- a Necropolis persistence system stopped from failing;
- a route closed safely rather than reopened;
- a corrupted district contained behind wards.

---

# 27. Restoration

Restoration improves or replaces damaged function.

Valid restoration categories include:

- physical repair;
- ecological restoration;
- archive recovery;
- historical investigation;
- route reopening;
- identity stabilisation;
- boundary repair;
- political settlement;
- Necropolis reform;
- cleansing;
- settlement rebuilding.

The central rule is:

> **Restoration does not require returning the world to a pristine earlier state.**

A restored site may become:

- historically reconstructed;
- politically reformed;
- ecologically different but stable;
- converted to new civic use;
- deliberately preserved as ruin;
- decommissioned safely;
- shared between former claimants.

---

# 28. Irreversible Loss and New Stable States

The Veil supports persistent consequences.

Some outcomes may permanently change:

- settlement ownership;
- route alignment;
- archive access;
- ecological composition;
- Memory Sea shoreline;
- district architecture;
- authority control;
- claimant legitimacy;
- Necropolis law.

Permanent change is allowed when it is:

- deterministic;
- saved;
- signposted where severe;
- produced by valid player/event action;
- not equivalent to hidden deletion of essential save data.

A **new stable state** is fully canonical even when it differs from the site's historical form.

# 32. Cross-Realm Interface Rule

The Ancestral Veil may interact with other realms, but each realm retains ownership of its own systems.

The rule is:

> **FCC-03 defines Ancestral consequences and interfaces. It does not absorb another realm's mechanics into Ancestral canon.**

Cross-realm interaction may involve:

- materials;
- diplomacy;
- historical records;
- research;
- creatures/plants where ecologically valid;
- corruption;
- portal progression;
- Pocket Realm modules;
- event aftermath.

Normal physical entry remains through **Veilgate**.

No resonance, dream, root, memory, ship, procession, death event or authority permission creates a second standard Ancestral access method.

# 41. Cross-Realm Personhood and Identity Safety

A person entering the Veil remains governed by universal personhood.

- permanently erase their identity;
- force them to become a spirit upon death;
- store their complete consciousness in Memory Glass automatically.

Realm-local identity effects may disrupt presentation or recognition, but authoritative person identity remains stable.

# 45. Accessibility Contract

Identity, memory and corruption mechanics must remain understandable without relying on one sensory channel.

Required alternatives include:

- text/icon state;
- shape or pattern difference;
- map fallback;
- non-colour corruption cues;
- subtitle/text alternatives for audio suppression;
- reduced-distortion presentation options;
- clear indication of temporary versus permanent state.

Devouring Silence, Mourning Mist, Remnant Eater effects and corruption must not make the game inaccessible by design.

Reducing visual scrambling does not remove the underlying hazard.

# 46. State Validation Rules

A state transition is invalid if it:

- deletes authoritative identity data;
- creates an alternate normal Veil access method;
- converts ordinary dead entities into Veil population automatically;
- treats native danger as Void corruption without cause;
- creates a new material/creature/plant definition when state is sufficient;
- contradicts personhood;
- produces an unsourced historical truth;
- bypasses universal ownership;
- produces irreversible progression loss from untelegraphed procedural randomness.

Validators should flag such transitions before content certification.

# 50. FCC-03I Acceptance Gate

FCC-03I is ready for realm-local acceptance only when all of the following are true:

- Recognition, Memory Solidity, Identity Stability, Route State, Historical Reconstruction, Boundary Stability, Necropolis State and Void Corruption remain separate bounded axes;
- authoritative player/NPC/save identity cannot be permanently deleted by realm mechanics;
- recognition comes from explicit supported state rather than arbitrary language interpretation;
- memory state does not require simulation of every NPC's memories;
- historical reconstructions may remain disputed, incomplete or falsified;
- route state remains deterministic and saved;
- Necropolis is a cross-biome domain/state/polity family, not a ninth biome or universal afterlife destination;
- Necropolis instability cannot randomly duplicate or erase authoritative persons;
- engineered persistence is politically/morally open rather than automatically evil;
- Predatory Lineage Spirit uses bounded lineage/continuity state and remains separate from fixed authorities;
- Grave-Sea Procession uses valid route, migration and Memory Sea states without becoming a second portal system;
- native Veil degradation remains distinct from Void corruption;
- Void Between owns corruption origin while FCC-03 owns local consequences;
- corruption is state-first;
- irreversible transformed definitions require a genuine identity/lifecycle/material change;
- no automatic corrupted duplicate roster exists for materials, plants or creatures;
- cleansing, stabilisation and restoration are distinct concepts;
- restoration may produce a new stable future instead of restoring an exact past;
- all twelve event families have state interfaces;
- the five direct locked cross-realm relationships—Verdant Covenant, Somnolent Expanse, Void Between, Overworld and Pocket Realms—respect ownership boundaries;
- no cross-realm resonance creates an alternate normal Veil entry method;
- other realms remain universal-interface-only until their FCCs establish stronger dedicated links;
- save, multiplayer, performance and accessibility contracts preserve authoritative truth;
- FCC-03J has sufficient state information to perform final realm certification.

**FCC-03I Status:** **REALM-LOCAL LOCK CANDIDATE — pending owner review.**

Upon owner approval, change status to:

> **LOCKED FOR REALM-LOCAL FCC USE — pending final cross-realm reconciliation.**
