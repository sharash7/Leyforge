# 1. Purpose

FCC-03B defines the authoritative environmental canon for the **Ancestral Veil**.

It owns the realm-local definition of:

- the eight major biome families;
- their terrain and environmental identities;
- the way those biome families transition and overlap;
- bounded memory-, identity-, route- and reflection-state families;
- Memory Sea environmental behaviour;
- Processional Road environmental behaviour;
- Necropolis environmental overlays;
- the twelve canonical hazard families;
- environmental restoration and degradation states;
- deterministic generation constraints;
- low-end simulation scaling requirements;
- accessibility requirements for disorientation-oriented content;
- environmental suitability interfaces consumed by later FCC-03 documents.

FCC-03B does **not** redefine:

- universal death or respawn;
- portal runtime;
- exact material processing chains;
- exact flora biology;
- creature AI and creature-resource tables;
- settlement production systems;
- dungeon internal encounter scripts;
- authority behaviour;
- final block/item recipes;
- final visual palettes, texture rules or art direction.

Those are owned by FCC-03A or later FCC-03 documents and by universal Leyforge systems.

---

# 2. Environmental Canon Statement

The Ancestral Veil must read as a **complete inhabitable realm**, not a collection of disconnected ghost set-pieces.

Its environments are shaped by a combination of:

- ordinary terrain and geology;
- water and Memory Sea systems;
- spirit-active matter;
- remembered or reconstructed history;
- recognised places and relationships;
- active settlement maintenance;
- local identity stability;
- route infrastructure;
- political and cultural occupation;
- ecological processes;
- Necropolis engineering or persistence systems;
- native hazards;
- Void corruption where present.

Memory and identity may alter the **state** or **presentation** of selected environment content, but the realm is not generated by continuously simulating every memory held by every person.

The guiding rule is:

> **The Veil environment reacts to explicit, bounded and saved world state — not to an unbounded global memory simulation.**

# 3. Canonical Biome Roster

The Ancestral Veil contains exactly **eight major biome families** at the realm-content-foundation level:

1. **Echoing Hearthlands**
2. **Processional Roads**
3. **Memory Sea**
4. **Ossuary Orchards**
5. **Mourning Mists**
6. **Forgotten City Reflections**
7. **Predator Dark**
8. **Crownlands of the Remembered**

These are biome **families**, not single fixed presets.

Each family may contain:

- seed-derived regional variants;
- climate or terrain variants;
- cultural overlays;
- settlement states;
- abandoned or restored states;
- dungeon sites;
- authority territories;
- event states;
- local Necropolis overlays;
- native hazard states;
- Void-corrupted states where justified;
- rare authored landmarks.

A regional variant does not automatically become a new canonical biome ID. A separate biome definition is required only if its ecology, terrain logic, hazards or gameplay role becomes fundamentally different.

**Necropolis is not a ninth biome.** It is a cross-biome domain/state family defined in FCC-03A and further detailed later in FCC-03I.

# 4. Realm Topology

## 4.1 Regional Mosaic

The Ancestral Veil forms a connected large-scale mosaic of:

- inhabited reflective landscapes;
- long Processional route systems;
- inland waterways and Memory Sea coastlines;
- specialist orchard regions;
- mist provinces;
- incomplete or re-emerging city reflections;
- low-anchor dark regions;
- powerful sovereign territories.

The realm is not required to reproduce another realm's geography.

It may contain historical or cultural echoes of other places, but those are **Veil-native content expressions**, not continuously synchronised copies.

## 4.2 Scale

The Veil should support:

- long-distance exploration;
- settlement placement;
- roads and ferries;
- resource travel;
- biome-specific ecology;
- regional politics;
- dungeon distribution;
- authority territories;
- player-built infrastructure;
- persistent changes caused by restoration, conflict and settlement growth.

It should not feel like a sequence of isolated dungeon rooms connected by teleportation.

## 4.3 Verticality

Vertical terrain may include:

- low rolling Hearthland regions;
- raised Processional causeways;
- terraces and orchard ridges;
- flooded basins;
- sea cliffs;
- drowned city levels;
- elevated Crownland precincts;
- deep archive basements;
- root or ossuary cavities;
- dark ravines and anchor-depleted depressions.

Extreme non-Euclidean verticality belongs primarily to other realms, especially the Impossible Deep. Veil geometry may become uncanny through reflection, repetition, partial reconstruction or route logic without requiring unrestricted spatial impossibility.

# 5. Biome Transition Rules

Biome transitions should usually be **legible** through terrain, structures, ecology, route markers and hazard cues.

Examples include:

- **Echoing Hearthlands → Processional Roads:** stronger lantern lines, bridges, route markers, station architecture and recognised destination cues;
- **Processional Roads → Memory Sea:** deltas, ferries, reflective water, archive ports, causeways and shoreline infrastructure;
- **Echoing Hearthlands → Ossuary Orchards:** paler soils, specialist trees, custodian structures, bone-like mineral forms and orchard ecology;
- **Hearthlands / Orchards → Mourning Mists:** increasing fog, reduced sound clarity, unresolved-history markers and grief-linked environmental pressure;
- **stable inhabited regions → Forgotten City Reflections:** increasing architectural fragments, phantom foundations, partial streets and historical overlays;
- **weakly anchored regions → Predator Dark:** fewer route markers, weaker light, unstable labels, missing familiar cues and increased identity-predator pressure;
- **stable historical regions → Crownlands:** monumental architecture, stronger ceremonial law, sovereign markers, controlled routes and denser recognised identity state.

Abrupt transitions are allowed when justified by:

- authored boundaries;
- sovereign territory lines;
- broken route infrastructure;
- event states;
- Necropolis engineering;
- severe Void corruption;
- large historical fractures.

Abrupt transitions must still provide player-readable warning unless surprise is an intentional authored encounter feature.

# 6. Bounded Environmental Memory-State Model

Environmental memory state is a **regional/site state system**, not a global simulation of individual memories.

A biome, structure, route, district, archive, island or site may hold one or more explicit memory-related states.

## 6.1 Core Memory-State Families

Canonical environmental memory-state families are:

- **Memory-Stable** — the site's recognised identity and environmental form are strongly maintained;
- **Memory-Fading** — selected details, routes or structures are losing stability;
- **Memory-Eroded** — important environmental information is weak, fragmented or partially inaccessible;
- **Memory-Disputed** — multiple incompatible interpretations or claims affect the site;
- **Memory-Reconstructing** — recovered evidence is actively restoring or changing environmental presentation;
- **Memory-Restored** — a supported stable reconstruction has been achieved;
- **Memory-Suppressed** — information or environmental expression has been deliberately hidden or constrained;
- **Memory-Falsified** — a supported game state marks some environmental reconstruction as intentionally false or manipulated.

Not every site uses every state.

## 6.2 State Inputs

Memory-state changes may be driven by explicit inputs such as:

- recovered records;
- archive restoration;
- settlement maintenance;
- recognised names;
- repaired route anchors;
- authority outcomes;
- historical investigation;
- event resolution;
- political control;
- ritual maintenance;
- environmental damage;
- abandonment;
- Void corruption;
- cleansing or restoration projects.

## 6.3 State Consequences

Memory state may affect:

- route availability;
- structure solidity;
- map presentation;
- environmental detail;
- settlement confidence or services;
- dungeon access;
- historical clues;
- creature composition;
- hazard intensity;
- resource accessibility;
- faction claims;
- environmental ambience.

Underlying save-safe stable IDs and authoritative records remain intact regardless of presentation state.

# 7. Identity-Stability Environmental Model

Identity stability is separate from memory stability, although the two may interact.

Canonical site/region identity states include:

- **Identity-Anchored**
- **Identity-Stable**
- **Identity-Strained**
- **Identity-Unstable**
- **Identity-Obscured**
- **Identity-Restabilising**

These states may influence:

- recognition-dependent route use;
- local structure permissions;
- map labels;
- NPC recognition cues;
- certain creature behaviours;
- selected dungeon mechanics;
- hazard resistance;
- local claims or ceremonial access.

Identity instability must never:

- erase the player profile;
- destroy NPC stable IDs;
- remove save ownership;
- silently delete quests;
- permanently corrupt inventory or progression;
- make required accessibility information impossible to recover.

# 8. Route-State Environmental Model

Processional Roads and related ferries use explicit route-state families.

Canonical route states include:

- **Open**
- **Restricted**
- **Unrecognised**
- **Damaged**
- **Obscured**
- **Lost**
- **Contested**
- **Restoring**
- **Restored**
- **Hazardous**

A route state may depend on:

- physical infrastructure;
- recognised destination state;
- route-anchor status;
- faction or sovereign permissions;
- environmental hazards;
- historical knowledge;
- local settlement operation;
- authority outcomes.

Route state is deterministic from seed + authoritative saved state + explicit current conditions.

It is not generated by freeform semantic interpretation of arbitrary player text.

# 9. Canonical Environmental Hazard Roster

The Ancestral Veil has **twelve canonical hazard families**.

| # | Hazard Family | Canonical Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Memory Erosion** | Weakens selected environmental memory state and route/interaction reliability without deleting authoritative memory or save data. |
| 2 | **Identity Instability** | Temporarily disrupts recognition-dependent systems while preserving stable identities underneath. |
| 3 | **Route Loss** | Makes Processional or relationship-based routes unavailable, hidden or unreliable until conditions are restored. |
| 4 | **Mourning Mist** | Reduces visibility, sound clarity and navigation confidence in grief-linked regions. |
| 5 | **Devouring Silence** | Suppresses selected sound, names, markers and relationship cues inside strongly authored dangerous zones. |
| 6 | **Predator Dark Exposure** | Raises pressure from identity predators and weakens environmental anchor cues. |
| 7 | **Hostile Possession / Influence** | Bounded spirit influence using explicit status, resistance and recovery mechanics. |
| 8 | **Memory Sea Storm** | Produces navigation, echo, creature-migration and shoreline/island-access hazards. |
| 9 | **False / Conflicting Historical Reconstruction** | Creates investigation and navigation risk where environmental history cannot be assumed accurate. |
| 10 | **Necropolis Instability** | Failure, conflict or malfunction in local engineered persistence, embodiment, identity-storage or cessation systems. |
| 11 | **Veil Boundary Instability** | Local instability between physical and spirit-active states, especially around damaged infrastructure or thin boundaries. |
| 12 | **Void Corruption** | External corruption sourced from the Void Between; mechanically and visually distinct from native Veil hazards. |

These are hazard **families**, so individual sites may implement them differently without creating new top-level hazard categories.

# 10. Hazard Interaction Rules

## 10.1 Native Hazards Are Not Void Corruption

The following may occur naturally without Void involvement:

- Mourning Mist;
- Predator Dark;
- Memory Erosion;
- Route Loss;
- Devouring Silence;
- disputed historical reconstruction;
- local Necropolis instability;
- boundary weakness.

Void corruption may exploit or worsen these states, but it remains a separate cause and state family.

## 10.2 Hazards Must Be Recoverable Where Progression Requires It

Critical progression routes may be:

- stabilised;
- repaired;
- investigated;
- bypassed;
- politically reopened;
- cleansed;
- temporarily protected;
- navigated with specialist equipment.

The realm may contain optional permanently dangerous sites, but ordinary progression cannot depend on unrecoverable random failure.

## 10.3 No Hidden Permanent Data Destruction

No environmental hazard may permanently destroy essential player-owned identity, save, map, quest or progression data as a surprise effect.

Apparent loss may be represented through temporary UI or recognition disruption while authoritative data remains preserved.

# 11. Echoing Hearthlands

## 11.1 Identity

The **Echoing Hearthlands** are warm, inhabited regions shaped by maintained remembrance, community, domestic identity, memorial care and stable social anchors.

They establish that the Ancestral Veil is capable of ordinary life and settlement rather than existing only as a hostile spectral frontier.

They may resemble:

- villages;
- courtyards;
- gardens;
- household districts;
- commons;
- small farms;
- local shrines;
- archives;
- bridges;
- community halls.

They are **not** automatic copies of homes belonging to dead NPCs elsewhere.

## 11.2 Terrain Character

Common environmental forms include:

- gently rolling **Veil Soil**;
- exposed **Memory Stone** foundations;
- low ridges;
- warm pools and streams;
- stable paths;
- cultivated plots;
- low mist pockets;
- small reflective ponds;
- planted memorial gardens;
- inhabited stone/wood/clay structures.

## 11.3 Memory Behaviour

Selected maintained structures may strengthen, clarify or fade according to explicit site-maintenance and memory-state inputs.

Examples include:

- archive restoration improving a district's detail;
- abandoned community structures becoming less complete;
- recognised civic names restoring signage or route cues;
- settlement upkeep moving a site from Memory-Fading to Memory-Stable.

## 11.4 Dominant Hazards

Typical hazards are low to moderate and may include:

- Memory Erosion in abandoned areas;
- Mourning Mist after major events;
- boundary instability around damaged sites;
- historical reconstruction disputes.

## 11.5 Later-Document Suitability

This biome is especially suitable for:

- Hearth Settlements;
- Memorial Villages;
- Community Archives;
- Namekeeper Cranes;
- Memory Moths;
- Memorybacks;
- Mourning Lily;
- Lantern Moss;
- Namebridge Sanctuary variants;
- local Memory Wells;
- Quiet Markets.

The exact asset definitions belong to FCC-03D through FCC-03H.

# 13. Memory Sea

## 13.1 Identity

The **Memory Sea** is a vast oceanic biome of reflective water, islands, drowned structures, archive ports, echo currents and organisms adapted to ambient memory traces.

The sea may contain remembered voices, emotions, images or environmental impressions as bounded phenomena.

It is **not a literal ocean of harvested souls**.

## 13.2 Water and Island Behaviour

The Memory Sea uses the universal fluid and water simulation interfaces wherever possible, with realm-specific state layered above them.

Regional content may include:

- stable islands;
- temporary-access islands whose route state changes;
- drowned civic structures;
- shoals;
- reflective flats;
- archive reefs;
- ferry channels;
- sheltered harbours;
- storm corridors.

Persistent geographical changes must be deterministic and saved.

## 13.3 Memory Sea Storms

Storm events may alter:

- visibility;
- local route safety;
- ferry access;
- echo clarity;
- creature migration;
- salvage conditions;
- temporary shoreline accessibility;
- selected island route states.

A storm cannot randomly delete canonical history or permanently erase established world state.

## 13.4 Dominant Hazards

- Memory Sea Storm;
- Route Loss;
- Mourning Mist over water;
- boundary instability;
- false historical reconstruction around drowned sites;
- local Void corruption where breaches exist.

## 13.5 Later-Document Suitability

Especially suitable for:

- Echo Rays;
- Mourning Eels;
- Memory Koi;
- Echo Lotus;
- Memory Reed;
- Archive Ports;
- Floating Memory Settlements;
- Island Communities;
- Drowned Remembrance Halls;
- Echo Pearl ecology.

# 14. Ossuary Orchards

## 14.1 Identity

The **Ossuary Orchards** are pale specialist orchard regions dominated by Soulwood/Ossuary trees and the ecology required to maintain them.

The biome combines living cultivation, memorial practice, mineral interaction and spirit-active biology.

## 14.2 Corpse-Harvesting Supersession

Older implications that Ossuary trees must grow from imported dead bodies are superseded.

Canonical orchard growth is a realm-native ecological process that may depend on:

- local soils;
- specialist mineral conditions;
- orchard stewardship;
- memorial practices;
- spirit-active nutrients;
- recognised cultivation methods.

It does not require routine corpse harvesting.

- exposed Memory Stone;
- specialist root systems;
- Spirit Clay pockets;
- small pools;
- custodian paths;

## 14.4 Dominant Hazards

- Necropolis Instability in engineered sites;

- Memory Erosion in abandoned orchards;

# 15. Mourning Mists

## 15.1 Identity

The **Mourning Mists** are fog provinces shaped by unresolved mass grief, disaster, exile, betrayal, collective loss or damaged historical understanding.

The biome's purpose is not simply to be sad or spooky. It externalises unresolved history into environmental pressure that players can investigate, endure and sometimes change.

## 15.2 No Suffering Economy

Grief is **not** a farmable resource produced by causing suffering.

No canonical progression loop should incentivise creating misery simply to harvest a grief material.

## 15.3 Terrain Character

Common forms include:

- low wet plains;
- shallow pools;
- partially obscured roads;
- ruined memorial sites;
- broken archive stones;
- low hills;
- drowned paths;
- reed zones;
- fog-heavy settlements;
- investigation sites.

## 15.4 Environmental Behaviour

Mourning state may affect:

- visibility;
- sound propagation;
- route confidence;
- creature behaviour;
- settlement safety;
- historical clue readability;
- access to selected sites;
- restoration-project requirements.

Resolving the underlying historical or political cause may change the biome's state.

## 15.5 Dominant Hazards

- Mourning Mist;
- Memory Erosion;
- Route Loss;
- conflicting historical reconstruction;
- hostile influence;
- occasional Remnant Eater pressure;
- Void corruption only where separately present.

## 15.6 Later-Document Suitability

Especially suitable for:

- Cleansing Outposts;
- Refuge Settlements;
- Investigation Camps;
- Mist Striders;
- Mourning Eels in wet areas;
- Mist Fern;
- Mourning Lily;
- investigation events;
- selected Memory Eater activity.

# 17. Predator Dark

## 17.1 Identity

The **Predator Dark** is a family of regions where recognised identity, route continuity and stable remembrance are weak enough for identity predators and absence phenomena to dominate.

Predator Dark is **not automatically Void-corrupted**.

## 17.2 Terrain Character

Terrain may be physically ordinary but environmentally difficult to read.

Common features include:

- dim valleys;
- reduced landmark density;
- broken roads;
- dark Memory Stone outcrops;
- missing or obscured signs;
- sparse settlement remnants;
- low-light anchor refuges;
- silence pockets;
- fractured route infrastructure.

## 17.3 Environmental Language

The biome may reduce or distort:

- route markers;
- local labels;
- familiar silhouettes;
- stable lighting;
- audible cues;
- social recognition indicators;
- non-critical map presentation.

These are **presentation and bounded-state effects**. Authoritative data remains intact.

## 17.4 Accessibility Requirement

Players using reduced-disorientation, accessibility or high-readability settings must retain reliable alternatives through:

- icons;
- text;
- outlines;
- route pips;
- objective markers;
- audio alternatives;
- haptic or UI cues where supported.

The biome's challenge may remain, but accessibility settings must not be defeated merely to preserve atmosphere.

## 17.5 Dominant Hazards

- Predator Dark Exposure;
- Identity Instability;
- Devouring Silence;
- Route Loss;
- Memory Erosion;
- hostile influence;
- rare Void corruption as a separately telegraphed escalation.

## 17.6 Later-Document Suitability

Especially suitable for:

- Remnant Eaters;
- Lantern Wisps as navigation contrast;
- Anchor Fortresses;
- Lantern Refuges;
- Route-Survival Outposts;
- Devouring Silence Zones;
- Memory Eater territories;
- rescue and stabilisation events.

# 19. Necropolis Environmental Overlay

Necropolis is a cross-biome **domain/state family**.

It may appear in compatible locations such as:

- an engineered-persistence district within Forgotten City Reflections;
- an ossuary polity in the Orchards;
- a tomb-route city along Processional Roads;
- a sovereign persistence court in the Crownlands;
- an unstable artificial identity-storage facility in Predator Dark;
- a preserved-body enclave in the Hearthlands.

A Necropolis overlay may affect:

- local embodiment rules;
- structure operation;
- rights and law;
- labour organisation;
- persistence technology;
- cessation or continuation states of realm-native entities;
- identity-storage infrastructure;
- hazard intensity;
- faction politics;
- architecture and material use.

It does **not**:

- route ordinary dead characters into the Veil;
- take ownership of universal respawn;
- create generic soul resources;
- make the area inherently evil;
- automatically make all inhabitants undead.

# 21. Terrain Foundations and Distribution Boundary

FCC-03B establishes environmental distribution roles but does not define full material processing.

The major terrain/material foundations consumed from FCC-03C include:

- **Memory Stone** — common structural geology;
- **Veil Soil** — native terrain substrate;
- **Spirit Clay** — realm-native clay and ceramic foundation;
- **Memory Glass** — rarer translucent recording/optical mineral;
- **Soulwood** — restricted specialist Ossuary-tree material;
- the remaining approved primary/specialist materials where environmental deposits or ecology require them.

Biome generation may expose these according to suitability rules defined here and exact provenance rules in FCC-03C/D/E.

FCC-03B does not independently create additional major stone, soil, metal or timber families.

# 22. Water, Mist and Boundary Presentation

The realm should consume universal water, weather and fluid systems where possible rather than creating redundant physics stacks.

Realm-specific behaviour is layered through:

- Memory Sea state;
- echo presentation;
- mist density/state;
- spirit-active visibility;
- route overlays;
- boundary instability;
- event-driven environmental modifiers.

The legacy idea that Veil environments require completely separate physical laws is not adopted globally.

- biome-region placement;
- Memory Sea coastline and island foundations;
- Processional route graph candidates;
- settlement suitability;
- dungeon suitability;
- resource suitability;
- Veilgate arrival suitability;
- baseline hazard pressure;
- historical-reflection templates;
- Necropolis suitability;
- authority-territory candidates;
- large structure-site candidates.

Persistent simulation may alter the generated baseline after world creation, but saved state becomes authoritative.

The realm does not require a fixed handcrafted global map.

## 23.1 Generation Prohibitions

Generation must not:

- depend on live records of every death in other realms;
- create one spirit per deceased NPC;
- mirror every Overworld settlement;
- generate unbounded per-character memories;
- change established world geography purely because a distant unrelated NPC changed opinion;
- silently regenerate resolved historical districts after save/load.

---

# 24. World-State Persistence

Persistent environmental state should include only information needed to preserve meaningful differences from deterministic baseline generation.

Examples include:

- restored/damaged districts;
- route state changes;
- active settlement effects;
- resolved event states;
- reconstructed historical sites;
- authority-territory changes;
- Necropolis activation/deactivation;
- major hazard-state changes;
- player construction;
- Void corruption and cleansing states.

Derived ambient details may be regenerated deterministically where they do not affect authoritative gameplay.

---

# 25. Simulation Scaling and Low-End Profiles

The Ancestral Veil must remain compatible with Leyforge's broader scalability goals.

Lower simulation profiles may reduce:

- off-screen route-state update frequency;
- number of active reflection overlays;
- ambient spirit density;
- Memory Sea surface complexity;
- mist particles;
- reconstruction visual layers;
- distant settlement simulation detail;
- background identity propagation checks;
- distant creature ecology detail;
- decorative reflected architecture;
- non-critical environmental animation.

Lower profiles must preserve:

- all eight biome identities;
- progression-critical route state;
- Memory Sea gameplay identity;
- native hazard vs Void-corruption distinction;
- required settlements;
- required dungeons;
- authority outcomes;
- Veilgate safety;
- saved historical outcomes;
- player construction;
- canonical material/resource provenance.

The implementation may simplify **how often** a state is evaluated, not **whether** the canonical state exists.

---

# 26. Accessibility and Readability

The Veil intentionally uses themes of obscured identity, silence, fog and unreliable presentation. Those mechanics must remain accessible.

Accessibility settings may reduce, replace or supplement:

- heavy fog;
- label scrambling;
- audio suppression;
- visual identity distortion;
- flashing Memory Glass effects;
- rapid reconstruction transitions;
- dense transparency layers;
- disorienting route effects;
- camera or screen-space distortion.

Critical gameplay state must be available through redundant cues such as:

- text;
- icons;
- route symbols;
- outlines;
- objective summaries;
- audio;
- haptics where supported;
- environmental markers;
- accessibility overlays.

Accessibility is not allowed to remove the *mechanical consequence* of a hazard by default, but it may replace the sensory technique used to communicate that consequence.

This provides a shared deterministic basis for flora, creature, structure, settlement and dungeon placement.

# 29. Cross-Realm Environmental Interfaces

## 29.1 Verdant Covenant

Moonroot and Dreaming Root Depth content may have limited Ancestral resonance where explicitly authored.

That does not cause automatic biome blending or create a second dimensional access method.

## 29.2 Somnolent Expanse

Memory and dream may interact at specific sites, but FCC-03B does not own dream-network geography or dream-state simulation.

## 29.3 Void Between

Void corruption can alter Ancestral terrain, routes, materials and identity states.

FCC-03B owns **local environmental consequences**. The Void Between owns the deeper cosmological corruption source.

## 29.4 Overworld

Veil reflections may reference Overworld history without mirroring every Overworld location or synchronising every change.

## 29.5 Pocket Realms

Future constructed Pocket Realms may consume Veil-derived environmental modules related to memory, archives, identity anchoring or reflection. Pocket Realm construction rules remain outside FCC-03B.

- all eight major biome families are defined;
- the realm is a connected environmental world rather than isolated ghost set-pieces;

- low-end scaling preserves canonical progression and environmental identity;
- accessibility may replace disorientation presentation without deleting required game-state readability;
- later FCC-03 documents have explicit environmental suitability interfaces;
- no new major material, creature, settlement or dungeon roster has been silently invented here.
