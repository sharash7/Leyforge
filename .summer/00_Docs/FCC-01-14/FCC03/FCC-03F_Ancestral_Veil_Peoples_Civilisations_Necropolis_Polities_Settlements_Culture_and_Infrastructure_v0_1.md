# 1. Purpose

FCC-03F defines the authoritative **civilisation and settlement canon** of the Ancestral Veil.

It owns:

- the kinds of peoples and communities inhabiting the realm;
- civilisation and cultural archetypes;
- separation between ancestry, culture, faction and polity;
- Necropolis polities and engineered persistence societies;
- mixed physical, semi-corporeal and spirit-active settlements;
- settlement archetypes across all eight biomes;
- governance and civic institutions;
- household and housing adaptation;
- work, agriculture, industry and trade;
- cultural relationships to memory, identity, archives and routes;
- diplomacy and cross-realm enclaves;
- population movement, refugees, visitors and diaspora;
- route, ferry, market, archive and stabilisation infrastructure;
- cultural use of materials, flora and creatures;
- settlement responses to hazards, corruption and restoration.

FCC-03F does **not** own:

- exact named building definitions and structure blueprints, which belong to FCC-03G;
- exact dungeon/authority content, which belongs to FCC-03H;
- full corruption mechanics, which belong to FCC-03I;
- universal NPC scheduling, jobs, households, economy, combat or governance frameworks;
- exact prices, taxes, tariffs or market equations;
- the complete ancestry roster of Leyforge;
- final art palettes or architectural texture rules.

The aim is to make the Ancestral Veil a realm with **real societies**, not merely a supernatural adventure zone.

# 2. Civilisation Canon Statement

The Ancestral Veil supports old and young communities, native spirit-active populations, physical biological populations, mixed settlements, visitor enclaves, political courts, archivist societies, route-maintenance organisations, Memory Sea communities, Necropolis polities, reflection-based communities, displaced populations, sovereign territories, commercial settlements and historical institutions.

> **The Veil contains peoples with histories, economies, institutions and disagreements. Death-themed appearance, incorporeality, undeath or memorial identity does not determine morality, hostility, citizenship or personhood.**

No settlement is automatically evil because it contains undead residents, preserves bodies, uses memory technology, stores identities, maintains ancestor-like manifestations, studies death, practices engineered longevity or inhabits a Necropolis domain.

Likewise, no spirit court or archivist society is automatically benevolent.

---

# 3. Ancestry, Culture, Faction and Polity Separation

FCC-03F adopts the global separation rule:

- **Ancestry** defines biological, constructed or metaphysical lineage where relevant.
- **Culture** defines learned social practices, values, architecture, food, craft, law and institutions.
- **Faction** defines organised interests and allegiance.
- **Polity** defines governing territory or political community.
- **Settlement** defines a populated place.
- **Profession/order** defines vocational or institutional role.
- **Realm origin** defines provenance, not morality or culture.

A Human, Goblin, Dwarf, Elf, Awakened Construct, Veilborn or other approved ancestry may participate in more than one Ancestral culture.

A settlement may be mono-cultural, culturally mixed, multi-ancestry, majority physical, majority spirit-active, mixed-state, visitor-founded, diaspora-founded or politically divided.

**Veilborn**, where the global ancestry registry admits them, are an approved Ancestral-associated ancestry foundation but are not synonymous with all Ancestral inhabitants.

# 4. Legacy Civilisation Reconciliation

Earlier Atlas material described broad groups such as:

- ancestor councils;
- psychopomp orders;
- lost communities;
- memory archivists;
- divine custodians;
- spirit predators;
- living visitors;
- factions disputing treatment of the dead.

FCC-03F preserves these as **historical inspiration and possible local expressions**, not as the final mandatory faction roster.

Source-era assumptions are reconciled as follows:

- psychopomp functions become route-guidance, ferryman, rescue or threshold professions rather than universal transport of the dead;
- ancestor councils may be native ancestral/memory polities, memorial institutions or historically reconstructed communities rather than databases of dead NPCs;
- lost communities may be native, displaced, reconstructed, abandoned or historical-reflection populations;
- divine custodians may appear through specific cross-realm interfaces but are not mandatory realm-wide government;
- spirit predators belong to ecology unless a particular population qualifies as persons;
- living visitors remain a broad visitor/diaspora category.

# 6. Memory Archivists

**Memory Archivists** are communities and institutions focused on records, historical reconstruction, custodianship, contested evidence, preserved knowledge, Memory Glass use, oral/material history, access control and archive restoration.

They may be public civic institutions, independent orders, hereditary houses, universities, court archivists or travelling record-keepers.

Archivists do not automatically possess objective truth. Their records may be accurate, incomplete, biased, censored, damaged, competing, politically manipulated or genuinely mistaken.

Typical infrastructure may include libraries, secure archives, Memory Glass installations, catalogue stations, workshops, visitor reading spaces, restricted collections, map rooms and record-restoration facilities.

Exact structures belong to FCC-03G.

---

# 7. Processional Orders

**Processional Orders** maintain and regulate movement through the realm.

Their work may include:

- Processional Road maintenance;
- route surveying;
- route rescue;
- ferries;
- waystations;
- bridge stewardship;
- convoy protection;
- traveller registration;
- route-law enforcement;
- stabilisation;
- navigation training.

They do **not** universally transport dead souls.

Orders may be civic, commercial, religious, hereditary, sovereign, mercenary, charitable, court-appointed or independent.

They may disagree over tolls, route neutrality, refugee passage, foreign traders, authority jurisdiction and portal-side regulation.

They may work with Procession Hounds, Memorybacks, Namekeeper Cranes and Lantern Wisps without owning those creature systems.

---

# 8. Hearthland Communities

**Hearthland Communities** are settled populations of Echoing Hearthlands and other stable regions.

They provide the clearest proof that the Veil supports ordinary civilisation.

They may maintain homes, farms, gardens, workshops, markets, schools, archives, public halls, shrines, routes and defensive systems.

Their cultures may value continuity, names, neighbourhood history, mutual aid, preserved homes, local memory and community ritual, but they remain ordinary political and economic societies.

A Hearthland settlement may contain physical residents, spirit-active residents, mixed households, visitors, migrants, descendants, reconstructed beings or memory-born persons where universal personhood rules admit them.

No population requires an external death feed.

---

# 9. Ossuary Custodians

**Ossuary Custodians** are cultures, orders and communities specialising in stewardship of Ossuary Orchards, Soulwood, Gravebell, orchard wildlife, soil health, memorial landscapes, sustainable harvest and ecological restoration.

They may combine agriculture, forestry, craft, medicine, ritual and ecological law.

Custodians may be village cooperatives, temple-like orders, noble estates, public forestry services, clan institutions, commercial orchard organisations or conservation factions.

They may regulate Soulwood felling, deadwood collection, wildlife management, Grave Salt use, culturally significant trees, propagation stock and export.

These are local law/economy rules, not a universal “ask the forest” mechanic.

---

# 10. Necropolis Polities

**Necropolis Polities** are political communities associated with engineered, altered, preserved or institutionalised relationships to death, persistence, body-state, memory or identity.

Necropolis is **not a ninth biome**. It is a cross-biome domain/polity/state family.

Necropolis societies may exist in Hearthlands, Processional regions, Forgotten City Reflections, Crownlands, Ossuary regions, Memory Sea islands and other compatible sites.

Possible forms include:

- undead citizens;
- preserved bodies;
- body-replacement systems;
- artificial memory populations;
- tomb cities;
- identity-storage institutions;
- long-duration stasis;
- ritualised longevity systems;
- failed immortality infrastructure;
- “cannot end” anomalies;
- citizens using multiple body states.

These possibilities are not all mandatory.

If a Necropolis resident is a person under universal rules, they remain a person regardless of body decay, incorporeality, magical persistence, constructed body, memory-based existence, age or origin.

Necropolis citizens cannot be treated as loot, machines or corpse resources merely because of their state.

Political conflicts may concern succession, citizenship, ownership of preserved bodies, continuity of identity, resource access, maintenance burden, right to end persistence, right to continue persistence, historical legitimacy and relations with outsiders.

The game must support outcomes beyond extermination.

---

# 11. Spirit Courts

**Spirit Courts** are political, judicial and social institutions where identity, testimony, status, recognition, memory and local law carry unusual weight.

They may resemble courts, councils, aristocracies, civic tribunals, sovereign households, legal colleges or diplomatic assemblies.

Court decisions must rely on explicit game-state evidence such as registered identity, recognised witnesses, recorded agreements, evidence objects, reputation, authority, territory and legal claim.

They may not depend on unrestricted natural-language interpretation.

Spirit Courts are especially suitable for Crownlands but may exist elsewhere. Not every Crownland settlement is a Spirit Court and not every Spirit Court is monarchic.

---

# 12. Memory Sea Communities

**Memory Sea Communities** are populations adapted to aquatic and coastal Veil life.

They may live in island settlements, archive ports, floating structures, sheltered coves, canal cities, stilted districts or water-garden communities.

Common activities may include fishing, aquaculture, Echo Pearl stewardship, ferrying, navigation, waterborne trade, Memory Glass recovery, wetland cultivation and archive salvage.

They consume universal maritime/vessel systems where possible rather than creating a separate Veil-only ship engine.

Veil-specific adaptations may include route anchoring, mist navigation, Memory Sea hazards, spirit-compatible materials and Echo Pearl calibration.

---

# 13. Forgotten-City Claimants

**Forgotten-City Claimants** are communities, factions and institutions claiming rights over reappearing or reconstructed historic districts.

They may include descendants, cultural successors, local residents, archivists, political heirs, new civic groups, foreign occupiers, conservationists or researchers.

Historical reflection becoming more solid does not automatically decide ownership.

Claims may compete through documentary evidence, continuity of residence, cultural ties, repair work, legal recognition, negotiation, force or authority decree.

The player may reveal historical truth without the game automatically declaring one claimant morally correct.

---

# 14. Living / Physical Visitor Enclaves

These are settlements founded or maintained by physical visitors, migrants, traders, researchers, diplomats, adventurers or cross-realm communities.

Physical enclaves may require explicit support for food, water, ordinary beds, physical sanitation, storage, medicine, material repair, imported goods and portal logistics.

This does not imply native spirit-active populations have no needs; their needs may simply differ.

Visitor enclaves may become permanent mixed settlements, embassy districts, trade stations, research colonies, restoration bases or temporary camps.

They remain subject to local ownership and law.

---

# 15. Predator-Dark Survival Communities

These populations survive near or within weak-identity and Predator Dark environments.

They specialise in anchor redundancy, safe naming/recognition systems, route discipline, low-profile settlement layouts, rescue, identity stabilisation, defensive lighting/ward systems and controlled evacuation.

Their cultures need not be grim or militaristic. They may be mutual-aid refuges, frontier towns, fortified research communities, caravan stations, rescue orders or exile settlements.

Their expertise is a survival adaptation, not an ancestry.

# 17. Universal Settlement Needs

Ancestral settlements consume universal settlement-development systems.

Where the universal settlement system uses Leyforge's seven canonical needs, Veil settlements map to:

1. **Housing**
2. **Provisions**
3. **Health**
4. **Work**
5. **Safety**
6. **Infrastructure**
7. **Morale**

Realm adaptation may change how a need is satisfied.

Housing may support physical, spirit-active or mixed occupants. Provisions may differ between biological and non-biological populations. Health may include identity stabilisation in addition to physical medicine. Safety may include route, memory and possession hazards. Infrastructure may include anchors, ferries and Veil-Lanterns. Morale may include community continuity, cultural sites and historical security.

No realm adaptation may silently delete a universal settlement need without a global-system decision.

---

# 18. Housing and Occupancy

Housing may be designed for physical, primarily incorporeal, semi-corporeal, mixed, visitor or protected occupants.

Different occupancy may require collision-compatible rooms, privacy boundaries, anchor points, physical beds, rest spaces, climate control, Veil Silk screens, Memory Glass partitions and accessible routes.

“Spirit dwelling” is a building adaptation, not proof that spirits need no housing or civic support.

Households remain governed by universal NPC/household systems where applicable.

---

# 19. Governance and Citizenship

No single government model defines the realm.

Possible governance includes councils, courts, monarchies, elected bodies, guild/order government, cooperative stewardship, archive boards, route authorities, Necropolis constitutional systems and sovereign individual authorities.

Citizenship and legal recognition may depend on ordinary political rules plus bounded Veil-specific records such as settlement citizenship, court recognition, route registration, property claims, archive identity records, diplomatic status and visitor permits.

The system must distinguish:

- being remembered;
- being legally recognised;
- being physically present;
- being a citizen;
- being an owner.

These are not interchangeable.

---

# 20. Economy and Trade

Ancestral settlements participate in the universal economy.

They may trade food, Soulwood goods, Spirit Clay ceramics, Memory Stone masonry, Memory Glass products, Oathbone fittings, Veil Silk textiles, Grave Salt, Echo Pearls, Soul Silver products, medicines, archival services, maps, transport, cultural goods and imported mundane resources.

There is **no generic soul economy**.

Spirits, identities, memories of persons and undead citizens are not ordinary commodities.

Knowledge and records may be traded or licensed where ownership and permission allow, which is distinct from selling persons or protected identities.

FCC-03F does not create a universal Veil currency. Settlements may use universal currencies, local coinage, barter, credit, contracts, service exchange, authority tokens, tolls or market permits through the economy system.

# 24. Signature Structure Interface

Existing source material established six signature Ancestral structure concepts:

1. **Namebridge Sanctuary**
2. **Hall of Remembered Doors**
3. **Ancestral Embassy**
4. **Memory Well**
5. **Quiet Market**
6. **Veil-Lantern Network**

FCC-03F recognises their **civilisation roles**, while FCC-03G owns their exact structure definitions.

Broad roles:

- Namebridge Sanctuary → safe arrival, recognition and stabilisation;
- Hall of Remembered Doors → identity/history/route civic institution;
- Ancestral Embassy → diplomacy;
- Memory Well → controlled civic memory/record interaction;
- Quiet Market → mixed-state trade;
- Veil-Lantern Network → public route and safety infrastructure.

Older functional concepts such as arrival shrine, memory archive, spirit dwelling, cleansing house and oath guardian may survive as generic structure families where FCC-03G reconciles them.

The old **death-recovery infrastructure** label is superseded by:

> **identity stabilisation, memory recovery, cleansing, route rescue and safe-return infrastructure**

# 25. Processional Roads and Settlement Layout

Earlier building-pack material established that Ancestral settlement layouts can respond to memory and recognised names rather than physical distance alone.

FCC-03F retains this only in bounded form.
