# 1. Purpose

FCC-03A defines the authoritative high-level identity, metaphysical rule families, progression model, portal-access contract, canon boundaries, system ownership, and implementation-facing constraints for the **Ancestral Veil**.

The Ancestral Veil is a **complete persistent realm** with its own:

- geography and eight major biome families;
- geology, terrain and physical resources;
- spirit-active materials;
- plants, fungi and specialist Soulwood/Ossuary ecology;
- physical, semi-corporeal and incorporeal native creatures;
- cultures, settlements, courts, orders and Necropolis polities;
- roads, ferries, route infrastructure and Memory Sea travel;
- political and historical disputes;
- dungeons, guardians and realm authorities;
- hazards, events and long-term realm states;
- ordinary settlement, building, crafting and trade activity;
- cross-realm relationships and eventual full-realm progression.

FCC-03A does **not** hold every individual asset definition. Later FCC-03 documents own the detailed canon for biomes, materials, flora, creatures, civilisations, structures, blocks, items, recipes, dungeons, authorities, events, corruption states and art handoff.

This document establishes the rules those later documents must obey.

---

# 2. Canon Statement

The **Ancestral Veil** is a major persistent realm in which **memory, recognised identity, history, remembrance, reflection, route relationships and spirit-active matter can have physical consequences**.

Its central fantasy is:

> **A realm where memory and identity can shape places, routes, cultures and inhabitants, while the realm remains a self-contained world rather than an automated afterlife simulation.**

The Veil is not merely gloomy, spectral or death-themed scenery. It must support meaningful exploration, settlement, diplomacy, resource use, ecology, infrastructure, political conflict, historical reconstruction and long-term change.

The realm may contain:

- native spirits and semi-corporeal beings;
- fully physical wildlife and biological ecology;
- memory-born and reflection-born entities;
- long-lived communities and civilisations;
- reconstructed historical manifestations;
- ancestral or memorial beings;
- collective cultural identities;
- engineered persistence systems;
- undead or altered-body societies;
- Necropolis domains and polities;
- Memory Sea ecosystems;
- visitors, migrants and enclaves from other realms;
- ordinary geological and biological resources;
- specialist spirit-active materials;
- identity predators and dangerous absence phenomena;
- historical reflections that may be accurate, incomplete, biased, disputed, manipulated or reconstructed.

A spirit resembling an ancestor is **not automatically** the persisted spirit record of an NPC who died elsewhere. A memory-being reconstructing a historical person is **not automatically** that original person resurrected.

---

# 3. Explicit Non-Identity: What the Ancestral Veil Is Not

The Ancestral Veil is **not**:

- the universal afterlife;
- the automatic destination of dead players;
- the automatic destination of dead NPCs;
- the automatic destination of dead creatures;
- a database containing one spirit record for every death in the game;
- the owner of player respawn;
- the owner of tombstones or death-recovery inventory logic;
- the owner of universal resurrection;
- the owner of universal soul migration;
- a generic undead dimension;
- a moral category in which death-themed or undead societies are presumed evil;
- a mandatory destination for every culture that practices funerary rites;
- a second copy of the Somnolent Expanse dream system;
- a universal historical truth machine whose apparitions are always correct.

These exclusions are **canon**, not implementation suggestions.

Older sources that implied automatic death travel, routine near-death entry, universal spirit migration or resurrection law are superseded by this FCC set.

# 5. Realm Fantasy Pillars

## 5.1 Memory Can Become Place

Selected memories, records, shared cultural understanding, recognised names and stable identity anchors can influence the solidity, accessibility or presentation of Veil locations.

Examples include:

- remembered buildings strengthening or fading;
- historical districts becoming more complete as evidence is recovered;
- routes becoming readable once recognised relationships are established;
- archive spaces changing state when records are restored;
- Memory Sea islands forming around durable historical patterns;
- memorial sites becoming stable through sustained care.

These effects use **explicit game state**. The game does not simulate every memory possessed by every NPC.

## 5.2 Identity Has Mechanical Weight

Identity-sensitive mechanics may include:

- recognised names;
- titles and offices;
- route permissions;
- identity anchors;
- archive access;
- historical claims;
- cultural legitimacy;
- temporary recognition disruption;
- local recognition by structures or entities.

Identity hazards must never silently:

- delete the player profile;
- destroy save data;
- permanently erase NPC identity records;
- corrupt stable IDs;
- invalidate unrelated progression.

## 5.3 History Is Contested

The Veil preserves, reconstructs and distorts history, but does not guarantee one objective interpretation simply because something appears supernatural.

Records may contain:

- accurate testimony;
- missing context;
- contradictory witnesses;
- propaganda;
- ritualised cultural memory;
- degraded information;
- reconstructed guesses;
- deliberate falsification;
- politically motivated omission;
- genuine unknowns.

Gameplay can therefore require investigation, comparison and evidence rather than accepting the first apparition as truth.

## 5.4 Spirit Ecology Is Ecology

Veil-native spirits are inhabitants or organisms, not generic loot containers.

Some are intelligent persons. Others may be:

- animals;
- predators;
- pollinators;
- scavengers;
- route-following fauna;
- colony organisms;
- environmental entities;
- memory-born life;
- semi-corporeal or incorporeal creatures.

There is no generic universal `Soul` inventory item produced when a Veil creature dies.

## 5.5 Physical and Spiritual Matter Coexist

The Veil contains ordinary physical matter alongside spirit-active phenomena.

It therefore supports:

- physical construction;
- settlement development;
- farming and food production where appropriate;
- mining and material gathering;
- trade and logistics;
- water/sea travel;
- crafted machinery;
- specialist spirit-active devices;
- mixed physical/spiritual architecture.

The realm is not composed entirely of intangible ghost matter.

## 5.6 Death-Themed Content Is Not Morality

Undeath, memorial culture, body preservation, engineered longevity, altered persistence, identity storage and Necropolis systems are morally neutral categories by default.

Moral evaluation follows actions such as:

- consent or coercion;
- exploitation;
- personhood recognition;
- violence;
- rights;
- deception;
- political oppression;
- harm;
- restoration;
- stewardship.

## 5.7 Multiple Outcomes Matter

Major conflicts may support:

- investigation;
- negotiation;
- restoration;
- identity stabilisation;
- cleansing;
- route repair;
- historical reconstruction;
- political reform;
- recognition or rejection of claims;
- containment;
- sealing;
- relocation;
- combat.

Kill-only resolution is not the default design assumption.

# 6. Canonical Realm Laws

The Ancestral Veil uses bounded rule families. These are gameplay contracts, not unrestricted metaphysical simulation.

## 6.1 Recognition Law

A name, title, claim, archive entry, relationship, community identity or place becomes mechanically significant only after an explicit supported state establishes recognition.

Recognition may arise through:

- formal introduction;
- registration;
- archive confirmation;
- ritual acknowledgement;
- legal or political recognition;
- discovered historical evidence;
- inherited or transferred office;
- an authored realm interaction.

Recognised identity may affect:

- route access;
- archive permissions;
- Court standing;
- wards;
- claims;
- settlement services;
- structure responses;
- selected creature behaviour;
- dungeon state;
- authority outcomes.

**Prohibited implementation:** treating arbitrary typed or spoken text as automatically metaphysically binding.

## 6.2 Memory Solidity Law

Selected Veil structures, routes, reflections and phenomena can gain or lose solidity according to bounded memory-state inputs.

Possible inputs include:

- documented evidence;
- local remembrance strength;
- archive restoration;
- authorised ritual maintenance;
- settlement upkeep;
- recovered historical records;
- major event outcomes;
- authority state;
- regional instability.

The simulation uses discrete states, authored transitions or regional summaries rather than continuous per-person memory simulation.

## 6.3 Relationship Route Law

Some **Processional Roads**, ferries and associated crossings may connect recognised relationships, destinations or historical links rather than simple Euclidean distance.

Examples:

- a route between two communities linked by long-standing treaty;
- a road that becomes usable once a historical destination is correctly identified;
- a ferry route keyed to an established archive or name-anchor;
- a bridge that recognises a legitimate claimant or sanctioned traveller.

These routes remain deterministic from seed, saved state and explicit conditions. They do not teleport players arbitrarily.

## 6.4 Historical Reflection Law

Forgotten City Reflections, Memory Archive spaces and Memory Sea sites may become more complete, accurate or solid as supporting evidence is recovered.

Possible states include:

- fragmentary;
- disputed;
- reconstructed;
- corroborated;
- falsified;
- restored;
- politically contested;
- deliberately suppressed.

Restoration does not automatically prove one faction's preferred interpretation.

## 6.5 Spirit Interaction Law

Spirit-active materials, tools, barriers and structures interact with incorporeal or semi-corporeal entities through explicit capabilities and tags.

Examples include:

- Veil Silk screens or bindings;
- Soul Silver fittings and specialist equipment;
- Memory Glass viewing/recording surfaces;
- Grave Salt boundary stabilisation;
- Echo Pearl route/stability devices;
- Oathbone ritual or structural components.

No universal rule states that every silver object, every spell or every magical material affects all spirits identically.

## 6.6 Identity Stability Law

Identity can be temporarily destabilised by selected hazards or entities, especially in Predator Dark and Devouring Silence content.

Bounded effects may temporarily disrupt:

- map labels;
- route recognition;
- NPC recognition cues;
- selected UI names;
- local permissions;
- objective clarity;
- specific identity-sensitive abilities.

These effects must preserve authoritative underlying records and recover cleanly.

## 6.7 Necropolis State Law

**Necropolis** is a cross-biome domain/state family, not a ninth biome and not a universal afterlife function.

A Necropolis domain may exist where death, embodiment, persistence, remains, memory, identity storage, undeath or cessation has been deliberately:

- altered;
- engineered;
- trapped;
- institutionalised;
- ritualised;
- preserved;
- redirected locally;
- made politically significant.

Possible Necropolis content includes:

- undead or altered-body societies;
- engineered longevity;
- artificial memory populations;
- tomb cities;
- body-preservation cultures;
- identity-storage institutions;
- failed immortality systems;
- persistent ruler-states;
- environments in which ending or changing a state is unusually difficult.

Necropolis content does **not** imply that ordinary deceased people from other realms have been automatically routed there.

## 6.8 Boundary Stability Law

Some Veil regions possess stronger or weaker interaction between physical and spirit-active states.

Boundary stability can affect:

- visibility of entities;
- solidity of selected structures;
- route reliability;
- specialist material behaviour;
- ward requirements;
- cleansing or stabilisation work;
- local hazard intensity.

Boundary changes use bounded regional or site states, not unrestricted phase simulation.

# 7. Legacy Time-Law Reconciliation

Earlier Atlas material described Veil time as **“narrative rather than uniform.”**

That phrase is retained as historical design provenance but is **not adopted as a realm-wide time-warp system in FCC-03A**.

Current canon does not require:

- global time dilation;
- reversed chronology;
- arbitrary time skips;
- save-time divergence;
- NPC ageing desynchronisation;
- freeform causality rewriting.

Authored sites, dreams, memories or historical reconstructions may present subjective chronology, repeated scenes or non-linear information, but those are content presentations or bounded local mechanics unless a later FCC decision explicitly defines a true time-law effect.

This avoids silently creating an expensive and fragile realm-wide simulation mechanic that was never separately approved.

# 8. Universal Death and Respawn Ownership Exclusion

This section supersedes older source wording that implied universal death travel, automatic spirit migration, resurrection law or routine near-death entry.

## 8.1 Player Death

Player death remains governed by Leyforge's universal death/failure system.

The existing universal design may include, according to progression and settings:

- a tombstone or equivalent recovery object containing inventory/experience;
- respawn at a valid bed;
- shrine;
- home point;
- village respawn point;
- world spawn;
- other universally defined recovery locations.

The Ancestral Veil is not invoked merely because the player dies.

## 8.2 NPC and Creature Death

Dead NPCs and creatures from other realms do **not** automatically become Ancestral Veil entities.

The game must not create a one-to-one persistent Veil spirit record for every death.

## 8.3 Resurrection

FCC-03 does not own universal resurrection mechanics.

If a later universal or specialist system supports resurrection, it may consume Veil materials, knowledge, sites or permissions where deliberately designed, but the Veil does not become the mandatory simulation backend for resurrection.

## 8.4 Historical Ancestors and Spirit Beings

Ancestral figures may exist as:

- realm-native spirits;
- long-lived memory beings;
- reconstructed historical manifestations;
- authored entities;
- collective cultural identities;
- deliberately created memorial intelligences;
- ancient beings associated with a lineage or civilisation.

They are not automatically generated from prior NPC death records.

## 8.5 Funerary Culture

Funerary rites, memorial traditions and burial practices may matter culturally, politically or magically without becoming universal transit instructions for the dead.

Different cultures may believe different things about death. Those beliefs do not override developer/runtime truth unless a specific mechanic explicitly supports them.

# 9. Portal Access Contract

All major Leyforge realms use **one canonical normal portal type**.

The Ancestral Veil's canonical portal is the:

# **Veilgate**

The universal Realm Access system owns:

- portal runtime;
- route binding;
- transition orchestration;
- destination resolution;
- multiplayer authority;
- save/load behaviour;
- safe-return state;
- portal linking;
- failure/recovery handling.

FCC-03 owns the Veilgate's:

- identity;
- realm-specific material language;
- progression role;
- local requirements;
- destination-side presentation;
- realm-specific discovery and political hooks.

## 9.1 Veilgate Identity

The Veilgate should read as a **quiet recognised threshold**, not a generic violent rift.

Its physical language may include:

- Memory Stone frame architecture;
- Memory Glass surfaces or interfaces;
- Soul Silver fittings;
- an Echo Pearl keystone/core;
- Grave Salt stabilisation channels;
- inscriptions;
- recognised names;
- lanterns;
- reflective surfaces;
- Processional or memorial architectural motifs where culturally appropriate.

The exact final recipe, quantities, acquisition order and cross-realm dependency are intentionally deferred to the **global portal progression reconciliation**.

This is not an unresolved realm-design gap. Portal progression must be solved across all realms together so that the overall dimension graph remains coherent and achievable.

## 9.2 One Normal Portal Rule

Older source concepts such as:

- funerary gates;
- ancestral shrines;
- spirit roads;
- near-death crossings;
- remembered objects;
- medium rituals;
- sanctioned ferryman routes

are **not** alternate normal methods for entering the dimension.

Where retained, they may become:

- clues used to discover Veilgate knowledge;
- portal locations;
- activation knowledge;
- required permissions;
- portal stabilisation aids;
- local Veil routes;
- quest conditions;
- authored historical evidence;
- destination-side infrastructure.

They do not bypass the one-portal-per-realm rule.

## 9.3 Return Safety

- Normal return travel uses portal infrastructure under the universal Realm Access contract.
- First valid entry must resolve a safe or recoverable return route.
- Realm hazards may make travel dangerous but cannot silently strand a normal progression save through arbitrary procedural failure.
- Deliberate no-return challenge content, if ever authored, must be clearly signposted and governed by universal route rules.
- The Ferryman Sovereign may influence Veil-local route law and portal-side infrastructure but cannot override universal save-safety guarantees without an explicitly supported challenge state.

## 9.4 Portal Progression Dependencies

The final Veilgate recipe may require:

- Overworld materials;
- Veil research or recovered knowledge;
- another realm's material;
- specialist crafted components;
- authority or faction permission;
- a discovered key or stabiliser;
- a prerequisite portal technology tier.

Which of these become mandatory is deferred until all realm portals are compared together.

# 10. Progression Model

The eventual full-realm progression follows this broad structure.

## Stage 1 — Foreshadowing and Research

The player learns that the Ancestral Veil exists through source-supported clues such as:

- historical research;
- memorial structures;
- spirit evidence;
- damaged portal evidence;
- threshold teaser content;
- specialist scholarship;
- cross-realm discoveries.

Current Core implementation may expose only a bounded threshold/teaser and does not need to provide the complete realm.

## Stage 2 — Veilgate Knowledge

The player discovers:

- the existence of the Veilgate;
- its broad construction logic;
- its keystone/stabilisation requirements;
- any knowledge or permission prerequisites.

## Stage 3 — Construction and Activation

Once global progression conditions are satisfied, a valid Veilgate can be constructed and activated.

The final recipe is not locked in FCC-03A.

## Stage 4 — Safe Threshold Arrival

First arrival must occur in a valid survivable destination envelope appropriate to the route state.

The player should be able to learn the realm rather than immediately fail due to unknowable mechanics.

## Stage 5 — Identity and Route Adaptation

Early Veil progression teaches:

- recognised names;
- route markers;
- identity anchors;
- local hazard readability;
- Grave Salt/lantern/stability concepts;
- spirit interaction;
- safe use of Processional infrastructure.

## Stage 6 — Regional Exploration

The player reaches deeper biome families through ordinary exploration plus realm-local route systems.

Progression may involve:

- Processional Roads;
- ferries;
- Memory Sea travel;
- archive routes;
- political permissions;
- restored districts;
- Crownland access;
- stabilised Predator Dark crossings.

## Stage 7 — Historical and Political Integration

The player becomes involved in:

- archive disputes;
- competing historical claims;
- Necropolis politics;
- Memory Sea cultures;
- Processional law;
- Forgotten City restoration;
- Crownland diplomacy;
- settlement relationships.

## Stage 8 — Dungeon and Authority Outcomes

Major sites and authorities become accessible through multiple resolution paths.

Progression may reward:

- route access;
- knowledge;
- stabilisation capability;
- political change;
- structure access;
- material grants;
- restoration states;
- specialist crafting;
- combat trophies where appropriate.

## Stage 9 — Advanced Realm Integration

Late Veil participation may include:

- exporting stabilised materials;
- constructing Veil-compatible infrastructure elsewhere;
- building permanent settlements or outposts;
- advanced Memory Glass/Soul Silver/Veil Silk use;
- cross-realm historical projects;
- high-risk Predator Dark expeditions;
- major Necropolis diplomacy;
- portal-network integration;
- Void-corruption response.

## 10.1 Progression Prohibitions

Normal progression must **not** require the player to:

- die in order to enter the Veil;
- intentionally trigger near-death as a portal substitute;
- harvest generic souls;
- kill spirits as a universal resource gate;
- join one mandatory faction;
- accept one culture's historical interpretation as universally correct;
- defeat every authority through combat.

# 11. Survival and Entity-Needs Boundary

Older Atlas material suggested that ordinary hunger might not apply in many Veil regions.

FCC-03A does **not** establish a realm-wide replacement hunger system.

Instead:

- player survival remains owned by universal/player systems;
- physical and biological Veil-native beings may eat where their species requires it;
- incorporeal beings do not automatically use ordinary biological hunger;
- local food availability and cuisine are handled by later FCC-03 content;
- identity erosion, memory instability, Mourning Mists and other realm hazards are **additional realm risks**, not a replacement for all universal survival systems.

This prevents the Ancestral Veil from silently overriding player survival ownership.

# 15. Civilisation and Personhood Boundary

FCC-03 supports many kinds of inhabitants without collapsing them into one category.

The following must remain distinct where applicable:

- biological ancestry;
- culture;
- faction;
- citizenship;
- political allegiance;
- spirit/physical embodiment state;
- memory-born origin;
- Necropolis status;
- visitor/migrant status;
- religious belief;
- historical claim.

A spirit is not automatically an ancestor.  
An ancestor is not automatically a faction member.  
An undead person is not automatically a Necropolis citizen.  
A Necropolis citizen is not automatically hostile.  
A historical reflection is not automatically a legally recognised person.  
A person may belong to the Veil without having originated through death.

Document 13 and the appropriate FCC civilisation documents remain political/personhood owners.

# 17. Block, Item and Resource Boundary

The universal Leyforge single-definition rule applies throughout FCC-03.

If something is fundamentally a placeable block and, when broken, is intended to remain/recover as that same block:

- it has one canonical **Block** definition;
- inventory uses its generated/reference block-form representation;
- a duplicate independently authored Item definition is prohibited.

Separate Item definitions exist only for genuinely distinct objects such as:

- powders;
- fibres;
- ingots;
- plates;
- wires;
- tools;
- reagents;
- creature products;
- food;
- crafted components;
- non-placeable materials;
- transformed outputs.

No Veil material automatically receives every possible shape. Derived forms require physical, cultural, magical or gameplay justification.

# 18. Corruption Boundary

The Ancestral Veil contains native hazards that can resemble horror, decay, silence, predation, grief or identity loss without being Void corruption.

Examples of native non-Void phenomena include:

- Mourning Mists;
- Predator Dark;
- Remnant Eaters;
- unstable memories;
- contested reflections;
- Necropolis failures;
- Devouring Silence;
- route loss.

**Void corruption remains a distinct external corruption source.**

Void influence may exploit weakened Veil regions, but native Veil phenomena must not be retroactively relabelled as corruption.

Severe irreversible Void transformation may create distinct corrupted material or creature definitions only when anatomy, behaviour or material identity changes enough to justify a new canonical definition.

# 19. Core Teaser vs Full-Realm Canon

Earlier production planning defines a bounded **Ancestral Veil threshold teaser** for Core scope.

FCC-03 does not automatically promote the full realm into the current Core implementation milestone.

The teaser may provide:

- evidence;
- history/Codex knowledge;
- a safe threshold interaction;
- future portal clues;
- limited spirit phenomena;
- verified return.

The full realm defined by FCC-03 is a later production target unless the roadmap is deliberately changed.

This distinction protects scope while allowing the final content canon to be designed now.

# 20. Cross-Realm Interface Summary

## 20.1 Verdant Covenant

Moonroot regions may have limited natural resonance with the Ancestral Veil.

This does not create a second portal method. Normal interdimensional entry still uses the Veilgate.

## 20.2 Somnolent Expanse

Memory, dream and identity themes may interact, but FCC-03 does not own dream-network simulation.

Somnolent mechanics must be consumed through Somnolent interfaces.

## 20.3 Void Between

Void corruption can damage Veil matter, identity states, routes or structures.

# 21. Implementation Feasibility Rule

The Ancestral Veil's **player-facing fantasy and canon are authoritative; any particular implementation technique is not**.

If a proposed memory, identity, route, reflection or dual-state mechanic would require fragile, unbounded or excessive computation, implementation may use:

- deterministic state machines;
- authored variants;
- regional summaries;
- cached route graphs;
- event-driven updates;
- limited interaction volumes;
- discrete identity/recognition tokens;
- tagged entity capabilities;
- LOD summaries;
- approximations under lower simulation settings.

The implementation may simplify *how* the effect is calculated, but should preserve:

- readability;
- player agency;
- save safety;
- deterministic authority;
- the realm's identity;
- intended gameplay consequences.

# 23. FCC-03A Acceptance Gate

FCC-03A is ready for realm-local acceptance when all of the following are true:

- the Ancestral Veil is clearly defined as a complete realm rather than an afterlife database;
- universal player death/respawn ownership is explicitly excluded;
- NPC/creature death does not automatically create Veil entities;
- spirit entities are not generic soul resources;
- identity and memory mechanics are bounded, deterministic and save-safe;
- history may be contested rather than automatically true;
- physical and spirit-active matter coexist;
- Necropolis is defined as a cross-biome domain/state family;
- the one-portal-per-realm rule is applied through **Veilgate**;
- old access concepts are reclassified rather than retained as portal bypasses;
- progression does not require death, soul harvesting or kill-only outcomes;
- old universal resurrection/death-travel language is superseded;
- the legacy narrative-time concept is prevented from silently becoming a realm-wide simulation system;
- later FCC-03 document ownership is explicit;
- Core teaser scope is separated from eventual full-realm canon;
- implementation scaling is permitted without changing canon;
- final portal recipe remains deliberately deferred to global portal progression reconciliation.
