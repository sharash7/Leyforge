# 1. Purpose

FCC-02E defines the authoritative Verdant Covenant canon for:

- the final twelve named creature foundations/species used to establish the realm's gameplay-bearing fauna;
- habitat, diet, ecological role, activity, lifecycle, population, and migration foundations;
- the relationship between creatures and the eight Verdant biome families;
- creature interactions with seasonal, Covenant, Threshold, Resonance, blight, rot, and Void-corruption states;
- creature-derived materials and non-lethal resource acquisition;
- domestication, taming, stewardship, transport, husbandry, and settlement relationships;
- creature variants and the distinction between ordinary state variation and genuinely distinct creature definitions;
- elite, guardian, invasive, blighted, and corrupted creature states;
- simulation scaling for low-end worlds;
- registry, provenance, block/item, recipe, and art-handoff boundaries.

This document completes the Verdant Covenant's realm-local creature ecology beyond the five foundational families defined in World Content Atlas Set 24.

It does **not** own:

- flora, crops, fungi-as-botanical-content, or plant propagation - FCC-02D;
- material identities derived from plant/geology systems - FCC-02C;
- civilisation industries, creature husbandry buildings, markets, or settlement layouts - FCC-02F;
- exact item definitions, recipe quantities, equipment recipes, food recipes, or crafting station requirements - FCC-02G;
- dungeon guardian encounter implementations or realm authorities - FCC-02H;
- detailed irreversible corruption transformations and restoration chains - FCC-02I;
- final stable-ID normalisation, asset certification, or visual material inheritance - FCC-02J, FCC-12, and FCC-14.

# 2. Canon Statement

The Verdant Covenant is a functioning magical ecology, not a themed spawn table.

Every canonical creature must have a reason to exist beyond combat. A creature may:

- graze;
- pollinate;
- prune;
- hunt;
- decompose;
- disperse seeds;
- maintain waterways;
- shape terrain;
- protect territory;
- indicate environmental state;
- migrate with seasons;
- participate in settlement economies;
- provide renewable materials;
- threaten or stabilise agriculture;
- interact with Covenant or Court law;
- reveal hidden routes;
- support transport;
- become a restoration partner;
- become a regional hazard under exceptional conditions.

The guiding rule is:

> **A creature exists because it belongs to the realm's ecology, culture, progression, or world simulation - not because the game needs another enemy with a health bar.**

Creature diversity may be visually broader than the twelve canonical gameplay-bearing foundations through age, sex, season, region, health, mutation, colour, size, faction influence, blight, and other bounded variants.

Those variants do not automatically create new creature registry definitions.

# 3. Source Reconciliation and Expansion Rule

World Content Atlas Set 24 established five foundational Verdant creature families:

1. Oathstag
2. Pollen Drake
3. Seasonal Fox
4. Thornbound Hunter
5. Rotcap Colony

The Atlas explicitly described those five as foundations rather than the final ecological ceiling.

FCC-02E therefore preserves those five foundations and expands the final realm-local named roster to twelve through seven additional ecological species:

6. Thornsilver Weaver
7. Moonroot Warden
8. Riverback
9. Crown Glider
10. Thornshell
11. Rotgrazer
12. Dreamburrower

The original five retain continuity with their earlier stable IDs where practical. The additional seven require canonical IDs during FCC-02J/final registry normalisation.

No previous creature family is silently deleted by this expansion.

# 5. Universal Verdant Creature Rules

## 5.1 State Variation Before Registry Multiplication

The following normally remain states or variants of an existing creature definition:

- juvenile / adult / elder;
- sex where visually relevant;
- spring / summer / autumn / winter state;
- region or biome tint;
- Court influence;
- health and injury;
- hunger;
- breeding state;
- migration state;
- tame / wild / bonded;
- blighted;
- lightly corrupted;
- scarred;
- domesticated lineage;
- elite size or experience;
- ordinary procedural cosmetic variation.

A new creature definition is justified only where anatomy, ecological role, behaviour, progression function, or resource logic changes enough that the entity is no longer meaningfully the same creature.

## 5.2 Corruption Is Normally a State

Void corruption does not automatically create `Corrupted <Creature>` registry entries.

Early and moderate corruption is normally represented through:

- corruption intensity;
- visual state;
- behaviour changes;
- altered targeting;
- reduced social recognition;
- abnormal resource interaction;
- spreading contamination;
- altered ecology.

Only irreversible transformations with materially different anatomy, lifecycle, AI, ecological role, or progression function may later be promoted to distinct corrupted creature definitions.

## 5.3 Natural Rot Is Not Corruption

Rotcap Colonies and Rotgrazers are natural ecology.

They must not be presented as corrupted simply because they are associated with decomposition.

FCC-02E recognises four distinct concepts:

1. natural decomposition;
2. ecological disease/blight;
3. magical imbalance;
4. genuine Void corruption.

These may interact but remain distinguishable.

## 5.4 No Generic Fantasy Loot

Verdant creatures do not use a universal monster-part loot model.

No creature automatically drops:

- generic Essence;
- generic Soul;
- random fantasy crystal;
- generic Monster Meat;
- arbitrary magical dust;
- unrelated equipment;
- currency with no provenance.

Creature rewards must come from legitimate ecological or authored sources such as:

- shed antlers;
- moulted scales;
- fibre gathered from nests;
- naturally discarded spines;
- eggs where culturally/legal conditions allow;
- milk, secretions, wax, nectar, or similar outputs where biologically justified;
- hides/meat from legitimate hunting;
- salvage from a dangerous encounter;
- husbandry products;
- settlement trade;
- knowledge;
- tracking access;
- faction or Court rewards.

Exact inventory item definitions are admitted by FCC-02G only when a recipe, trade, food, equipment, quest, or other gameplay dependency actually needs them.

# 6. Creature Material and Harvesting Philosophy

## 6.1 Sustainable Acquisition

Where practical, Verdant creature resources should support non-lethal acquisition through:

- shedding;
- moulting;
- grooming;
- abandoned nests;
- naturally lost antlers;
- managed husbandry;
- symbiotic cultivation;
- voluntary exchange through intelligent communities where applicable;
- recovery from naturally deceased creatures;
- predator/prey ecosystem salvage.

Lethal hunting remains possible where appropriate to survival, culture, danger, population management, food, or player choice.

The game does not impose a universal moral judgement on hunting, but ecological and political consequences may exist.

## 6.2 Provenance

Any creature-derived crafting item must record or infer sufficient provenance for systems that require it, including where relevant:

- source creature family;
- state/variant;
- acquisition method;
- quality;
- season;
- corruption/blight state;
- processing state;
- legal/ownership status where settlement or Court systems consume it.

## 6.3 Creature Products Are Not Automatically Major Materials

Creature-derived outputs are generally **components, food resources, fibres, hides, biological reagents, or trade goods**, not new major material families.

Major material status requires cross-realm material reconciliation through FCC-12.

# 7. Oathstag

**Legacy Stable ID:** `creature.realm.verdant.oathstag`

## 7.1 Identity

The **Oathstag** is the iconic large browser of the Sunlit Canopy and associated seasonal routes.

It combines the earlier Atlas court-linked grazer role with the FCC interview's colossal-root/canopy browser concept.

Oathstags are:

- tall, long-legged ungulates adapted to enormous root networks and elevated forest terrain;
- capable of traversing broad branchways, root shelves, ridges, and living paths unavailable to ordinary Overworld deer;
- strongly influenced by seasonal migration states;
- visually associated with antler growth and Covenant recognition;
- ecologically important browsers and seed movers.

They are not automatically sacred, tame, or friendly.

## 7.2 Habitat

Primary:

- Sunlit Canopy;
- seasonal migration corridors;
- Greatheart forest edges.

Secondary:

- Pollenwild transitions;
- Seasonal Nexus approaches;
- selected Court lands.

## 7.3 Diet

Primary diet may include:

- leaves;
- young shoots;
- fallen Dawnfruit;
- canopy herbs;
- seasonal browse;
- selected fungi/mineral lick behaviour where ecologically justified.

## 7.4 Seasonal and Covenant Interaction

Oathstag antlers may change through bounded visual/game states associated with:

- season;
- age;
- herd status;
- recognised Court territory;
- honoured or broken local Covenant state.

This is not unrestricted interpretation of player speech.

Migration routing consumes explicit seasonal and regional state from FCC-02A/B.

## 7.5 Social Behaviour

Oathstags form herds of variable size.

World simulation may represent:

- grazing groups;
- family groups;
- seasonal migration herds;
- solitary mature individuals;
- rare Court-associated ceremonial herds.

## 7.6 Resource Relationships

Potential gameplay-bearing outputs include:

- **Oathstag Antler** - preferably shed and recoverable non-lethally; useful for carving, ritual fittings, tools, ornament, or specialist crafting where admitted by FCC-02G;
- hide/leather from legitimate hunting or natural death where a recipe requires it;
- meat as a food source where culturally/legal conditions allow.

Oathstag Antler does not automatically become a major material family.

## 7.7 Settlement Relationship

Possible settlement roles:

- protected seasonal herd;
- managed migration partner;
- ceremonial animal;
- dangerous crop/browser pressure;
- selectively domesticated or semi-managed herd;
- rare mount research candidate only if later movement design proves physically and production-feasible.

No universal mount guarantee is created by FCC-02E.

# 10. Thornbound Hunter

**Legacy Stable ID:** `creature.realm.verdant.thornbound_hunter`

## 10.1 Identity

The **Thornbound Hunter** is the principal apex predator and territorial living-terrain hunter of the Thorn-Court Marches.

It is a native ecological entity, not an inherently evil monster.

Its body incorporates thorn, bark-like armour, flexible plant-like growth, or symbiotic vegetation sufficient to visually bind it to Marches ecology without making it a generic plant construct.

## 10.2 Habitat

Primary:

- Thorn-Court Marches;
- Court border forests;
- Wardthorn corridors;
- abandoned treaty zones.

Rare:

- Rotwild interfaces;
- Court-controlled hunting grounds.

## 10.3 Predation and Territory

Primary prey may include:

- Thornshell;
- smaller fauna;
- vulnerable browsers;
- invasive/blighted creatures.

Behaviour may be altered by explicit Court or territorial state so that some individuals preferentially target:

- intruders;
- oathbreakers recognised by bounded Covenant systems;
- blighted fauna;
- competing predators.

It does not magically infer moral guilt.

## 10.4 Resource Relationships

Potential components where gameplay requires them:

- naturally shed/broken **Thornbound Spine**;
- hide/armoured tissue from legitimate hunting;
- biological samples used for ward or predator research.

These remain specialist components, not major material families.

## 10.5 Domestication

The Thornbound Hunter is **not** an ordinary domestic animal.

Exceptional bonding, Court stewardship, magical handling, or authored quest relationships may exist, but routine livestock breeding should not trivialise its apex-predator identity.

# 11. Rotcap Colony

**Legacy Stable ID:** `creature.realm.verdant.rotcap_colony`

## 11.1 Identity

The **Rotcap Colony** is a coordinated decomposer fungal collective whose mobile fruiting bodies can act as creature entities.

FCC-02D owns Rotcap Fungus as botanical/fungal content.

FCC-02E owns the behaviour of an established colony once it becomes a mobile, reactive, population-bearing creature system.

## 11.2 Ecological Role

Rotcap Colonies:

- break down dead vegetation;
- recycle damaged living structures;
- consume decaying organic matter;
- redistribute nutrients;
- compete with harmful blight;
- sometimes spread too aggressively into weakened living systems.

They are natural decomposition actors.

## 11.3 Behaviour

A colony may appear as:

- mostly stationary fungal bed;
- distributed mobile fruiting bodies;
- coordinated defensive cluster;
- migrating decomposer front;
- restoration partner around damaged woodland;
- ecological hazard when overabundant.

## 11.4 Blight and Corruption

A Rotcap Colony may become:

- diseased;
- blight-carrying;
- magically imbalanced;
- Void-corrupted.

Those are states separate from its ordinary ecology.

## 11.5 Resources

Rotcap-derived food, reagents, spores, or tissue must be defined through plant/fungal and item governance rather than generic monster drops.

Routine killing is not required for useful Rotcap products.

# 13. Moonroot Warden

**Provisional Canonical ID:** `creature.realm.verdant.moonroot_warden`

## 13.1 Identity

The **Moonroot Warden** is a living root-bodied quadrupedal guardian associated with ancient Moonroot Groves.

It is physically native to the Verdant Covenant while possessing limited sensitivity to memory, spirit resonance, and nearby Ancestral Veil affinity.

It is not a dead soul and does not implement afterlife mechanics.

## 13.2 Habitat

Primary:

- Moonroot Groves;
- Memory Grove settlements;
- old root sanctuaries;
- Spirit Moss-rich zones.

Rare:

- Dreaming Root transition regions;
- protected ancient sites.

## 13.3 Behaviour

Moonroot Wardens may:

- patrol recognised grove boundaries;
- protect old roots and Memory Seed sites;
- react to vandalism or destructive harvesting;
- become neutral toward recognised guests;
- indicate spirit/memory disturbances;
- assist restoration after ecological damage.

## 13.4 Spirit Resonance

Their Veil affinity is represented through explicit sensory/status systems such as:

- detecting spirit-active phenomena;
- reacting to Memory Seed encoding;
- responding to Spirit Moss state;
- perceiving certain incorporeal or memory-linked entities where cross-realm rules allow.

It does not create direct access to the Ancestral Veil.

## 13.5 Resource Relationship

The Moonroot Warden should not be treated as a routine resource animal.

Possible salvage from naturally destroyed/defeated Wardens may include root-like biological components only where a later recipe explicitly requires them.

The preferred gameplay value is ecological, protective, navigational, and relational.

## 14.2 Habitat

Primary:

- Living Riverlands;
- reed wetlands;
- slow channels;
- tributaries;
- settlement-adjacent waterways.

Riverbacks may:

- build root/reed dams;
- open side channels;
- maintain pools;
- clear blocked water;
- gather reeds;
- create fish/amphibian habitat;
- unintentionally flood farms or roads.

## 14.4 Social Behaviour

Riverbacks live in family or colony groups.

Colonies may maintain:

- lodges;
- feeding grounds;
- local channels;
- defended nurseries.

## 14.5 Settlement Relationship

Potential roles:

- managed waterway partners;
- tolerated ecological engineers;
- nuisance flood creators;
- semi-domesticated work animals;
- settlement mascots or culturally significant wildlife.

## 14.6 Resources

Possible legitimate outputs may include:

- fur/hide where hunting systems require it;

# 17. Rotgrazer

**Provisional Canonical ID:** `creature.realm.verdant.rotgrazer`

The **Rotgrazer** is a Rotwild herbivore/decomposer-grazer adapted to diseased vegetation, fungal growth, fallen plant matter, and ecological waste.

## 17.2 Habitat

Primary:

- Rotwilds;
- forest dieback zones;
- restoration fronts;
- fungal clearings.

## 17.3 Diet

May consume:

- decaying leaves;
- dead wood;
- diseased but non-Void vegetation;
- safe fungal growth;
- fallen fruit;
- decomposer-rich ground matter.

It should avoid or become harmed/changed by genuine Void contamination unless corrupted.

## 17.4 Ecological Role

Rotgrazers:

- reduce diseased biomass;
- spread decomposer fungi;
- return nutrients to soil;
- clear space for regrowth;
- support restoration cycles.

## 17.5 Resource Relationships

Potential food/hide resources may exist where gameplay needs them.

Rotgrazer products are not inherently tainted merely because the animal lives in the Rotwilds.

Blight/corruption state must be checked separately.

- create temporary tunnels;

- `dream_resonance_intensity`;

- enter another dimension through ordinary burrowing;

## 18.5 Resource Relationship

The species does not require a unique harvest item.

Its primary value is:

- navigation;
- environmental signalling;
- root ecology;
- possible controlled burrow interaction.

# 20. Food-Web Foundations

FCC-02E does not require a fully simulated energy model for every individual creature.

Instead, Verdant ecology uses bounded trophic relationships.

## 20.1 Major Browser / Grazer Layer

Includes:

- Oathstag;
- Thornshell;
- Rotgrazer;
- Riverback in herbivorous/omnivorous contexts.

These consume defined vegetation categories and create pressure on flora without calculating every mouthful in unloaded regions.

## 20.2 Small / Medium Consumer Layer

Includes:

- Seasonal Fox;
- Crown Glider;
- Riverback;
- Pollen Drake.

These consume small fauna, fruit, insects, seeds, fungi, nectar, or mixed diets.

## 20.3 Apex / Territorial Layer

Includes:

- Thornbound Hunter;
- large/elite Pollen Drake variants only where appropriate;
- authored guardian interactions outside ordinary food-web logic.

## 20.4 Decomposer Layer

Includes:

- Rotcap Colony;
- Rotgrazer;
- Rootcap/Rotcap fungal ecology from FCC-02D;
- ambient decomposer fauna.

## 20.5 Pollination / Propagation Layer

Includes:

- Thornsilver Weaver;
- Pollen Drake;
- Crown Glider;
- ambient pollinators;
- Riverback and Oathstag where seed movement applies.

# 21. Population and Reproduction Rules

## 21.1 Loaded-Area Simulation

In actively simulated areas, creatures may use:

- age states;
- hunger/foraging needs;
- breeding windows;
- nesting/denning;
- territorial relationships;
- herd/colony membership;
- migration targets;
- injury;
- fear/threat response;
- local resource pressure.

## 21.2 Unloaded-Area Simulation

Outside active areas, population simulation must collapse into aggregate state such as:

- estimated population;
- health;
- reproduction pressure;
- migration direction;
- habitat quality;
- predation pressure;
- disease/blight pressure;
- settlement interaction;
- corruption exposure.

The game must not simulate every creature continuously across an unloaded realm.

## 21.3 Population Recovery

Population recovery depends on:

- habitat suitability;
- breeding state;
- food availability class;
- predation pressure;
- hunting pressure;
- blight/corruption;
- seasonal conditions;
- migration connectivity.

No species should respawn infinitely in a way that ignores habitat destruction.

At the same time, low-end simulation may approximate these relationships rather than model them individually.

# 22. Migration

Migration is a core Verdant ecological feature but remains deterministic and performance-bounded.

Possible migratory species include:

- Oathstag;
- Pollen Drake;
- Thornsilver Weaver;
- selected Seasonal Fox populations;
- Crown Glider dispersal groups.

Migration consumes:

- season;
- biome suitability;
- route connectivity;
- Court restrictions where explicit;
- habitat damage;
- major events;
- settlement barriers;
- blight/corruption.

Unloaded migration is represented through regional population transfer rather than pathfinding thousands of off-screen agents.

---

# 23. Creature and Covenant-Law Interaction

Verdant creatures do not understand every legal or social concept as humans do.

Creature interaction with realm law must be explicit and species-appropriate.

Examples:

- Oathstag migration may respond to recognised seasonal/Court route states;
- Thornbound Hunters may recognise bounded territorial/oathbreaker status;
- Moonroot Wardens may recognise guest/protected-grove permissions;
- domesticated or managed creatures may recognise settlement ownership/handling state;
- wildlife may respond to hospitality-protected zones only where a specific ecological or magical rule justifies it.

The game must not perform unrestricted natural-language legal reasoning for wildlife.

---

# 24. Creature and Seasonal-State Interaction

Seasonal state may affect:

- migration;
- breeding;
- coat/colour;
- feeding;
- aggression;
- activity period;
- nest placement;
- resource shedding;
- population distribution;
- susceptibility/resistance to hazards.

Not every species requires four completely different AI packages.

Seasonal change should be implemented primarily through parameters, state tables, appearance layers, animation selection, diet weighting, and habitat suitability.

---

# 25. Creature and Settlement Relationships

Verdant settlements may classify nearby creature populations as:

- domestic;
- semi-domestic;
- managed wild;
- protected;
- tolerated;
- pest;
- dangerous;
- invasive;
- sacred/culturally significant;
- trade/resource partner;
- restoration partner.

Classification may differ by culture or settlement.

Examples:

- Thornsilver Weavers may be actively supported by fibre cultivators;
- Riverbacks may be welcomed by one river settlement and removed from another due to flooding;
- Oathstags may be protected along migration corridors but hunted elsewhere;
- Seasonal Foxes may be tolerated around grain stores if they suppress worse pests;
- Thornbound Hunters may be Court-protected territorial fauna;
- Rotgrazers may be deliberately introduced into restoration zones.

FCC-02F owns the final cultural/settlement implementation of these relationships.

# 26. Domestication, Taming, and Husbandry

Domestication must be species-specific.

## 26.1 Strong Candidates

Likely manageable or domesticable under appropriate progression:

- Riverback;
- Thornsilver Weaver;
- selected Oathstag populations;
- Seasonal Fox as companion/semi-domestic candidate;
- selected Crown Gliders.

## 26.2 Managed Wild Species

Better represented through habitat management than conventional domestication:

- Pollen Drake;
- Rotgrazer;
- Rotcap Colony;
- Thornshell.

## 26.3 Normally Non-Domestic

- Thornbound Hunter;
- Moonroot Warden;
- Dreamburrower where husbandry adds no clear gameplay value.

Exceptional authored relationships may override normal classifications without creating a universal species rule.

## 26.4 No Instant Taming

Feeding one arbitrary item should not universally tame complex fauna.

Taming/husbandry may depend on:

- repeated safe interaction;
- habitat;
- juvenile raising;
- settlement knowledge;
- seasonal timing;
- Court or cultural permission;
- specialised structures;
- species-specific food;
- breeding lineage;
- magical handling only where appropriate.

# 28. Hunting, Butchery, and Food

Creature food systems consume universal Leyforge hunting/butchery rules rather than creating a Verdant-only meat framework.

Realm-local rules may modify:

- legality;
- cultural acceptance;
- Court restrictions;
- ecological consequence;
- seasonal availability;
- contamination/blight checks;
- species-specific yields.

Not every creature is edible.

Not every culture eats every edible creature.

Food safety must distinguish:

- ordinary healthy meat;
- spoiled meat;
- diseased/blighted meat;
- Void-corrupted tissue;
- culturally prohibited but physically edible food.

Exact foods and recipes belong to FCC-02G.

- unusually large Thornshell;

- oversized Riverback colony leader;

- health;

Named unique encounter characters may be authored later, but ordinary elite variation must not inflate the canonical creature-family roster.

# 30. Dungeon and Guardian Interface

FCC-02E provides creature ecology to FCC-02H but does not own dungeon encounter design.

Verdant dungeon families may consume ordinary realm fauna where suitable.

Examples:

- Living Palace Roots may contain Oathstags, Moonroot Wardens, Crown Gliders, Rotcap colonies, or site-specific living guardians;
- Broken Covenant Gardens may use displaced pollinators, blighted fauna, Seasonal Foxes, or restoration partners;
- Thorn-Hunt Labyrinths may use Thornbound Hunters and Thornshells;
- Rot-Heart Groves may use Rotgrazers, Rotcap Colonies, blighted fauna, and corrupted variants;
- Seasonal Vaults may use season-state creature variants in addition to their dedicated guardian family.

Dedicated dungeon guardian families are owned by FCC-02H and are not counted among the twelve ordinary creature foundations.

# 31. Realm Authority Interface

The three fixed Verdant realm authorities remain outside the ordinary creature roster:

- Seasonal Regent;
- Rot-Crowned Hart;
- Oathbreaker Thorn.

They are realm-scale authorities/encounter identities rather than routine wildlife definitions.

FCC-02H owns their final encounter, resolution, reward, succession, and world-state contracts.

The Rot-Crowned Hart may visually relate to Oathstag ecology but is **not** an ordinary Oathstag variant.

# 32. Blight and Disease

Ordinary disease and ecological blight may affect creatures independently of Void corruption.

Blight may alter:

- health;
- reproduction;
- movement;
- diet;
- aggression;
- herd/colony behaviour;
- appearance;
- resource safety;
- habitat preference.

Blighted populations may sometimes recover through:

- habitat restoration;
- medicine;
- removal of contaminated food/water;
- fungal/ecological rebalancing;
- Court or settlement intervention;
- time and reduced pressure.

FCC-02I owns detailed restoration/corruption transformations.

---

# 33. Void Corruption

Void corruption is an external contaminating force whose ultimate origin belongs to the Void Between canon.

Creature corruption may produce:

- altered silhouette;
- tissue replacement;
- unnatural growth;
- behavioural hostility or disorientation;
- loss of normal migration;
- corrupted resource output;
- contamination spread;
- altered environmental interaction.

Early corruption may be cleansable.

Severe irreversible corruption may justify a new creature definition only if the transformation creates a substantially new content identity.

Not every corrupted state should yield a valuable collectible resource; otherwise corruption becomes something the player is encouraged to farm indiscriminately.

# 34. Creature Simulation Profiles

Each creature must receive a production simulation profile during implementation.

## 34.1 Suggested Profile Classes

### Profile A - Ambient / Low Cost

Suitable for:

- Crown Glider;
- ordinary Seasonal Fox populations;
- passive Pollen Drake groups.

Features:

- simple local needs;
- coarse unloaded population model;
- inexpensive group logic.

### Profile B - Social / Ecological

Suitable for:

- Oathstag;
- Riverback;
- Thornsilver Weaver;
- Thornshell;
- Rotgrazer.

Features:

- herd/colony membership;
- habitat interaction;
- seasonal or ecological behaviour;
- aggregate off-screen simulation.

### Profile C - Territorial / Specialist

Suitable for:

- Thornbound Hunter;
- Moonroot Warden;
- Dreamburrower.

Features:

- territory or site state;
- richer perception/behaviour only when loaded;
- simplified unloaded representation.

### Profile D - Colony / Distributed Organism

Suitable for:

- Rotcap Colony.

Features:

- colony-level authoritative state;
- limited active fruiting bodies;
- aggregate biomass/spread in unloaded simulation.

These profile labels are FCC guidance rather than engine class names.

# 35. Low-End World Simulation Contract

Verdant creature ecology must remain compatible with world-creation settings intended for low-end computers.

Lower simulation levels may reduce:

- number of actively simulated creature agents;
- pathfinding frequency;
- breeding checks;
- migration route detail;
- herd cohesion updates;
- scent/tracking calculations;
- ecological interaction frequency;
- off-screen simulation resolution.

Lower simulation levels must **not** change canonical content identity.

A Riverback remains a Riverback, an Oathstag remains an Oathstag, and ecological outcomes must remain directionally consistent even when calculated through coarser aggregate models.

Deterministic seeds and saved authoritative state must preserve continuity when simulation quality changes.

---

# 36. AI and Behaviour Boundaries

Creature AI may use:

- behaviour trees;
- utility selection;
- bounded state machines;
- navigation;
- perception;
- group coordination;
- deterministic or seeded variation;
- local environmental querying;
- authored magical rules.

Creature AI must not require:

- unrestricted language-model reasoning;
- natural-language interpretation of every Covenant statement;
- continuous full-realm cognition;
- expensive individual simulation while unloaded;
- unbounded procedural ability generation.

Any optional future Living World Intelligence layer may propose high-level ecological events, but authoritative creature systems validate and execute all actions.

# 37. Presentation and Art-Handoff Foundations

Every creature definition passed to final art production must specify:

- canonical silhouette;
- scale class;
- locomotion type;
- biome/environment palette relationship;
- material composition where relevant;
- seasonal visual states;
- age/sex variation where used;
- blight state;
- corruption state;
- emissive/magical features;
- animation requirements;
- hit/reactive regions where gameplay needs them;
- creature-product appearance where a named output exists.

The later Material, Colour & Texture Style Governance documents must treat creature surfaces according to their actual biological/material composition.

Examples:

- Thornshell plates should visually read as biological armour with bark/thorn influence, not literal random wood blocks;
- Moonroot Warden surfaces should inherit root/wood/material families defined by the art canon rather than arbitrary colours;
- Rotcap Colony must visually distinguish healthy decomposition from blight and Void corruption;
- seasonal creature variants must remain recognisably the same species.

# 44. Retired / Reinterpreted Creature Assumptions

The following assumptions are rejected or superseded:

- the five Set 24 creature families are the entire Verdant fauna catalogue;
- every biome requires a totally unique creature rig;
- every creature must drop a bespoke crafting item;
- rot-associated creatures are automatically corrupted;
- every seasonal appearance requires a new creature definition;
- every corrupted state requires a separate creature definition;
- Thornsilver Fibre is produced as ordinary insect silk;
- Moonroot spirit resonance means Moonroot creatures are dead souls;
- Dreaming Root Depths creatures can bypass the universal portal-access system;
- creature populations respawn independently of habitat state;
- all creature behaviour must be simulated continuously while unloaded.

# 45. Realm-Local Creature Completeness Gate

FCC-02E is realm-locally complete when the following remain true:

- twelve canonical gameplay-bearing creature foundations/species are defined;
- all eight biome families have meaningful ecological coverage;
- the original five Set 24 creature foundations remain reconciled;
- major trophic/ecological roles are covered;
- pollination, decomposition, browsing, predation, seed dispersal, water engineering, and Root Depth ecology are represented;
- creature-derived resource rules are provenance-based;
- no generic monster-loot economy has been introduced;
- non-lethal resource acquisition is supported where sensible;
- domestication/husbandry status is bounded by species;
- seasonal state does not multiply registries unnecessarily;
- blight and Void corruption remain separate;
- low-end simulation has defined fallbacks;
- dungeon guardians and realm authorities remain owned by FCC-02H;
- exact item/recipe admission remains owned by FCC-02G;
- final IDs/art certification remain owned by FCC-02J/FCC-14.

This gate is satisfied by FCC-02E v0.1, subject to later cross-realm reconciliation.

The final Verdant Covenant creature foundation is:

1. **Oathstag** - iconic large canopy browser and seasonal migrator.

8. **Riverback** - amphibious local waterway ecosystem engineer.
9. **Crown Glider** - upper-canopy seed disperser and fruit feeder.

Together these species establish the Verdant Covenant as a living ecological realm rather than a collection of combat encounters.
