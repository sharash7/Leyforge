# 1. Purpose

FCC-02A defines the authoritative high-level identity, realm laws, progression model, portal-access contract, canon boundaries, ownership rules, and implementation-facing constraints for the **Verdant Covenant**.

This document does **not** attempt to hold the full asset roster by itself. The remainder of FCC-02 owns the complete Verdant Covenant catalogue for biomes, materials, flora, fauna, civilisations, structures, dungeons, authorities, blocks, items, recipes, hazards, events, corruption states, and art-handoff data.

The Verdant Covenant must be treated as a complete persistent realm with its own ecology, civilisations, politics, resources, structures, hazards, and long-term state. It is not a single biome, a one-off dungeon, a temporary event map, or a themed reskin of the Overworld.

---

# 2. Canon Statement

The **Verdant Covenant** is a major persistent realm defined by living ecology, binding social and metaphysical agreements, seasonal power, responsive landscapes, colossal vegetation, living architecture, and cultures whose relationship with the environment is both practical and political.

Its central fantasy is:

> **A living world where ecology, season, recognised identity, promises, hospitality, and territorial relationships can have real consequences - but those consequences are expressed through bounded, deterministic gameplay systems rather than unrestricted natural-language magic or uncontrolled simulation.**

The realm is beautiful, abundant, dangerous, politically complex, and morally varied. It must not collapse into either of the following stereotypes:

- a universally friendly fairy forest;
- an inherently evil trickster realm.

Fae, seasonal courts, river peoples, wandering cultures, mortal diasporas, refugees, enclaves, guardians, predators, and other inhabitants may be cooperative, hostile, neutral, exploitative, compassionate, lawful, deceptive, or internally divided according to culture, faction, history, generated state, and individual behaviour.

# 4. Realm Fantasy Pillars

## 4.1 Living Abundance

The Verdant Covenant is biologically and magically productive. Colossal trees, layered canopy habitats, flowering plains, living waterways, root networks, fungal systems, pollinator ecologies, and seasonal growth define the realm.

Abundance does not mean infinite free resources. Extraction method, ecological damage, territorial claims, local law, seasonal state, and stewardship can affect availability and consequence.

## 4.2 Structured Covenant Law

Promises, names, thresholds, hospitality, and recognised relationships can matter materially, but only through explicit gameplay states and authored rule systems.

The game must never attempt to interpret every spoken or typed sentence as a magical contract.

## 4.3 Seasonal Power

Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter are metaphysical and ecological alignments as well as climatic states. They influence regions, courts, creatures, resources, events, and magic without requiring every location to use the same season simultaneously.

## 4.4 Ecology as Infrastructure

Living organisms may perform roles that other realms fulfil through conventional engineering: structural growth, water routing, filtration, conduits, route stabilisation, lighting, defensive boundaries, cultivation, storage, and environmental regulation.

This does not prohibit crafted machinery or imported technology. Verdant civilisation may combine living systems with ordinary construction, magic, metalwork, automation, and cross-realm technology where culturally and physically appropriate.

## 4.5 Political Nature

Forests, rivers, thresholds, settlements, seasonal territories, and living structures may exist inside recognised social or legal relationships. Environmental interaction therefore has political as well as ecological consequences.

## 4.6 Restoration Rather Than Binary Purity

The realm supports repair, regrowth, cleansing, ecological recovery, treaty repair, and restoration. Major problems do not automatically require destruction or combat.

# 5. Canonical Realm Laws

Verdant Covenant law is divided into bounded rule families. These laws are gameplay contracts, not unrestricted simulation.

## 5.1 Identity Law - Recognised Names

A name gains metaphysical significance only when recognised through an explicit game event or state, such as:

- a true name being deliberately given;
- formal Court registration;
- a recognised title;
- naming a place through an authorised system;
- a living structure or territory accepting a person or group;
- a ritual, pact, office, inheritance, or other explicit identity-binding process.

Recognised identity may affect:

- permissions;
- doors and living routes;
- wards;
- diplomacy;
- contracts;
- ownership;
- ritual targeting;
- magical targeting;
- access to structures or services.

**Prohibited implementation:** parsing ordinary dialogue to decide whether a name became magically binding.

## 5.2 Covenant Law - Promises

Promises become magically relevant only through explicit, mutually understood formal promise interactions supported by the game.

A recognised covenant may produce states such as:

- active obligation;
- fulfilled;
- disputed;
- suspended;
- transferred where lawful;
- oathbroken;
- repaired/reconciled;
- voided under valid conditions.

Consequences can alter reputation, permissions, routes, wards, Court relationships, environmental responses, quests, services, or selected magical effects.

**Prohibited implementation:** treating casual conversation as enforceable magical promises.

## 5.3 Threshold Law - Hospitality

Hospitality is represented through explicit host, guest, place, invitation, and threshold states.

Recognised hospitality can provide protections and obligations while a guest remains within the recognised threshold.

Possible effects include:

- temporary sanctuary;
- restricted aggression;
- access to food, shelter, services, or routes;
- duties imposed on host and guest;
- consequences for deliberate violation;
- modified Court or settlement reputation.

## 5.4 Resonance Law - Emotion and Community State

The realm may respond to strong or sustained emotional conditions when those conditions are represented by authored simulation states.

Valid sources include:

- settlement morale or distress;
- Court-scale celebration or mourning;
- powerful entities;
- major rituals;
- long-running conflicts;
- realm events;
- corruption pressure;
- large community states.

Possible effects include local ecology, weather, flowering, colour state, creature behaviour, resource state, or temporary environmental overlays.

**Prohibited implementation:** every NPC mood directly rewriting terrain every frame.

## 5.5 Seasonal Law

Seasonal influence exists at multiple scopes:

- broad Court-aligned territories;
- ordinary regional seasonal cycles;
- local ecological states;
- temporary events;
- rituals;
- political influence;
- magical anomalies;
- corruption or restoration states.

A region may therefore be aligned with one Seasonal Court while experiencing a different ordinary seasonal state.

## 5.6 Living Territory Law

Certain ancient organisms, Court territories, intelligent rivers, living structures, and authored sites may possess recognised territorial states.

Territorial response can influence:

- access;
- path availability;
- harvesting permissions;
- defensive flora;
- settlement placement;
- construction;
- reputation;
- local creature hostility;
- restoration opportunities.

## 5.7 Rare Authored Fae-Law Exceptions

Rare powerful entities, ancient places, dungeons, authorities, or realm events may impose authored rule exceptions that feel more spontaneous or folkloric.

Examples may include losing the ability to find a particular path again, being barred from a named threshold, or having a specific permission altered after a major encounter.

These exceptions must be deliberately authored and state-driven. They are not a license for arbitrary runtime rule generation.

# 6. Seasons and Seasonal Courts

## 6.1 Four Broad Alignments

The canonical broad seasonal alignments are:

1. Spring
2. Summer
3. Autumn
4. Winter

A seasonal alignment is **not** one monolithic faction.

Each alignment may contain multiple:

- kingdoms;
- courts;
- tribes;
- city-states;
- settlements;
- spirit communities;
- cultural traditions;
- rival claimants;
- political movements.

The phrase **Seasonal Court** may therefore refer to an individual polity or court operating within one of the four broad alignments, not to a single universal government for that season.

## 6.2 Seasonal Geography

Major Court regions can have strong seasonal identity, while ordinary Verdant regions run their own seasonal cycles.

Local seasonal states may be changed by:

- ecology;
- magic;
- Court influence;
- ritual;
- community emotion;
- major authorities;
- corruption;
- restoration;
- realm events.

## 6.3 Seasonal Convergence

The **Seasonal Nexus** biome family is the principal place where multiple seasonal influences converge and where rare mixed-season states may occur.

Mixed states must be bounded combinations, not uncontrolled procedural rule invention.

# 7. Portal Access Contract

## 7.1 Global Rule Consumed by Verdant Covenant

All major Leyforge realms use **one canonical portal type as their normal entry method**.

Portal travel is owned by the universal Realm Access system. FCC-02 defines the Verdant-specific identity, requirements, materials, progression role, and destination behaviour of its portal but does not redefine shared portal simulation, save, multiplayer, linking, or transport code.

The Verdant Covenant's canonical portal is the:

# **Covenant Portal**

No other normal access method may bypass the Covenant Portal.

Older concepts such as ancient covenant gates, Fae invitations, living-key rituals, seasonal crossings, dream-root paths, or negotiated Court routes are reclassified as one or more of the following where retained:

- clues used to discover Covenant Portal knowledge;
- portal components;
- activation permissions;
- attunement conditions;
- quest or political requirements;
- portal stabilisation aids;
- special authored narrative exceptions;
- local routes **within** the Verdant Covenant rather than dimension-entry methods.

They are not separate normal interdimensional travel systems.

## 7.2 Covenant Portal Identity

The Covenant Portal must visually communicate Verdant identity through living growth, root structure, foliage, seasonal response, or other organic architecture.

It is not required to use the same rectangular frame silhouette as portals to other realms. The universal system must support realm-specific portal geometry while maintaining common linking and transition behaviour.

The portal uses a dedicated keystone/core:

**Covenant Heart**

The Covenant Heart is a crafted or deliberately obtained functional component, not a naturally mined raw material.

Its exact recipe, quantities, initial acquisition path, replacement rules, and advanced variants are owned by later FCC-02 crafting/access documentation. The first-realm-access path must remain reachable without requiring materials that can only be obtained after entering the Verdant Covenant.

## 7.3 Portal Progression Rules

- The Covenant Portal is intended to be one of the more approachable major-realm portals compared with late-game realm portals.
- First access may rely primarily on Overworld progression, knowledge, botanical/magical materials, and a valid Covenant Heart acquisition path.
- Later portal construction may use Verdant-native resources for improved stability, convenience, resilience, or advanced configuration.
- Once validly established, a portal is normally reusable rather than consuming the full construction cost per trip.
- Return travel also uses portal infrastructure under the universal Realm Access contract.
- Portal arrival must resolve to a valid, non-arbitrary destination region suitable for the route's configured risk tier.
- Seed variation may change where or under what local circumstances a usable portal can be established, but it must not invalidate progression.

## 7.4 Cross-Realm Progression Principle

Verdant Covenant itself does not require a prior dimension for ordinary first access unless later global reconciliation proves a progression reason to change that decision.

Other realms may require Verdant resources, Covenant Portal knowledge, authority outcomes, or other cross-realm components. Conversely, advanced Verdant projects may later consume materials obtained from other dimensions.

This creates a progression web rather than forcing every realm into an identical recipe tier.

# 8. Progression Architecture

The Verdant Covenant supports progression without a single mandatory linear quest sequence.

## 8.1 Phase 1 - Discovery

The player discovers evidence that the realm exists and learns enough of the universal portal system and Verdant-specific requirements to pursue Covenant Portal construction or activation.

Discovery sources may include:

- structures;
- scholars;
- magical research;
- Codex knowledge;
- environmental clues;
- faction information;
- relics;
- world events;
- old portal remains;
- cross-realm evidence.

No fixed POC gate, guaranteed first portal location, or mandatory legacy Forest Hamlet route is canon.

## 8.2 Phase 2 - First Portal Establishment

The player obtains or crafts the required Covenant Portal components and establishes a valid portal.

The process must be readable and recoverable. If a generated world lacks one optional discovery route, other validated routes must exist.

## 8.3 Phase 3 - Arrival and Adaptation

Early Verdant play introduces:

- living terrain;
- seasonal state;
- covenant and threshold concepts;
- local hazards;
- native materials;
- realm settlement and diplomacy patterns;
- ecological harvesting expectations.

The player is not required to swear allegiance to a Court to survive or progress.

## 8.4 Phase 4 - Regional Expansion

Progression opens deeper or more politically sensitive regions through combinations of:

- exploration;
- improved equipment;
- ecological knowledge;
- settlement relationships;
- Court permissions;
- portal/route infrastructure;
- restoration projects;
- dungeon outcomes;
- crafted tools;
- magic;
- reputation;
- world-state change.

## 8.5 Phase 5 - Seasonal and Political Power

The player increasingly interacts with Seasonal Courts, major settlements, contested territories, living infrastructure, and authority-scale problems.

Progression may involve supporting, opposing, reconciling, bypassing, or transforming political powers rather than joining a predefined faction.

## 8.6 Phase 6 - Deep Verdant Systems

Later realm progression can involve:

- Seasonal Nexus content;
- Dreaming Root Depths;
- major living structures;
- advanced living construction;
- realm-scale restoration;
- authority encounters;
- advanced Covenant Portal infrastructure;
- cross-realm interactions;
- Void-corruption containment;
- Pocket Realm Verdant modules/imprints where unlocked by the broader game.

## 8.7 No Mandatory Boss-Kill Gate

Major authority progression must not assume that every authority is killed.

Authority outcomes may grant progression through:

- knowledge;
- permission;
- route access;
- political recognition;
- crafted proof;
- restoration;
- resource grants;
- negotiated agreements;
- containment;
- combat trophies where combat is the chosen resolution.

# 9. Difficulty, Simulation and Scalability

Verdant systems must preserve the realm fantasy across different world-simulation and hardware settings.

## 9.1 Simulation Scaling

Lower simulation settings may simplify:

- off-screen seasonal updates;
- ecological population detail;
- Court political simulation;
- living-river recalculation;
- background settlement behaviour;
- migration granularity;
- long-distance corruption propagation;
- ambient creature population modelling.

They must not remove the realm's identity or invalidate progression.

## 9.2 Deterministic Bounded Rules

Realm laws, living paths, seasonal transitions, dungeon reconfiguration, ecological states, and political consequences must use deterministic or bounded state models compatible with save/load, multiplayer authority, debugging, and reproducibility.

## 9.3 Feasibility Rule

If a planned Verdant mechanic proves computationally excessive, technically brittle, or disproportionately expensive to maintain, it may be simplified while preserving the intended fantasy.

The implementation should prefer robust authored systems over fragile simulation theatre.

---

# 10. Death, Respawn and Failure Ownership

The Verdant Covenant does **not** define a special death system.

Player death remains owned by Leyforge's universal death/recovery systems. Normal death returns the player through the configured respawn/recovery flow defined elsewhere in canon.

Verdant laws may affect local hazards, rescue conditions, safe zones, permissions, or recovery opportunities, but the realm must not override the universal death/respawn contract unless a separately approved global system explicitly exposes such an interface.

---

# 11. Corruption, Rot, Blight and Restoration Boundaries

## 11.1 Natural Rot

Decay, decomposition, fungal activity, and Rotwild ecology are natural parts of the realm.

Rot is not inherently evil and is not automatically Corruption.

## 11.2 Ecological Blight

Blight represents ecological disease, imbalance, harmful magical growth, invasive conditions, or damaged living systems.

Blight may be serious and dangerous while remaining independent from Void corruption.

## 11.3 Void Corruption

All genuine Corruption ultimately traces to the **Void Between**, even when local cultures misidentify its origin.

Void corruption can exploit already damaged or unstable ecosystems, but it is a separate phenomenon from natural rot and ordinary blight.

## 11.4 Material and Creature Transformation

Corruption is normally represented first as a state or overlay on an existing creature, material, block, biome, or structure.

A separate canonical definition is created only when corruption produces an irreversible transformation with sufficiently different physical properties, anatomy, behaviour, crafting role, or gameplay identity.

This rule prevents automatic `Corrupted X` duplication across the entire registry.

## 11.5 Restoration

Where appropriate, the realm supports:

- ecological repair;
- cleansing;
- regrowth;
- treaty repair;
- stabilisation;
- containment;
- habitat restoration;
- settlement recovery;
- authority restoration.

Not every damaged state must be permanently reversible, but recovery paths should exist where they strengthen gameplay and world continuity.

# 12. Civilisation and Sovereignty Principles

## 12.1 Verdant Pact

The **Verdant Pact** is an umbrella diplomatic framework, not a single unified civilisation.

Participation may include or exclude different:

- Seasonal Courts;
- river communities;
- Moonroot settlements;
- nomadic peoples;
- mortal diasporas;
- neutral enclaves;
- refugee groups;
- living settlements;
- disputed embassies.

Membership, recognition, hostility, neutrality, and treaty relationships may vary by generated world history and current state.

## 12.2 No Biological Monopoly

No ancestry or species inherently owns the realm.

Culture, citizenship, settlement, faction, ancestry, profession, and political allegiance remain separate data concepts.

## 12.3 Living Settlements

Verdant architecture may be:

- grown;
- crafted;
- assembled;
- imported;
- hybrid.

Living construction is culturally important but not mandatory for every settlement or object.

# 13. Harvesting and Ecological Ethics

Verdant content must support resource provenance and ecology rather than treating the realm as an unlimited fantasy-resource quarry.

Potential acquisition methods include:

- conventional harvesting;
- pruning;
- cultivation;
- shedding;
- moulting;
- abandoned nests;
- symbiotic collection;
- domestication;
- stewardship;
- trade;
- faction grants;
- dungeon salvage;
- restoration rewards;
- carefully controlled extraction.

Certain living materials may respond to age, ecological health, magical conditions, or recognised permission.

This is a gameplay and provenance rule, not a universal morality meter. Cultures and factions may disagree about acceptable extraction practices.

# 14. Cross-Realm Interfaces

## 14.1 Ancestral Veil

Moonroot regions may possess limited natural spirit resonance with the Ancestral Veil.

This does not give Verdant systems ownership over death, afterlife, Ancestral Veil ecology, or Veil travel.

## 14.2 Somnolent Expanse

The Dreaming Root Depths may under rare authored conditions connect or resonate with the Somnolent Expanse.

Such interaction must consume the Somnolent Expanse's dream-system interfaces rather than reimplement dream simulation inside FCC-02.

Normal interdimensional entry still follows the canonical portal rule.

## 14.3 Void Between

Void corruption may appear in Verdant regions through breaches, contaminated routes, imported material, creatures, or major events.

Verdant owns the local consequences and restoration content; the Void Between owns the ultimate corruption origin and its deeper cosmological truth.

## 14.4 Pocket Realms

Later Pocket Realm systems may consume Verdant-derived realm modules or imprints for growth, seasons, living ecology, or compatible environmental behaviours.

FCC-02 does not own Pocket Realm construction rules.

# 16. Block and Item Canon Boundary

The universal Leyforge single-definition rule applies throughout FCC-02.

If something is fundamentally a placeable block and, when broken, remains or recovers as that same block, it has **one canonical Block definition**.

Inventory representation for that block uses the generated/reference block-form projection.

A separately authored Item definition is created only when there is a genuinely distinct object, component, material, drop, processed form, tool, consumable, or other non-block identity.

FCC-02 must not create duplicate Item records for Verdant blocks merely because players can carry them.

---

# 17. Derived-Form Eligibility

No material automatically receives every block or item shape.

Derived forms are allowed only where the material's physical, cultural, magical, or functional properties justify them.

Examples of the governing principle:

- structural woods may support logs, planks, beams, stairs, slabs, doors, furniture, and selected mechanisms;
- flexible fibres may support thread, cord, cloth, lattice, bindings, armour components, and conduits;
- optical materials may support lenses, panes, inlays, devices, and selected decorative forms;
- fluids remain fluids, containers, reagents, catalysts, or processed products rather than arbitrary solid blocks;
- seeds remain seeds, growth anchors, biological components, or crafted devices unless a genuine processed construction material is defined;
- powders and salts become reagents, mixtures, mortar additives, ward components, or contained products rather than automatic masonry.

This principle is mandatory for later FCC-02 block/item/recipe documents.

---

# 18. Naming Convention

FCC-02 consumes the global Leyforge naming rule:

> **Use plain descriptive names for ordinary real-world materials and forms. Use distinctive fantasy names only for genuinely unique, magical, dimensional, or invented Leyforge substances, species, structures, and concepts. Derived forms use predictable naming.**

Avoid unnecessary layered common/scientific/cultural naming systems.

One clear canonical player-facing name is preferred per content concept unless a lore alias has an actual gameplay or narrative reason to exist.

# 19. Source Supersession and Reconciliation Rules

The following Verdant directions supersede older source assumptions where conflict exists:

1. **One canonical Covenant Portal** replaces multiple unrelated normal interdimensional access methods.
2. POC-specific gates, locations, sequences, and guaranteed routes remain retired.
3. Seasonal alignments contain multiple polities and cultures; they are not four universal monolithic factions.
4. Verdant Pact is an umbrella diplomatic framework, not one civilisation.
5. Covenant, Identity, Threshold, Resonance, Seasonal, and Territorial laws use explicit bounded gameplay states.
6. Natural rot, ecological blight, and Void corruption are distinct phenomena.
7. Genuine Corruption ultimately traces to the Void Between.
8. Realm authorities may have non-lethal and non-combat resolutions.
9. Realm access, death, movement, inventory, crafting, economy, combat, and other universal systems remain outside FCC-02 ownership.
10. Materials and creatures do not receive automatic corrupted duplicates.
11. Materials do not receive automatic shape permutations.
12. Exact content rosters in later FCC-02 documents supersede older Set 24 foundation counts where the newer roster deliberately expands or reconciles them.

---

# 20. Realm Progression Invariants

The following must remain true in every valid implementation:

- The Verdant Covenant is reachable through the Covenant Portal under valid world progression.
- A seed cannot make required first access permanently impossible.
- Portal destination logic cannot routinely place players into arbitrary unavoidable lethal terrain.
- The player is not forced into allegiance with a Seasonal Court to access the realm's fundamental content.
- Court politics, ecology, and realm laws provide multiple approaches where practical.
- Major authority progression does not require kill-only outcomes.
- Natural rot remains distinguishable from genuine Void corruption.
- Cross-realm mechanics consume the owning realm/system interfaces.
- Lower simulation settings preserve the same core progression and fantasy.
- No POC-specific content is required for production progression.
