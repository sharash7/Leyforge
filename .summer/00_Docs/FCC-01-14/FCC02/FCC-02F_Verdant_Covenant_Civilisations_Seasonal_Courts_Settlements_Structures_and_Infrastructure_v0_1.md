# 1. Purpose

FCC-02F defines the authoritative Verdant Covenant canon for:

- civilisation structure;
- Seasonal Court organisation;
- Verdant Pact participation and diplomacy;
- settlement archetypes;
- built and grown architecture;
- signature structures and realm landmarks;
- civic, agricultural, ecological, transport, storage, market, diplomatic, defensive, archival, ritual, and utility infrastructure;
- biome-specific settlement suitability;
- settlement-scale use of living materials and organisms;
- settlement relationships with Covenant, Threshold, Identity, Resonance, seasonal, ecological, blight, and corruption states;
- realm-local structure families and their relationship to older Atlas foundations;
- player integration into Verdant settlements and construction;
- deterministic settlement/world-generation constraints;
- scalable simulation requirements for low-end worlds;
- structure-state, provenance, registry, and art-handoff boundaries.

This document does **not** own:

- universal settlement simulation, NPC cognition, jobs, needs, schedules, economy, diplomacy, combat, movement, transport physics, or automation rules;
- the universal portal framework or cross-realm travel mechanics;
- exact block definitions, exact item definitions, recipe quantities, crafting stations, or stable-ID normalisation;
- dungeon layouts, dungeon guardians, bosses, or realm authorities;
- detailed event scheduling, corruption transformation chains, or final art/material profiles.

Those systems are consumed through their established interfaces and are owned by their respective Leyforge documents and later FCC documents.

The guiding rule is:

> **FCC-02F defines what Verdant civilisations build, grow, inhabit, maintain, contest, restore, trade through, and recognise. It does not create parallel versions of universal gameplay systems.**

# 2. Canon Statement

The Verdant Covenant is not governed by one people, one Court, one ancestry, one city, or one political system.

It contains a plural civilisation landscape of:

- Seasonal Courts;
- Verdant Pact enclaves;
- river communities;
- Moonroot communities;
- canopy settlements;
- mobile seasonal peoples;
- nomadic and semi-nomadic groups;
- mortal diasporas;
- Rootborn and other realm-adapted peoples;
- neutral enclaves;
- blight refugees;
- restoration communities;
- border settlements;
- independent estates;
- disputed embassy sites;
- living settlements with strong ecological integration;
- mixed-culture settlements;
- communities that reject Verdant Pact membership.

Civilisation identity is therefore generated from combinations of:

- culture;
- ancestry;
- citizenship;
- faction;
- seasonal alignment;
- settlement form;
- biome;
- political history;
- diplomatic relationships;
- economic role;
- magic/technology profile;
- ecological relationship;
- current world state.

No ancestry inherently owns the Verdant Covenant.

No Seasonal Court represents all inhabitants aligned to that season.

No Verdant settlement must use only living architecture.

No living settlement is automatically benevolent.

No conventional masonry settlement is automatically culturally foreign.

The core civilisation fantasy is:

> **Societies live inside an active ecology whose plants, seasons, rivers, territories, thresholds, and recognised agreements can all matter to public life.**

# 4. Verdant Civilisation Model

## 4.1 Verdant Pact

The **Verdant Pact** is an umbrella diplomatic framework.

It is not:

- a single empire;
- a racial identity;
- a universal fae government;
- a mandatory alliance;
- a single settlement network;
- a required player faction.

Potential members or recognised participants may include:

- Seasonal Courts;
- river communities;
- Moonroot settlements;
- canopy settlements;
- nomadic peoples;
- Rootborn communities;
- mortal diasporas;
- mixed settlements;
- restoration enclaves;
- neutral enclaves;
- refugee communities;
- guilds;
- diplomatic missions;
- individual sovereign settlements.

Participation may be:

- full;
- associate;
- treaty-bound;
- observer;
- neutral;
- suspended;
- contested;
- rejected.

World history may therefore generate meaningful differences between seeds without changing the canonical content roster.

## 4.2 Pact Functions

The Verdant Pact may support:

- inter-settlement diplomacy;
- ecological agreements;
- migration agreements;
- trade protocols;
- route maintenance;
- hospitality standards;
- neutral diplomatic sites;
- restoration cooperation;
- Court mediation;
- refugee passage;
- shared responses to blight or Void incursion;
- disputes over extraction;
- disputes over living infrastructure;
- recognition of thresholds, titles, routes, and settlement rights.

The Pact does not automatically control all such systems.

## 4.3 Pact Enclaves

**Verdant Pact Enclave** remains a canonical culture/settlement foundation inherited from the Atlas.

An enclave is a settlement or district organised around negotiated coexistence with living ecology and surrounding political powers.

Possible features include:

- grown structures;
- grafted bridges;
- seasonal rooms;
- pollen courts;
- treaty markers;
- public thresholds;
- mixed-material construction;
- seed libraries;
- ecological service spaces;
- embassy gardens;
- living water infrastructure.

An enclave may occur inside or outside direct Seasonal Court territory.

# 5. Seasonal Alignment and Seasonal Courts

## 5.1 Four Broad Alignments

The four canonical Seasonal alignments are:

1. **Spring**
2. **Summer**
3. **Autumn**
4. **Winter**

They are metaphysical, ecological, cultural, magical, and political alignments.

They are not four universal kingdoms.

## 5.2 Multiple Courts per Alignment

Each alignment may contain multiple:

- kingdoms;
- courts;
- tribes;
- city-states;
- estates;
- monasteries;
- communes;
- noble houses;
- guild powers;
- ritual authorities;
- independent settlements;
- rival claimants.

Two Spring Courts may be political enemies.

A Winter Court may ally with a Summer Court.

A settlement may recognise one Court ceremonially while trading with another.

## 5.3 Court Identity

A Court may be defined by combinations of:

- season;
- territory;
- ruler or council;
- recognised title;
- cultural history;
- ecological doctrine;
- preferred architecture;
- agricultural specialisation;
- magical tradition;
- diplomatic posture;
- legal custom;
- current generated political state.

## 5.4 Court Territory

Court territory may be expressed through:

- recognised boundaries;
- threshold structures;
- wardthorn lines;
- living roads;
- seasonal vegetation;
- public markers;
- patrol/guardian ecology;
- treaties;
- settlement allegiance;
- natural landmarks.

Territory does not require hard invisible walls.

## 5.5 Court Membership

Court citizenship or allegiance is distinct from ancestry.

Any sapient ancestry may potentially participate where culture and generated history support it.

## 5.6 Seasonal Regent

The **Seasonal Regent** is defined in FCC-02H as a realm authority-scale office/title.

FCC-02F only recognises that:

- the office can affect Court politics;
- settlements may recognise or reject a Regent;
- succession or challenge can produce realm-scale political consequences;
- public structures may host or respond to Regent-related state.

Boss/authority implementation remains outside this document.

# 6. Civilisation and Population Archetypes

The following are canonical population/civilisation archetypes.

They are not necessarily single factions.

## 6.1 Seasonal Court Societies

Court-aligned populations with formal seasonal identity.

Possible forms:

- court capitals;
- estates;
- villages;
- ritual communities;
- military/guardian outposts;
- market settlements;
- seasonal pilgrimage sites.

## 6.2 Mortal Diasporas

Peoples from other realms or the Overworld who have established long-term communities in the Verdant Covenant.

They may:

- retain original cultural practices;
- adapt to local Covenant law;
- adopt living architecture;
- maintain conventional architecture;
- form mixed communities;
- participate in the Verdant Pact;
- remain politically independent.

## 6.3 Living Settlements

Settlements where a significant part of the physical environment is actively grown or maintained as living infrastructure.

This is a settlement form, not a species.

## 6.4 Wandering Seasonal Peoples

Mobile or semi-mobile communities whose routes follow:

- seasonal transitions;
- pollinator migrations;
- herd movements;
- ritual calendars;
- trade circuits;
- Court gatherings;
- resource cycles.

## 6.5 Neutral Enclaves

Settlements or districts that deliberately maintain political neutrality.

They may specialise in:

- diplomacy;
- trade;
- mediation;
- refugee support;
- archives;
- travel services;
- restoration work.

## 6.6 Blight Refugees

Communities displaced by:

- ecological blight;
- failed harvests;
- territorial change;
- Court conflict;
- Void incursion;
- damaged living infrastructure.

They are not a permanent ancestry or culture category.

## 6.7 Restoration Enclaves

Communities organised around restoring damaged ecosystems or infrastructure.

Possible functions:

- seed preservation;
- soil repair;
- cleansing;
- river repair;
- habitat recovery;
- blight containment;
- wildlife rehabilitation;
- Court-neutral ecological work.

## 6.8 Disputed Embassy Sites

Diplomatic settlements or structures whose sovereignty, recognition, access, or treaty status is contested.

These are useful generators of political quests without requiring universal war.

- orcs;
- elven kindreds;
- Rootborn;
- Mycelians;

A person's:

- ancestry;
- culture;

# 8. Architecture Philosophy

Verdant architecture may be:

- **grown**;
- **crafted**;
- **assembled**;
- **carved**;
- **woven**;
- **cultivated**;
- **imported**;
- **hybrid**.

The realm must not be visually reduced to “everything is a tree house.”

## 8.1 Grown Architecture

Grown structures may use:

- Greatheart living forms;
- Living Heartwood;
- trained branches;
- root masses;
- Living Reeds;
- Wardthorn;
- Thornsilver Fibre;
- Bloomstone-supported growth;
- living conduit tissues;
- cultivated canopy platforms.

## 8.2 Crafted Architecture

Crafted structures may use:

- Greatheart Wood;
- Dawnwood;
- Bloomwood;
- Rootstone;
- processed Bloomstone;
- imported metal;
- glass;
- ceramic;
- textile;
- conventional universal construction materials.

## 8.3 Hybrid Architecture

Hybrid construction is canonical and common.

Examples:

- Rootstone foundations with living Greatheart frames;
- Dawnwood floors with Thornsilver tension supports;
- Bloomwood screens attached to living walls;
- imported iron fittings on Rootstone bridges;
- Bloomstone stabilisers embedded in grown conduit systems;
- conventional machines housed in living structures.

## 8.4 Architecture Must Follow Material Eligibility

FCC-02F does not author impossible material-shape combinations.

Construction eligibility follows FCC-02C and later FCC-02G/FCC-12.

Examples:

- Season Sap is not a wall block;
- Memory Seeds are not generic bricks;
- Pollen Amber is not mass structural masonry;
- Thornsilver Fibre is not a stone block substitute;
- Living Heartwood is not required for ordinary cheap furniture.

# 9. Settlement Growth Philosophy

Verdant settlements may grow physically and politically over time through universal settlement progression.

Growth can involve:

- new districts;
- cultivated bridges;
- expanded root platforms;
- additional markets;
- waterway works;
- agricultural terraces;
- living defence belts;
- public archives;
- Court buildings;
- Pact infrastructure;
- refugee districts;
- restoration districts;
- trade infrastructure;
- imported technology.

The realm does not require a second settlement-progression system.

Universal settlement needs remain authoritative.

Verdant settlements may satisfy those needs through realm-specific means.

For example:

- **Housing** may use grown chambers or crafted homes;
- **Provisions** may use gardens, orchards, crops, fishing, trade, and preserved food;
- **Health** may use medicinal gardens and universal healthcare systems;
- **Work** may use cultivation, crafting, trade, archives, restoration, Court service, or automation;
- **Safety** may use Wardthorn, patrols, walls, terrain, diplomacy, or universal defence systems;
- **Infrastructure** may use Rootways, bridges, waterworks, conventional roads, machines, or mixed systems;
- **Morale** may be influenced by festivals, seasonal spaces, culture, security, community, and universal social systems.

# 10. Settlement Archetype Registry

FCC-02F locks the following twenty-four principal settlement/site archetypes.

They define repeatable world-generation and authored-content families.

They are not twenty-four mandatory named cities.

## 10.1 Sunlit Canopy

### 10.1.1 Canopy Settlement

A settlement distributed across major branches, trunk platforms, suspended walkways, and vertical ecological layers.

Core functions may include:

- housing;
- fruit cultivation;
- canopy trade;
- pollinator management;
- glider access;
- vertical freight;
- branch-route maintenance.

### 10.1.2 Greatheart Enclave

A settlement closely integrated with one or more mature Greatheart Trees.

Potential features:

- living chambers;
- Rootstone foundation zones;
- stewardship spaces;
- controlled Living Heartwood use;
- branch bridges;
- ecological governance.

### 10.1.3 Embassy Platform

A high-access diplomatic settlement or district built to accommodate outsiders, traders, Court representatives, and neutral meetings.

It may include conventional architecture for visitors unfamiliar with living structures.

## 10.2 Moonroot Groves

### 10.2.1 Moonroot Shrine Village

A low-light settlement integrated with ancient roots, memory practices, Spirit Moss, and lunar ecology.

### 10.2.2 Oath Court

A civic/legal settlement or district where recognised agreements, mediation, titles, thresholds, and public records are central.

### 10.2.3 Memory Grove Settlement

A community organised around living archives, Memory Orchards, remembrance, ecological stewardship, and carefully maintained root networks.

## 10.3 Pollenwild Meadows

### 10.3.1 Seasonal Nomad Camp

A mobile settlement adapted to seasonal migration and rapidly changing flowerland conditions.

### 10.3.2 Mobile Market

A travelling market settlement that follows migration, harvest, festival, and Court routes.

### 10.3.3 Festival Ground

A recurring gathering site for seasonal celebrations, trade, Court diplomacy, contests, rituals, and migration rendezvous.

## 10.4 Living Riverlands

### 10.4.1 Reed Village

A wetland or river-edge settlement using Living Reeds, platforms, boats, light bridges, and water-sensitive construction.

### 10.4.2 River Court Settlement

A politically organised river community whose law, routes, trade, and public works interact with local river behaviour.

### 10.4.3 Living Lock / Waterway Station

A settlement-scale infrastructure node managing river transit, freight, irrigation, flood response, ecological passage, and route agreements.

It consumes universal waterway/maritime mechanics.

## 10.5 Thorn-Court Marches

### 10.5.1 Court Estate

A formal territorial residence or civic estate associated with a Court, house, claimant, or recognised authority.

### 10.5.2 Border Keep / Grove

A defended border settlement blending architecture, terrain, Wardthorn, patrols, and territorial law.

### 10.5.3 Treaty Outpost

A border-facing settlement for negotiation, inspection, trade, dispute resolution, safe passage, and neutral contact.

## 10.6 Rotwilds

### 10.6.1 Blight Camp

A temporary or semi-permanent operational settlement responding to ecological blight.

### 10.6.2 Quarantine Settlement

A settlement designed to contain biological, magical, or ecological hazards without assuming all rot is corruption.

### 10.6.3 Restoration Enclave

A long-term settlement specialising in damaged-land recovery, seed banks, cleansing, habitat restoration, and ecological research.

## 10.7 Seasonal Nexus

### 10.7.1 Court Summit Site

A major diplomatic gathering site suitable for multi-Court negotiations, succession politics, seasonal disputes, and interregional agreements.

### 10.7.2 Seasonal Ritual Complex

A public/ritual infrastructure family supporting seasonal alignment, convergence management, festivals, and large-scale environmental work.

### 10.7.3 Realm Route Hub

A major **intra-realm** transport and logistics node linking roads, Rootways, waterways, Court routes, and other local movement systems.

It is not an alternate interdimensional portal system.

## 10.8 Dreaming Root Depths

### 10.8.1 Hidden Root City

A concealed subterranean settlement built within colossal root architecture and natural cavities.

### 10.8.2 Rootway Station

A transit, freight, communication, rest, and maintenance node for living root-network infrastructure.

### 10.8.3 Dream-Root Sanctuary

A protected Root Depth site for sleep, dream-resonance study, navigation recovery, ecological research, and rare Somnolent-related narrative content.

It does not provide normal interdimensional travel.

# 12. Signature Structure Registry

FCC-02F locks seven principal signature realm structures/projects.

## 12.1 Arrival Covenant Sanctuary

**Class:** Arrival / diplomacy / safety / orientation structure

- local orientation;
- Covenant-law explanation;
- diplomatic contact;
- trade services;

Variants may differ by:
