# 2. Canon Statement

The Verdant Covenant is botanically rich, but its final content catalogue must remain deliberate and production-manageable.

The realm therefore distinguishes between:

1. **canonical gameplay-bearing flora**, which receive named registry identities and defined uses;
2. **ambient ecological flora**, which provide biodiversity and visual variation without each becoming a separate gameplay object;
3. **procedural state variation**, which changes appearance, yield, season, health, age, corruption, or habitat without multiplying the underlying species definition.

The guiding rule is:

> **Botanical diversity may be visually broad, while the gameplay-bearing plant catalogue remains bounded, named, and mechanically justified.**

No future content pass should inflate the roster merely because a biome could theoretically support another flower, grass, fern, moss, or fungus.

New named plant families require a distinct gameplay, ecological, cultural, material, food, or progression role.

# 4. Universal Botanical Naming Rule

FCC-02D consumes the universal Leyforge naming convention locked in FCC-02A:

- ordinary real-world concepts use clear descriptive names;
- genuinely unique Leyforge species or substances may use distinctive fantasy names;
- derived forms retain predictable names;
- unnecessary scientific/common/cultural duplicate naming layers are avoided.

Examples:

- Greatheart Tree -> Greatheart Log -> Greatheart Wood / Greatheart Planks / Greatheart Beam;
- Dawnwood Tree -> Dawnwood Log -> Dawnwood Planks;
- Bloomwood Tree -> Bloomwood Log -> Bloomwood Planks;
- Thornsilver Vine -> Thornsilver Fibre;
- Living Reed -> Living Reed Bundle or processed reed products where admitted by FCC-02G.

Lore aliases may exist in authored dialogue, but the registry and player-facing core catalogue retain one canonical name per content identity unless a strong gameplay reason requires otherwise.

# 5. Greatheart Tree

## 5.1 Canonical Identity

The **Greatheart Tree** is the colossal structural tree of the Sunlit Canopy and one of the defining organisms of the Verdant Covenant.

It is characterised by:

- immense age potential;
- extreme trunk and root scale;
- broad structural branches capable of supporting settlements, bridges, grown architecture, and layered ecosystems;
- high-value durable ordinary timber;
- the conditional development of **Living Heartwood** within qualifying mature individuals;
- ecological importance far beyond its value as a resource source.

The Greatheart Tree is not merely a large lumber tree. It is a habitat-forming organism and structural pillar of Sunlit Canopy ecology.

## 5.2 Canonical Material Outputs

The tree may source:

- Greatheart Bark;
- Greatheart Log;
- Greatheart Wood;
- Greatheart timber derivatives owned by FCC-02C/G;
- rare conditional **Living Heartwood**;
- approved seeds, shoots, leaves, resins, or minor reagents where recipes require them.

Ordinary Greatheart Wood and Living Heartwood are distinct material identities.

## 5.3 Living Heartwood Formation

Living Heartwood is not guaranteed in equal quantity in every Greatheart Tree.

Its formation depends on a combination of:

- age;
- health;
- ecological stability;
- magical/environmental conditions;
- sustained living integrity;
- local seasonal history;
- significant stewardship or realm-state conditions where appropriate.

Young, damaged, diseased, poorly situated, or aggressively exploited Greatheart Trees may contain little or no true Living Heartwood.

Ancient thriving individuals may support meaningful Living Heartwood cores.

## 5.4 Harvesting

Ordinary Greatheart timber may be obtained through normal forestry where local law, ownership, and ecological conditions allow.

High-value Living Heartwood harvesting must preserve the FCC-02A harvesting philosophy:

- destructive extraction is possible but may carry ecological, political, legal, or resource-regeneration consequences;
- stewardship, selective harvesting, natural shedding, cultivated growth, negotiated access, or consent-style interactions may provide superior outcomes;
- the game does not require every player to behave altruistically, but ecological consequence must be represented rather than ignored.

## 5.5 Living Construction

Greatheart material can exist in at least two broad post-harvest states:

1. **ordinary processed Greatheart timber**, which is stable and largely inert;
2. **living-preserved material**, produced only through specialist harvesting/processing that retains or supports biological activity.

Living Heartwood remains the premium specialist material for genuinely living structural systems.

## 5.6 Reproduction and World Generation

Greatheart Trees may generate as:

- ancient mature giants;
- younger canopy trees;
- groves;
- culturally protected individuals;
- settlement-integrated trees;
- damaged or recovering specimens;
- rare blighted or Void-corrupted specimens subject to FCC-02I.

World generation must not place critical progression behind the destruction of a unique irreplaceable Greatheart individual.

# 6. Dawnwood Tree

## 6.1 Canonical Identity

The **Dawnwood Tree** is the principal sun-bearing upper-canopy tree associated with:

- strong sunlight;
- upper-canopy and emergent-crown ecology;
- fruit production;
- sap collection;
- radiant or light-responsive botanical outputs;
- medium-strength general-purpose timber.

It is especially prominent in the Sunlit Canopy and may appear in compatible Pollenwild, Seasonal Nexus, settlement, or cultivated regions.

## 6.2 Material Outputs

Canonical outputs may include:

- Dawnwood Bark;
- Dawnwood Log;
- Dawnwood timber derivatives;
- **Dawnfruit** as a canonical wild/managed food family;
- Season Sap where the tree is in a valid charged state;
- flowers, nectar, leaves, or minor reagents where specifically required.

## 6.3 Dawnfruit

**Dawnfruit** is the canonical fruit family associated with Dawnwood ecology.

It may support:

- direct food use;
- preserved foods;
- beverages;
- cooking;
- trade;
- alchemical or seasonal recipes where justified.

Dawnfruit may express controlled seasonal, ripeness, quality, or biome-source variation without becoming separate species definitions for every colour or season.

## 6.4 Timber Role

Dawnwood is:

- less structurally dominant than Greatheart Wood;
- stronger and more general-purpose than highly flexible/decorative Bloomwood in many construction contexts;
- particularly suited to light-responsive, magical, decorative, or upper-canopy applications where later systems require them.

Exact mechanical statistics remain owned by the final material/equipment and block registries.

# 7. Bloomwood Tree

## 7.1 Canonical Identity

The **Bloomwood Tree** is the flowering major tree family associated with:

- abundant blossoms;
- pollen;
- nectar;
- seasonal colour;
- fine/flexible timber;
- pollinator ecology;
- **Pollen Amber** formation.

It is common in or near Pollenwild Meadows, flowering Sunlit Canopy regions, cultivated groves, seasonal settlements, and compatible Court territories.

## 7.2 Material Outputs

Canonical outputs may include:

- Bloomwood Bark;
- Bloomwood Log;
- Bloomwood timber derivatives;
- resin;
- flowers;
- pollen;
- nectar;
- Pollen Amber through the ecological formation process defined in FCC-02C;
- approved seeds or botanical reagents.

## 7.3 Timber Role

Bloomwood is canonically:

- lighter than Greatheart timber;
- flexible;
- well suited to decorative and fine construction;
- suitable for selected furniture, panels, trim, frames, instruments, light structural work, and specialist crafted components where admitted by FCC-02G.

It is not limited to decoration, but it should not replace Greatheart as the realm's strongest ordinary timber.

## 7.4 Pollen Amber Relationship

Pollen Amber forms when compatible botanical resin and pollen combine and harden under valid ecological conditions.

Bloomwood is the primary canonical plant source supporting that formation.

Pollen Amber therefore belongs to an ecosystem that includes:

- Bloomwood resin production;
- flowering cycles;
- pollinator activity;
- seasonal state;
- environmental quality;
- collection and later processing.

The material is not generated as arbitrary mineable amber ore.

# 9. Thornsilver Vine

## 9.1 Identity

**Thornsilver Vine** is a climbing biological vine whose mature fibres can develop the characteristic flexible metallic properties of **Thornsilver Fibre**.

It commonly grows:

- around supporting trees;
- along living structures;
- across Thorn-Court and Pollenwild ecotones;
- in managed cultivation sites;
- where its pollinator/weaver symbiosis can be sustained.

## 9.2 Thornsilver Weaver Interface

The Thornsilver Vine participates in a required symbiosis with the **Thornsilver Weaver**, defined as a creature in FCC-02E.

The Weaver may contribute to:

- pollination;
- fibre separation or drawing;
- growth health;
- reproductive cycling;
- naturally shed usable fibre;
- colony or nest ecology.

FCC-02D owns the plant side of this relationship; FCC-02E owns creature behaviour and creature states.

## 9.3 Harvesting

Thornsilver Fibre should support non-lethal acquisition through:

- mature vine harvesting;
- pruned growth;
- shed woven fibre;
- managed symbiosis;
- cultivated production.

Killing Thornsilver Weavers is not required for normal Thornsilver progression.

# 30. Season Sap Botanical Ecology

## 30.1 Canonical Source Rule

**Season Sap** is not owned by one tree species.

It is a realm-wide magical botanical phenomenon expressed through approved vegetation under valid ecological conditions.

Potential sources include:

- Greatheart Trees;
- Dawnwood Trees;
- Bloomwood Trees;
- Seasonfern;
- selected mature vines or roots;
- specialised seasonal groves;
- other approved plants where explicit FCC content later requires them.

## 30.2 State Model

Season Sap remains one canonical material identity carrying state such as:

- Spring;
- Summer;
- Autumn;
- Winter;
- rare mixed/convergence state;
- source;
- purity;
- stability;
- resonance where required.

Terms such as Moon Sap or Dream Sap refer to source/resonance states of Season Sap, not separate material families.

## 30.3 Sustainable Collection

Collection methods may include:

- tapping;
- pruning;
- collection from naturally shed growth;
- specialist cultivated channels;
- event-based gathering;
- managed groves.

Aggressive extraction may reduce plant health, yield, Living Heartwood formation potential, or settlement/ecological standing.

# 31. Memory Seed Botanical Ecology

## 31.1 Canonical Source Rule

**Memory Seeds** are not restricted to one dedicated tree species.

Multiple approved Verdant plants may form Memory Seeds under special biological/magical conditions.

This avoids introducing a redundant "Memory Tree" timber family solely to justify the resource.

## 31.2 Formation Conditions

Memory Seed formation may depend on:

- mature plant health;
- exposure to significant events or sustained conditions;
- deliberate cultivation;
- ritual or specialist horticulture;
- local memory/resonance states;
- suitable season;
- compatible species.

## 31.3 Intentional Encoding

Skilled cultivation may intentionally encode Memory Seeds with:

- plans;
- environmental states;
- selected experiences;
- restoration patterns;
- living architecture templates;
- approved memory information.

Recording living beings or sensitive identity/memory content must obey the consent and identity boundaries defined in FCC-02A and universal systems.

## 31.4 Growth Expression

A Memory Seed may, under supported conditions, help express stored information into:

- living forms;
- architectural growth patterns;
- restoration templates;
- ecological reconstruction;
- specialist devices.

It must not function as unrestricted matter duplication or arbitrary memory resurrection.

# 32. Pollen Amber Ecology

Pollen Amber requires a real botanical production chain rather than abstract loot generation.

The canonical chain is:

1. compatible flowering vegetation produces pollen;
2. Bloomwood and approved resin-producing plants supply resin;
3. pollinators distribute, collect, or concentrate pollen;
4. resin and pollen combine under valid environmental conditions;
5. the mixture naturally hardens into Pollen Amber deposits or collectable growths;
6. later processing produces lenses, inlays, plates, or other eligible products.

Source plant and season may affect tint/state while the material remains one canonical Pollen Amber identity.

Pollen Amber must not be transformed into a generic gemstone mined from unrelated stone veins.

# 34. Botanical Season-State Model

Plants may react to the four broad seasonal states without multiplying registry entries.

Permitted seasonal effects include:

- foliage colour;
- flowers;
- fruiting;
- growth rate;
- dormancy;
- sap state;
- pollen quantity;
- yield;
- medicine/reagent potency;
- defensive behaviour;
- habitat suitability.

A single plant definition may therefore support multiple seasonal presentations.

Season state must be:

- serialisable;
- deterministic within world-state rules;
- compatible with low-end simulation settings;
- readable to players where gameplay-relevant.

# 44. Natural Rot, Blight, and Void Corruption

FCC-02D consumes the separation established in FCC-02A/B/C.

## 44.1 Natural Rot

Natural rot includes:

- fallen leaves;
- deadwood;
- fungal decomposition;
- seasonal dieback;
- ordinary decay.

It is a necessary ecological function.

## 44.2 Ecological Blight

Blight is a harmful ecological imbalance or disease state.

It may result from:

- poor soil;
- disrupted water;
- overharvest;
- invasive growth;
- magical imbalance;
- pollution;
- failed restoration;
- hostile events.

It is not automatically Void-derived.

## 44.3 Void Corruption

Void corruption is contamination originating through the Void-corruption lineage defined by broader canon.

It may rewrite:

- plant anatomy;
- growth pattern;
- material identity;
- behaviour;
- reproduction;
- environmental interaction.

Minor contamination is normally a state. Irreversible transformation may justify a distinct content definition only under the admission rules of FCC-02I/J.
