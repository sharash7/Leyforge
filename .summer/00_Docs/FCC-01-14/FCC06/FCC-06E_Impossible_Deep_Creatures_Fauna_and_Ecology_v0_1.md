# 1. Purpose

FCC-06E defines the authoritative creature, fauna and animal-ecology canon for the **Impossible Deep**.

It owns the realm-local definition of:

- the twelve principal creature families;
- ecological roles and food-web relationships;
- biome affinities;
- locomotion and environmental adaptation;
- temperament and encounter framing;
- grazing, predation, scavenging and filter-feeding;
- Coreworm ecosystem engineering;
- Pressure Pearl creature provenance;
- creature-derived material boundaries;
- husbandry and aquaculture compatibility;
- regional-state and event responses;
- giant/ancient/exceptional-individual rules;
- variable titan/world-scale encounter boundaries;
- cross-realm fauna export suitability;
- creature registry and art-handoff requirements.

FCC-06E does **not** finalise:

- exact health values;
- exact damage values;
- exact AI state-machine implementation;
- exact spawn density;
- exact breeding timers;
- exact taming percentages;
- exact loot tables;
- exact drop quantities;
- exact boss stats;
- final creature models;
- final animation timing;
- final colour palettes;
- final sound design;
- final stable IDs for every creature unless already established elsewhere.

Those belong to later implementation, art, registry and global-reconciliation documents.

# 2. Canon Statement

Impossible Deep fauna is a functioning ecology rather than a list of hostile cave monsters.

The realm's creature design must support:

- producer-consumer relationships;
- scavenging;
- filter feeding;
- grazing;
- predation;
- decomposition;
- migration;
- ecosystem engineering;
- warning behaviour;
- symbiosis;
- husbandry;
- civilisation interaction;
- regional-state response.

Many creatures are dangerous under the wrong conditions, but **hostility toward the player is not their sole ecological purpose**.

# 5. Pressure Crab

## 5.1 Identity

The **Pressure Crab** is a heavily armoured scavenger adapted to compressed environments, vent margins and pressure-variable habitats.

It is not the primary source of Pressure Pearls.

## 5.2 Ecology

Pressure Crabs may feed on:

- carrion;
- discarded mollusc tissue;
- fungal matter;
- mineral-encrusted detritus;
- vent organisms;
- small invertebrates.

They can help clean:

- Pressure Garden basins;
- aquaculture systems;
- vent margins;
- settlement waste zones.

## 5.3 Behaviour

Typical behaviour is:

- defensive;
- opportunistic;
- territorial around food;
- non-hostile until approached or threatened.

Large individuals may be dangerous because of:

- crushing claws;
- heavy shells;
- sudden defensive charges;
- pressure-adapted strength.

## 5.4 Material Provenance

Potential creature-derived materials include:

- **Pressure Shell**;
- meat where culturally appropriate;
- chitinous sealing components.

**Pressure Pearl does not come from Pressure Crabs.**

That provenance is owned primarily by Vault Molluscs and related filter fauna.

# 6. Coreworm

## 6.1 Identity

The **Coreworm** is a megafaunal burrowing creature capable of tunnelling through hot or deeply compressed rock.

It is one of the defining ecosystem-engineer species of Impossible Deep.

## 6.2 Ecosystem Engineering

Coreworm tunnels may:

- create new regional routes;
- expose ore deposits;
- drain or redirect fluids;
- destabilise old tunnels;
- connect sealed caverns;
- reveal fossil districts;
- open migration corridors;
- damage settlements;
- become trade infrastructure after stabilisation.

The creature therefore changes the physical world around it.

## 6.3 Seismic Sensitivity

Coreworms sense and respond to:

- vibration;
- mining;
- machinery;
- seismic events;
- resonance signals.

Some cultures may redirect Coreworms using:

- controlled resonance;
- lure systems;
- vibration barriers;
- food;
- known migration patterns.

## 6.4 Encounter Philosophy

A Coreworm encounter does not automatically require combat.

Possible player/civilisation responses include:

- divert;
- lure;
- feed;
- study;
- protect;
- exploit a tunnel after passage;
- drive away;
- kill as a last resort.

## 6.5 Exceptional Individuals

**Coreworm remains a creature family.**

The old Coreworm boss hook is reconciled as:

> exceptional ancient, colossal or unusually influential Coreworm individuals.

Such an individual may become:

- a major encounter;
- a regional crisis;
- a settlement threat;
- a route-creation opportunity.

This does not create a second boss-only Coreworm species.

# 7. Fossil Grazer

## 7.1 Identity

The **Fossil Grazer** is a browser adapted to Fossil Metropolises and ancient mineralised ecologies.

It feeds on combinations of:

- Chasm/Fossil lichens;
- fungal growth;
- spore colonies;
- mineral-organic films;
- soft growth within fossil matrices.

## 7.2 Ecological Role

Fossil Grazers help:

- spread spores;
- move dormant micro-ecologies;
- expose old surfaces;
- maintain open feeding paths;
- connect living ecology to ancient fossil environments.

## 7.3 Relationship to Fossil Spore Colonies

Fossil Grazers may act as dispersal vectors for:

- viable spores;
- fungal fragments;
- microbial communities.

This does not mean they recreate entire extinct ecosystems automatically.

## 7.4 Behaviour

They are primarily grazers, not apex predators.

Threat behaviour may include:

- herd defence;
- charging;
- territorial response around feeding grounds;
- panic stampedes during seismic events.

# 8. Blackwater Lantern

## 8.1 Identity

The **Blackwater Lantern** is a bioluminescent ambush predator of the Blackwater Vault-Seas.

Its most important ecological/encounter identity is **false-route illumination**.

## 8.2 Lure Behaviour

Blackwater Lanterns may imitate or exploit:

- settlement lights;
- route beacons;
- bioluminescent navigation patterns;
- prey-attracting light sequences.

This makes them dangerous to:

- swimmers;
- divers;
- submersible crews;
- migrating fauna.

## 8.3 Ecological Role

They prey on:

- smaller fish;
- aquatic invertebrates;
- juvenile fauna;
- weak or isolated prey.

They are not magical evil entities merely because they lure travellers.

## 8.4 Readability

Experienced travellers may learn to distinguish:

- real route lights;
- settlement signals;
- Blackwater Lantern patterns.

Deep cultures may develop:

- signal codes;
- beacon colours/patterns;
- sonar/resonance confirmation;
- escort practices.

Final visual coding belongs to later art documents.

# 9. Seismic Caller

## 9.1 Identity

The **Seismic Caller** is a vibration-based territorial creature of Seismic Forests and tectonically active regions.

It communicates through low-frequency resonance transmitted through:

- stone;
- roots;
- Resonance Trees;
- cavern structures.

## 9.2 Ecology

Seismic Callers may use calls for:

- territory;
- mating;
- herd/colony coordination;
- alarm;
- migration;
- quake response.

## 9.3 Earthquake-Warning Role

Their behaviour may change before major seismic events.

Deep societies may read:

- call frequency;
- movement;
- silence;
- colony synchronisation

as part of earthquake warning.

They are not perfectly accurate supernatural predictors.

## 9.4 Dangerous Resonance

Exceptional calls may:

- disturb loose stone;
- trigger local defensive flora;
- frighten prey;
- amplify unstable terrain.

They do not automatically cause continent-scale earthquakes.

## 9.5 Seismic World-Beast Relationship

An exceptional ancient Seismic Caller lineage may produce a **Seismic World-Beast** encounter.

However:

- Seismic World-Beast is a variable encounter-scale category;
- it is not synonymous with Seismic Caller;
- other colossal native species may also fill that encounter category.

# 10. Vault Mollusc

## 10.1 Identity

The **Vault Mollusc** is a large pressure-adapted filter feeder associated with:

- Pressure Gardens;
- Blackwater margins;
- sealed aquatic habitats;
- mineral-rich basins.

It is the principal canonical Pressure Pearl producer.

## 10.2 Pressure Pearl Provenance

Vault Molluscs may create Pressure Pearls through long-term biomineral growth.

Pearls may be:

- shed;
- recovered from abandoned shells;
- cultivated through aquaculture;
- harvested from dead individuals;
- obtained through hunting where allowed.

Pressure Pearl material canon remains owned by FCC-06C.

## 10.3 Ecology

Vault Molluscs filter:

- suspended nutrients;
- Blackwater Mat fragments;
- microbes;
- mineral-organic particles.

They may improve water quality in managed habitats.

## 10.4 Husbandry

Pressure Communities and Blackwater settlements may raise Vault Molluscs for:

- food;
- filtration;
- Pressure Pearls;
- shell material;
- cultural purposes.

Husbandry requires suitable:

- pressure;
- chemistry;
- water flow;
- food availability.

# 11. Invertwing

## 11.1 Identity

The **Invertwing** is a gliding animal adapted to Inverted Caverns and changing local gravity orientation.

## 11.2 Locomotion

Invertwings exploit:

- gravity direction;
- air currents;
- chamber geometry;
- gravity-shear boundaries.

They may:

- glide between ceiling forests;
- launch from underside cliffs;
- transition across known gravity zones.

## 11.3 Environmental Readability

Their flight behaviour can help telegraph:

- gravity direction;
- stable transition routes;
- changing air currents.

## 11.4 Ecology

Invertwings may feed on:

- Ceiling Fern growth;
- insects/microfauna;
- Quakefruit-like produce where present;
- fungal fruiting bodies.

Some ecotypes may be more omnivorous.

# 12. Stoneburrower

## 12.1 Identity

The **Stoneburrower** is a small-to-medium tunnelling creature associated with Seismic Forests and mineral-rich Pressure Stone.

## 12.2 Ecology

Stoneburrowers may feed on:

- mineral growths;
- fungi;
- roots;
- small invertebrates.

They create small tunnels that can:

- aerate substrate;
- redistribute spores;
- expose mineral surfaces;
- create microhabitats.

## 12.3 Civilisation Interaction

They can be:

- agricultural pests;
- useful soil/substrate engineers;
- hunted food;
- monitored as tremor indicators.

They should not be designed purely as disposable hostile mobs.

The **Emberback** is a heat-adapted scavenger/grazer of Emberbone Forges and other warm Deep environments.

## 14.2 Adaptations

Emberbacks may possess:

- insulating mineral plates;
- heat-resistant tissue;

- behaviours that avoid lethal thermal zones.

They may feed on:

- mineral plates;

# 15. Gravity Skimmer

## 15.1 Identity

The **Gravity Skimmer** is anomaly-adapted fauna of Gravity Wells.

It navigates between local gravity centres using gravity gradients rather than ordinary horizontal locomotion.

## 15.2 Locomotion

Gravity Skimmers may:

- drift;
- glide;
- launch;
- orbit;
- change body orientation;
- exploit low-gravity corridors.

Their movement must obey established regional gravity profiles rather than arbitrary animation-only flight.

## 15.3 Ecology

They may feed on:

- suspended microorganisms;
- floating plant/fungal matter;
- small anomaly fauna;
- mineral-organic particles.

## 15.4 Civilisation Interaction

Gravity Skimmer behaviour may help explorers identify:

- stable gravity lanes;
- dangerous shear boundaries;
- active anomaly centres.

They may also interfere with:

- research equipment;
- suspended farms;
- gravity machinery.

# 16. Chasm Strider

## 16.1 Identity

The **Chasm Strider** is an extreme-environment creature adapted to Coreward Chasms.

It is built around survival in:

- severe heat;
- high pressure;
- unstable footing;
- seismic disturbance;
- low-resource habitats.

## 16.2 Ecology

Chasm Striders may be:

- omnivorous;
- scavenging;
- opportunistic grazers.

Likely foods include:

- Chasm Lichen;
- Vent Bloom;
- carrion;
- exposed microbial growth.

## 16.3 Movement

They are adapted to:

- broken ledges;
- unstable rock;
- narrow fractures;
- changing terrain.

They do not ignore gravity or collapse hazards completely.

## 16.4 Expedition Interaction

Coreward explorers may:

- observe Strider routes;
- use their movement to infer safe terrain;
- hunt them;
- avoid territorial groups;
- learn from migration behaviour.

# 18. Predator / Prey Balance

Impossible Deep must not treat all creatures as aggressive toward the player.

Creature aggression can depend on:

- hunger;
- territory;
- nesting;
- injury;
- migration;
- pressure state;
- seismic state;
- corruption;
- civilisation activity;
- provocation.

A peaceful grazer may become dangerous during:

- panic;
- migration;
- habitat collapse.

A predator may avoid a prepared settlement.

---

# 19. Creature Response to Pressure

Different fauna may have:

- narrow pressure tolerance;
- broad tolerance;
- life-stage-specific tolerance;
- habitat-dependent tolerance.

Pressure changes can affect:

- migration;
- reproduction;
- feeding;
- aggression;
- mortality.

Pressure adaptation is ecological rather than a universal “Deep creature immunity.”

---

# 20. Creature Response to Gravity

Gravity-specialist fauna include:

- Invertwing;
- Gravity Skimmer.

Other creatures may still react to Gravity Drift.

Possible responses include:

- sheltering;
- migration;
- disorientation;
- changed feeding;
- panic.

A creature's local adaptation does not grant arbitrary universal gravity immunity.

---

# 21. Creature Response to Seismic Activity

Seismic events may influence:

- Coreworm migration;
- Seismic Caller behaviour;
- Stoneburrower activity;
- Fossil Grazer movement;
- Emberback migration;
- Chasm Strider routes.

These reactions help make seismic change legible through ecology.

---

# 22. Creature Response to Blackwater States

Blackwater events may alter:

- Brineback migration;
- Vault Mollusc filtration;
- Blackwater Lantern hunting;
- juvenile habitat;
- Pressure Crab scavenging.

Blackwater Mineral Storms and Upwellings can therefore create ecological as well as traversal consequences.

---

# 23. Creature Response to Stoneflow

Stoneflow may:

- displace burrowers;
- expose new food;
- bury nests;
- change Coreworm routes;
- isolate populations.

Stoneflow does not automatically kill all creatures in its region.

Native fauna may have adaptive behaviour.

---

# 24. Creature Response to Fold Shift

Fold Shift can alter:

- migration routes;
- predator/prey contact;
- access to feeding grounds;
- settlement encounters.

Fauna may discover new route connections before civilisations map them.

This can create useful ecological clues.

---

# 25. Creature Response to Void Corruption

Ordinary Deep fauna are **not** Void-corrupted by default.

Void corruption requires genuine Void provenance.

Corrupted fauna may exhibit:

- behavioural alteration;
- physical mutation;
- ecological disruption;
- altered reproduction;
- hostile anomaly behaviour.

Corrupted forms remain states/variants unless irreversible transformation justifies a distinct entity definition.

---

# 26. Husbandry & Aquaculture

Impossible Deep supports creature husbandry where ecology and culture make sense.

Potentially manageable fauna include:

- Vault Molluscs;
- Brinebacks;
- Pressure Crabs;
- selected small fauna.

Not every creature is tameable.

Coreworms, Seismic Callers, Blackwater Lanterns, Gravity Skimmers or Chasm Striders may require specialised authored systems before any domestication claim is made.

---

# 27. Pressure Pearl Aquaculture

Pressure Pearl production should favour ecological systems such as:

- Vault Mollusc cultivation;
- managed Blackwater/Pressure Garden basins;
- water-quality control;
- feeding;
- pressure maintenance.

This prevents Pressure Pearl from becoming a random generic mob drop.

# 28. Creature-Derived Material Governance

Creature materials must have explicit provenance.

Examples:

- Pressure Crab → Pressure Shell;
- Vault Mollusc → Pressure Pearl / shell / food;
- Brineback → oils/hide/meat where appropriate;
- Emberback → heat-resistant hide/plates;
- Fossil Grazer → hide/bone/food where culturally appropriate.

FCC-06C owns established material families.

FCC-06E owns creature provenance.

FCC-06G/FCC-13 own final Item definitions and recipes.

---

# 29. No Mandatory Creature Exploitation

Progression must not require exterminating a species merely to access its ecological resource where alternate provenance is reasonable.

Examples:

- Pressure Pearls may come from aquaculture or abandoned shells;
- route knowledge may come from observation;
- Coreworm tunnels may be used without killing the Coreworm.

Some cultures may still hunt or farm creatures.

That is authored civilisation content rather than a universal moral law.

---

# 30. Exceptional Individuals

Any principal creature family may produce:

- old individuals;
- unusually large individuals;
- named individuals;
- territorial leaders;
- rare ecotypes.

However, **exceptional does not automatically mean boss**.

Boss/authority classification belongs to FCC-06H.

# 31. Titan-Scale Boundary

Impossible Deep permits extraordinarily large fauna.

This does not mean:

- every species scales infinitely;
- every creature has a titan variant;
- titan size automatically grants Realm Authority status.

Variable titan/world-scale encounter families are authored separately.

The established **Seismic World-Beast** category may draw from an exceptional native lineage without becoming a normal fauna registry entry.

# 40. Route Ecology

Fauna may interact with routes in specific ways.

Examples:

- Coreworm tunnels become roads;
- Invertwings mark gravity-safe air paths;
- Chasm Striders reveal stable ledges;
- Seismic Caller behaviour warns of unstable regions;
- Brineback migration affects sea routes;
- Blackwater Lanterns create false navigation cues.

This makes ecology part of exploration.

---

# 41. Dungeon Ecology

Dungeon families may contain native fauna where provenance supports it.

Examples:

- Pressure Vault → Pressure Crabs / Vault Molluscs;
- Inverted Mine → Invertwings;
- Fossil City → Fossil Grazers / Stoneburrowers;
- Seismic Forge → Emberbacks / Seismic Callers;
- Blackwater Citadel → aquatic fauna.

Guardians are not automatically the same as native fauna.

---

# 42. Creature-Hazard Separation

A creature may use a hazard without owning the hazard system.

Examples:

- Blackwater Lantern uses darkness;
- Seismic Caller uses vibration;
- Gravity Skimmer uses gravity;
- Emberback survives heat;
- Coreworm creates collapse risk.

Universal/environmental hazard systems remain authoritative.

---

# 43. Creature Progression Philosophy

Fauna difficulty can increase with:

- biome extremity;
- regional states;
- age;
- size;
- corruption;
- scarcity;
- territory.

But the realm does not require a strict linear creature ladder.

A Pressure Crab in a dangerous pressure basin may be more threatening than a nominally “later” creature in stable habitat.

---

# 44. Cross-Realm Export Suitability

Living Impossible Deep fauna use bounded compatibility states:

- compatible;
- managed habitat required;
- stressed;
- dormant;
- loses specialist capability;
- invasive;
- cannot survive.

Examples:

- Pressure Crab may require compatible pressure/humidity;
- Vault Mollusc requires water chemistry and pressure;
- Invertwing may struggle without suitable gravity/airspace;
- Gravity Skimmer may lose core locomotion outside anomaly conditions;
- Emberback requires tolerable thermal habitat;
- Chasm Strider may survive outside Coreward conditions but lose its ecological niche.

No creature is arbitrarily destroyed just because it crosses a Deepgate.

---

# 45. Invasive-Ecology Boundary

Exported fauna may become invasive if:

- habitat is compatible;
- predators are absent;
- food is abundant;
- reproduction succeeds.

Invasive behaviour is not automatically assigned to all Deep species.

It should be authored where ecologically plausible.

---

Impossible Deep fauna may appear in Overworld-controlled settings through:

# 48. Ancestral Veil Interface

Fossil remains and living Deep fauna remain physically distinct from spirit ecology.

A fossil is not automatically a soul.

A living Deep creature is not automatically spiritually bound to the Ancestral Veil.

Authored historical/spiritual links remain possible.

# 49. Somnolent Expanse Interface

Deep creatures may appear in:

- dreams;
- cultural symbolism;
- prophecy;
- nightmares.

Dream manifestations are not evidence that the physical species originates in the Somnolent Expanse.

---

# 50. Ascendant Reach Interface

Gravity-adapted fauna may support comparative study between:

- Gravity Wells;
