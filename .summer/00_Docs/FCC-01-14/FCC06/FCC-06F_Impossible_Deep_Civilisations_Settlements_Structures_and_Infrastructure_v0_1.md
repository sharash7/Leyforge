FCC-06F defines the authoritative civilisation, settlement, civic-structure and infrastructure canon for the **Impossible Deep**.

It owns the realm-local definition of:

- major civilisation foundations;
- settlement identity and diversity;
- cultural adaptation to Deep conditions;
- route ownership, maintenance and legal relationships;
- portal-control versus territorial-control boundaries;
- the sixteen principal settlement grammars;
- settlement engineering requirements;
- universal-building adaptation rules;

- agricultural and industrial settlement integration;

- Deep travel infrastructure;
- long-distance logistics;
- refuge and emergency engineering;

# 2. Canon Statement

Impossible Deep is a **civilised realm as well as an extreme environment**.

It contains:

- ancient underworld cultures;
- long-lived settlements;
- pressure-adapted communities;
- forge cities;
- Blackwater settlements;
- fossil archivist societies;
- nomadic tunnel communities;
- Surface-Origin Colonies;
- independent polities;
- diasporas;
- trade routes;
- disputed archaeological territories;
- infrastructure networks;
- agricultural systems;
- industrial complexes;
- ports;
- markets;
- refuges;
- frontier outposts.

The realm must not be treated as a wilderness dimension containing only ruins and monsters.

Its civilisations survive because they have learned to:

- engineer around pressure;
- build for seismic change;
- use redundant routes;
- compartmentalise settlements;
- manage atmosphere;
- maintain food production;
- exploit or avoid gravity anomalies;
- navigate Fold Shifts;
- live beside dangerous native fauna;
- rebuild after geological change.

# 3. Civilisation Diversity Rule

Impossible Deep is not dominated by one canonical underworld empire.

Instead, it contains many:

- cultures;
- city-states;
- kingdoms;
- republics;
- guild polities;
- temple communities;
- archive societies;
- nomadic confederations;
- trading leagues;
- frontier settlements;
- colonial descendants;
- mixed communities.

No single culture is automatically the realm's default people.

A generated settlement must not be treated as culturally interchangeable merely because it shares the same biome or engineering needs as another settlement.

---

# 4. Ancestry, Culture & Adaptation Boundary

**Pressure adaptation does not automatically define ancestry.**

A Deep population may be adapted through:

- biology;
- equipment;
- magic;
- architecture;
- cultural practice;
- long-term acclimatisation;
- mixed systems.

Therefore:

- living in Pressure Gardens does not automatically create one "Pressure Race";
- living in Gravity Wells does not automatically define one gravity species;
- forge-city citizenship does not define ancestry;
- Surface-Origin descendants may become culturally Deep without losing their historical origin.

Ancestry, culture, faction, settlement and realm provenance remain separate data concepts.

# 5. Seven Major Civilisation Foundations

Impossible Deep uses seven broad civilisation foundations for FCC-06 purposes:

1. **Ancient Underworld Cultures**
2. **Pressure Communities**
3. **Forge Cities**
4. **Blackwater Settlements**
5. **Fossil Archivists**
6. **Nomadic Tunnel Communities**
7. **Surface-Origin Colonies**

These are **foundations/archetypes**, not seven mandatory unified factions.

A specific culture may overlap multiple foundations.

Examples:

- a Blackwater Forge City;
- a nomadic Fossil Archivist society;
- a Surface-Origin colony that becomes a Pressure Community;
- an Ancient Underworld culture centred on an Inverted City;
- a Forge City whose government is run by an archivist council.

# 6. Ancient Underworld Cultures

## 6.1 Identity

**Ancient Underworld Cultures** are societies whose historical development is substantially rooted in Impossible Deep rather than being recent arrivals from another realm.

They may include:

- surviving ancient civilisations;
- successor cultures;
- descendants of collapsed empires;
- rediscovered isolated societies;
- cultures that preserved old institutions;
- cultures that intentionally rejected earlier traditions.

## 6.2 Historical Depth

"Ancient" does not mean:

- primitive;
- technologically stagnant;
- politically unified;
- culturally unchanged.

An Ancient Underworld Culture may possess:

- advanced pressure engineering;
- sophisticated seismic science;
- highly developed civic systems;
- ancient magical traditions;
- lost or partially recovered technology.

## 6.3 Settlement Relationship

Ancient Underworld Cultures may inhabit:

- Fossil Metropolises;
- Inverted Cities;
- Forge Cities;
- Blackwater settlements;
- Pressure Garden communities;
- mixed-biome infrastructure networks.

---

# 7. Pressure Communities

## 7.1 Identity

**Pressure Communities** are societies whose civic, economic and environmental life is strongly structured around controlled pressure habitats.

They are especially associated with Pressure Gardens but may occur elsewhere.

## 7.2 Core Practices

Pressure Communities may specialise in:

- pressure-lock maintenance;
- habitat sealing;
- Pressure Bulb agriculture;
- Vault Mollusc husbandry;
- Pressure Pearl aquaculture;
- Pressure Resin production;
- pressure medicine;
- pressure-safe architecture.

## 7.3 Social Diversity

A Pressure Community may be:

- village;
- monastic society;
- trade settlement;
- city-state;
- research enclave;
- agricultural federation;
- clan-based settlement.

The term describes adaptation and civic practice rather than government type.

# 8. Forge Cities

## 8.1 Identity

**Forge Cities** are settlements whose economic and civic identity centres on high-pressure, high-temperature or specialist deep industry.

## 8.2 Core Industries

They may work with:

- Abyssal Iron;
- Ventglass;
- Emberbone;
- Deepfire Ember;
- Fossil Alloys;
- pressure-resistant structures;
- advanced machinery.

## 8.3 Political Diversity

A Forge City may be:

- guild-governed;
- royal;
- republican;
- communal;
- temple-controlled;
- corporate/trade-league controlled;
- factionally divided.

"Forge City" is an industrial/settlement identity, not one unified faction.

## 8.4 Environmental Boundary

Forge Cities must still respect:

- heat;
- ventilation;
- pressure;
- seismic risk;
- food supply;
- worker safety;
- waste handling.

They are not magically immune to their own industry.

# 12. Surface-Origin Colonies

## 12.1 Canonical Name

The older phrase **surface colonies** is retained only as legacy shorthand.

The canonical FCC-06 term is:

> **Surface-Origin Colonies**

## 12.2 Identity

Surface-Origin Colonies are settlements originally founded by peoples from:

- the Overworld;
- another comparatively surface-like realm;
- later cross-realm expeditions.

They entered Impossible Deep through **Deepgates**.

They did not arrive by simply mining downward through Overworld geology.

## 12.3 Cultural Evolution

Over generations, Surface-Origin Colonies may:

- hybridise with Deep cultures;
- adopt native farming;
- change architecture;
- adopt pressure adaptation;
- become culturally Deep;
- retain historical surface traditions.

They are not permanently locked into "outsider" status.

# 13. Mixed & Hybrid Civilisations

The seven civilisation foundations may overlap.

A culture may combine:

- Surface-Origin ancestry/history;
- Pressure Community engineering;
- Forge City economy;
- Fossil Archivist institutions;
- Nomadic route traditions;
- Blackwater maritime identity.

Generation and authored content should prefer layered identities over mutually exclusive "culture classes."

# 14. Sovereignty Boundary

Impossible Deep distinguishes:

- portal control;
- route control;
- resource rights;
- settlement territory;
- archaeological rights;
- water rights;
- political sovereignty.

These are not automatically identical.

For example:

- controlling a Deepgate does not grant ownership of the surrounding continent;
- maintaining an Abyss Lift does not grant all mineral rights beneath it;
- holding a Blackwater dock does not grant ownership of the entire Vault-Sea;
- discovering a Fossil City does not automatically grant legal ownership.

---

# 15. Deepgate Control

A Deepgate may be:

- public;
- privately owned;
- faction controlled;
- neutral;
- toll-based;
- militarised;
- jointly governed;
- restricted by settlement law.

Deepgate ownership concerns portal infrastructure and access.

It does not automatically override:

- local settlement law;
- land ownership;
- route rights;
- archaeological claims;
- resource rights.

# 16. Route Rights

Because Deep geography is dynamic, societies may recognise formal rights and duties over routes.

Canonical route categories include:

- maintained tunnels;
- Abyss Lifts;
- folded routes;
- pressure corridors;
- Blackwater channels;
- Coreworm routes;
- seismic-safe passages;
- bridge systems;
- anchor routes.

## 16.1 Route Rights May Include

- toll collection;
- maintenance duty;
- rescue obligation;
- convoy priority;
- navigation rights;
- seasonal closure;
- emergency closure;
- mapping rights;
- trade guarantees.

## 16.2 Route Rights Do Not Automatically Include

- ownership of all adjacent territory;
- ownership of all resources beneath the route;
- political sovereignty over every traveller;
- control of unrelated Deepgates.

# 17. Coreworm Route Law

A Coreworm tunnel may become:

- public route;
- faction route;
- trade road;
- unstable hazard;
- protected migration corridor;
- contested resource corridor.

- using fresh tunnels;

- redirecting Coreworms.

# 18. Settlement Engineering Canon

Impossible Deep settlements must respond to environmental reality rather than using ordinary Overworld layouts with cosmetic cave walls.

Where relevant, planning must consider:

- pressure class;
- seal integrity;
- compartment isolation;
- structural bracing;
- heat management;
- seismic risk;
- gravity orientation;
- fluid containment;
- breathable atmosphere;
- refuge capacity;
- redundant routes;
- lift access;
- luminous/navigation systems;
- food security;
- Deepgate dependency;
- emergency evacuation.

Not every settlement requires every system at maximum strength.

Requirements depend on biome and local conditions.

---

# 19. Core Settlement Grammar

The realm-wide settlement grammar is:

> **compartmented vertical/deep networks with redundant seals and refuge chambers where environmental conditions require them.**

This may include:

- sealed districts;
- pressure doors;
- refuge rooms;
- emergency reservoirs;
- secondary tunnels;
- multi-level transit;
- structural buttressing;
- lift cores;
- route-marker networks.

Huge stable caverns may permit more open civic spaces.

Compartmentation remains a survival principle, not a visual requirement that every street be a corridor.

---

# 20. Universal Building Adaptation Rule

Impossible Deep reuses universal Leyforge building and settlement systems.

Buildings adapt to Deep conditions rather than creating duplicate "Deep versions" of every universal structure.

Canonical adaptations include:

- open streets → pressure corridors where required;
- wells → sealed reservoirs;
- warehouses → compartmented pressure storage;
- watchtowers → resonance/listening stations where vertical sight is less useful;
- farms → sealed/pressure-compatible farms where required;
- ports → pressure docks/submersible docks;
- roads → tunnels, bridges, lifts or Fold-linked routes.

This is an adaptation layer, not a duplicate building catalogue.

---

# 21. Sixteen Principal Settlement Grammars

Impossible Deep has exactly **sixteen principal settlement grammars** for FCC-06 purposes, grouped as two per top-level biome family.

These are generation and design foundations, not mandatory named settlements.

# 41. Signature Structure Roster

Impossible Deep has exactly **six realm-signature structure families**:

1. **Pressure Sanctuary**
2. **Deep Forge**
3. **Seismic Choir**
4. **Abyss Lift**
5. **Luminous Market**
6. **Worldroot Bastion**

These are distinctive realm structures.

Ordinary civic buildings remain inherited/adapted from universal systems.

# 42. Pressure Sanctuary

## 42.1 Identity

A **Pressure Sanctuary** is a reinforced refuge capable of maintaining controlled pressure and atmosphere around:

- Deepgate arrivals;
- hazardous routes;
- expedition staging areas;
- settlements.

## 42.2 Functions

It may provide:

- decompression;
- pressure adjustment;
- emergency shelter;
- recovery;
- equipment preparation;
- route information;
- temporary habitation.

## 42.3 Portal Boundary

Pressure Sanctuary does **not** replace the Deepgate.

A Sanctuary may contain or support a Deepgate.

The Deepgate remains the portal.

---

# 43. Deep Forge

## 43.1 Identity

The **Deep Forge** is the principal realm-specific high-pressure/high-temperature industrial structure.

## 43.2 Material Roles

It may support specialist processing involving:

- Abyssal Iron;
- Emberbone;
- Ventglass;
- Deepfire Ember;
- Fossil Alloys;
- extreme-pressure recipes.

## 43.3 Universal-System Boundary

The Deep Forge does not replace:

Exact recipe ownership belongs to FCC-06G.

---

# 44. Seismic Choir

## 44.1 Identity

A **Seismic Choir** is a networked resonance structure that listens to, maps and in some configurations influences bounded seismic patterns.

## 44.2 Functions

Possible functions include:

- earthquake warning;
- route mapping;
- structural monitoring;
- Seismic Crystal calibration;
- communication through stone;
- controlled geological work.

## 44.3 System Boundary

A Seismic Choir cannot arbitrarily:

- stop every earthquake;
- rewrite terrain anywhere;
- control all Coreworms;
- nullify seismic hazards realm-wide.

Its effects must be:

- bounded;
- infrastructure-dependent;
- readable.

---

# 45. Abyss Lift

## 45.1 Identity

An **Abyss Lift** is a major internal transport system designed for:

- enormous depth;
- pressure transitions;
- vertical logistics;
- gravity variation.

## 45.2 Possible Implementations

An Abyss Lift may use:

- cable lift;
- counterweight;
- multi-stage pressure elevator;

## 45.3 Portal Boundary

Abyss Lift is strictly **internal realm transportation**.

It is not:

- an Overworld connection;
- an alternate portal;
- a replacement for Deepgates.

# 47. Worldroot Bastion

## 47.1 Identity

A **Worldroot Bastion** is a rare fortress/settlement complex located where an actual:

- Worldroot;
- Verdant-linked root system;
- ancient cross-realm living structure

intersects Impossible Deep.

## 47.2 Functions

A Worldroot Bastion may:

- stabilise local routes;
- support foreign agriculture;
- host diplomacy;

- contain a Deepgate;

# 48. Signature Structure Count Boundary

Impossible Deep intentionally has **six** signature structures rather than matching another realm's count.

No additional structures should be promoted to signature status merely to create numerical symmetry.

Universal structures plus biome/culture adaptations provide the rest of the building catalogue.

---

# 49. Deep Forge vs Seismic Forge

This distinction is canonical:

- **Deep Forge** → settlement/industrial signature structure.
- **Seismic Forge** → dungeon/adventure grammar centred on unstable or contested seismic-industry systems.

They may share technologies.

They are not duplicate names for the same content.

---

# 50. Pressure Sanctuary vs Pressure Vault

This distinction is canonical:

- **Pressure Sanctuary** → active protective settlement/route infrastructure.
