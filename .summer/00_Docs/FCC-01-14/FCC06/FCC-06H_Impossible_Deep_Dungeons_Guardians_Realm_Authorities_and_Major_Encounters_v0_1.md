# 1. Purpose

FCC-06H defines the authoritative dungeon, guardian, major-encounter and Realm Authority canon for the **Impossible Deep**.

It owns:

- the five canonical dungeon families;
- dungeon-specific environmental mechanics;
- dungeon states and persistent aftermath;
- guardian-role definitions;
- non-combat guardian resolution;
- exactly three fixed Realm Authorities;
- authority identities, consequences and resolution principles;
- variable Fossil Sovereign encounters;
- exceptional Coreworm encounters;
- Seismic World-Beast encounters;
- titan-scale fauna boundaries;
- dungeon and authority reward provenance;
- escalation rules;
- major encounter persistence;
- realm-local adventure handoff to events, corruption and registry systems.

FCC-06H does **not** finalise:

- exact boss health;
- exact damage;
- exact combat phase timing;
- final move sets;
- exact loot quantities;
- exact dungeon dimensions;
- final encounter spawn rates;
- final procedural-generation algorithms;
- final dialogue;
- final stable IDs for all guardians;
- final art, animation or sound direction;
- exact quest scripting.

Those are deferred to implementation, quest, art and global registry documents.

# 3. Dungeon Philosophy

Impossible Deep dungeons are not ordinary Overworld ruins placed underground.

Each dungeon family must meaningfully use one or more Impossible Deep systems such as:

- pressure;
- gravity;
- seismic movement;
- Blackwater;
- heat;
- folded routes;
- archaeology;
- environmental engineering.

A dungeon must not depend entirely on arbitrary puzzle logic disconnected from the realm's physical laws.

---

# 4. Dungeon Persistence

Dungeons are persistent world sites.

They may exist in states such as:

- sealed;
- partially open;
- occupied;
- abandoned;
- reclaimed;
- restored;
- exploited;
- fortified;
- flooded;
- collapsed;
- politically controlled;
- Void-corrupted.

Important changes should persist.

A dungeon should not automatically reset to its original untouched state after completion.

---

# 5. Dungeon-State Ownership

Dungeon state may track:

- structural integrity;

# 6. Pressure Vault

**Canonical Dungeon Family:** Pressure Vault  
**Established Stable ID:** `dungeon.realm.deep.pressure_vault`

## 6.1 Identity

A **Pressure Vault** is a sealed or semi-sealed complex whose accessibility and survival conditions depend on controlled chamber pressure.

Potential origins include:

- archive;
- treasury;
- emergency shelter;
- research site;
- biological repository;
- civic shelter;
- pressure-industrial installation.

## 6.2 Core Mechanics

Pressure Vault gameplay may involve:

- pressure locks;
- damaged seals;
- decompression;
- flooded compartments;
- atmosphere routing;
- structural reinforcement;
- pump systems;
- emergency closures.

## 6.3 Failure Model

One damaged wall does not automatically destroy the entire dungeon.

Vaults use bounded compartment logic.

A breach may:

- flood one or more sections;
- change pressure states;
- seal doors;
- disable routes;
- expose hidden chambers;
- trigger emergency machinery.

## 6.4 Adventure Tone

Pressure Vaults can support:

- survival;
- exploration;
- rescue;
- archaeology;
- salvage;
- diplomacy;
- repair.

Not every Pressure Vault is hostile.

# 21. Guardian Philosophy

Guardians are not automatically enemies.

A guardian exists because something is being:

- protected;
- maintained;
- judged;
- regulated;
- inherited;
- contained.

Player interaction may therefore include:

- diplomacy;
- legal access;
- restoration;
- repair;
- succession;
- ritual proof;
- combat.

This principle applies to all five guardian families.

---

# 22. Guardian Identity Boundary

Guardian roles are **roles**, not mandatory biological species.

This prevents unnecessary duplication such as:

- one species named Vault Pressurekeeper;
- another species named Inversion Warden;
- another species named Fossil Custodian.

A guardian may be embodied differently across cultures, historical periods or generated sites.

---

Important guardians may persist across site-state changes.

Possible states include:

- active;
- damaged;
- allied;
- hostile;
- deposed;
- repaired;
- succeeded;
- absent;
- corrupted.

A restored dungeon may therefore retain memory of prior guardian outcomes.

# 24. Fixed Realm Authority Count

Impossible Deep has exactly **three fixed Realm Authorities**:

1. **Pressure Titan**
2. **Blackwater Matriarch**
3. **Fossil Crown**

Do not add a fourth fixed authority merely to represent:

- Coreworms;
- seismic world-beasts;
- Gravity Wells;
- every biome.

The fixed authority roster is intentionally compact.

---

# 25. Realm Authority Definition

A Realm Authority is not merely a large boss.

It is a persistent entity or authority whose condition can meaningfully alter:

- regional law;
- settlement access;
- routes;
- ecology;
- resource rights;
- political legitimacy;
- realm history.

Authority outcomes can persist beyond the encounter.

# 26. Pressure Titan

**Fixed Realm Authority #1**  
**Stable ID:** `boss.realm.deep.pressure_titan`

## 26.1 Identity

The **Pressure Titan** is a colossal Deep entity whose existence and movement are bound to major pressure basins and Coreward/deep geography.

Its movement can alter:

- safe pressure zones;
- caverns;
- trenches;
- routes;
- settlement risk.

## 26.2 Authority Domain

The Pressure Titan represents the realm's **physical environmental authority** dimension.

Its importance comes from world-scale pressure/geological consequences, not merely combat strength.

## 26.3 Awakening / Escalation

Pressure Titan activity may increase through:

- mining;
- war;
- major seismic change;
- Deepfire extraction;
- disturbance of ancient machinery;
- authored regional events.

## 26.4 Resolution Paths

Possible resolution paths include:

- containment;
- guidance;
- soothing;
- anchoring;
- ancient-machine negotiation/mediation;
- route sacrifice;
- symbiotic harvesting;
- combat and defeat.

No single resolution is universally mandatory.

## 26.5 Persistent Consequences

Outcomes may affect:

- pressure basins;
- safe settlement regions;
- mining access;
- trade routes;
- cavern stability;
- cultural relations.

---

# 27. Pressure Titan Scale Boundary

The Pressure Titan's name does not mean it is the only titan-sized being in Impossible Deep.

Other enormous fauna may exist.

However:

- titan size alone does not grant Realm Authority status;
- ordinary species do not become authorities merely by scaling up.

# 36. Authority vs Boss

Not every Realm Authority needs to use a conventional arena-boss structure.

An authority encounter may be:

- negotiation;
- world event;
- multi-stage expedition;
- political crisis;
- engineering operation;
- ritual;
- combat;
- combination.

Combat remains one option, not the universal definition.

---

# 37. Authority Death / Removal

If an authority is killed or permanently removed, the world must react.

Possible consequences include:

- ecological instability;
- political succession;
- route changes;
- pressure changes;
- new resource access;
- loss of protections;
- faction conflict.

No authority should simply disappear and leave the world unchanged.

---

# 38. Authority Assistance

Helping an authority can also reshape the world.

Possible outcomes include:

- safer routes;
- legal access;
- protected nurseries;
- stable pressure systems;
- restored archives;
- new diplomacy;
- controlled extraction.

Assistance is not automatically the "good" outcome.

Every authored outcome may carry trade-offs.

---

# 39. Non-Lethal Progression

Realm progression must remain possible through non-lethal authority outcomes.

Possible progression grants include:

- route access;
- knowledge;
- Deepgate targeting information;
- resource rights;
- specialist recipes;
- authority-specific relics;
- settlement alliances.

No fixed Realm Authority must be killed solely to unlock mandatory realm progression.

- regional political conflict.

---

# 52. Fossil Crown Event Integration

Fossil Crown awakening may instantiate or modify:

- Fossil District Exposure;
- Seismic Swarm;
- Deep Settlement Crisis;
- relic-machine activation.

---

# 53. Coreworm Event Integration

Exceptional Coreworm activity may instantiate:

- Coreworm Migration;
- Seismic Swarm;
- route change;
- resource exposure;
- settlement crisis.

---

# 54. Seismic World-Beast Event Integration

Seismic World-Beast activity may instantiate:

- Seismic Swarm;
- Fold Route Shift;
- Stoneflow Advance;
- migration events.

---

# 55. Void Corruption Boundary

Ordinary dungeons, guardians, authorities and major creatures are not Void-corrupted by default.

Therefore:

- Fossil Crown is not automatically Void;
- Pressure Titan is not automatically Void;
- Blackwater Matriarch is not automatically Void;
- ancient Coreworm is not automatically Void;
- Seismic World-Beast is not automatically Void.

Corrupted forms require genuine Void provenance and are governed further by FCC-06I.

---

# 56. Corrupted Dungeon States

Any dungeon family may receive a genuine Void-corrupted state where justified.

Possible effects include:

- altered inhabitants;
- corrupted machinery;
- broken pressure laws;
- hostile geometry;
- contaminated resources;
- abnormal ecology.

Corruption is an overlay/state unless irreversible transformation justifies new canonical content.

---

# 57. Authority Corruption

A fixed authority may become:

- threatened by corruption;
- partially corrupted;
- resisting corruption;
- corrupted in authored scenarios.

Corruption must not erase the authority's original identity.

A corrupted authority remains recognisably:

- Pressure Titan;
- Blackwater Matriarch;
- Fossil Crown

unless a later explicit transformation canon says otherwise.

---

# 58. Authority Conflict

The three fixed Realm Authorities are not automatically allies or enemies.

Their interests may conflict over:

- routes;
- settlements;
- archaeological awakening;
- ecology;
- resource extraction.

There is no required "boss council."

---

# 59. Authority Knowledge

Deep cultures may know:

- myths;
- partial histories;
- scientific theories;
- false stories;
- political propaganda

about authorities.

The player should not necessarily receive perfect objective knowledge at first contact.

---

# 60. Authority Discovery

Realm Authorities may be discovered through:

- exploration;
- archives;
- settlement politics;
- environmental clues;
- regional events;
- dreams/prophecy where cross-realm systems support it.

They should not all be revealed automatically on first entry.

---

# 61. Major Encounter Pacing

Impossible Deep does not enforce one mandatory linear boss sequence.

Players may encounter:

- dungeons;
- guardians;
- authorities;
- major fauna

in different orders depending on:

- exploration;
- settlement relationships;
- routes;
- world generation;
- progression capability.

Global progression may still require selected knowledge/capabilities.

---

# 62. Realm Climax Boundary

Impossible Deep does not require one singular final "Deep boss" to be considered complete.

Its climax may emerge from:

- authority confrontation;
- Coreward expedition;
- Seismic World-Beast event;
- Fossil Crown awakening;
- Pressure Titan crisis;
- multi-settlement disaster.

The realm supports multiple high-end narratives.

---

# 63. Coreward Major Encounters

Coreward Chasms may host:

- Pressure Titan activity;
- ancient Coreworms;
- Seismic World-Beasts;
- extreme expedition crises;
- lost infrastructure.

Coreward is an endgame-like high-risk region, but not every Coreward site is a boss arena.

---

# 64. Blackwater Major Encounters

Blackwater major encounters may include:

- Matriarch politics;
- Citadel conflicts;
- colossal fauna;
- mineral storms;
- nursery crises.

The Blackwater Matriarch remains the fixed authority, not every large aquatic creature.

---

# 65. Fossil Metropolis Major Encounters

Fossil Metropolis major encounters may include:

- Fossil Crown awakening;
- Fossil Sovereign legitimacy conflicts;
- relic-machine activation;
- contested excavation;
- ancient defence systems.

These can be political, archaeological or combat-focused.

---

# 66. Seismic Major Encounters

Seismic major encounters may include:

- Seismic World-Beast;
- Seismic Forgemaster conflict;
- Seismic Forge failure;
- giant Seismic Caller event;
- Coreworm migration.

These must use established seismic systems.

---

# 67. Gravity Major Encounters

Gravity Wells may host major encounters without requiring a fixed gravity authority.

Possible content includes:

- unstable research sites;
- giant anomaly fauna;
- Inversion Warden conflict;
- collapsed multi-anchor settlements.

This is intentional.

Not every biome needs its own boss god.

---

# 68. Authority Outcome Recording

Authority outcomes should record as needed:

- chosen resolution;
- authority status;
- affected regions;
- route changes;
- settlement consequences;
- faction response;
- reward/knowledge grants;
- ecological effects.

This data must persist across saves.

---

# 69. Guardian Outcome Recording

Important guardian encounters may record:

- active;
- allied;
- defeated;
- restored;
- succeeded;
- corrupted;
- bypassed legally.

This supports later dungeon-state changes.

---

# 70. Dungeon Completion Definition

A dungeon does not need one universal "cleared" flag.

Possible completion states include:

- explored;
- restored;
- allied;
- conquered;
- stabilised;
- excavated;
- rescued;
- politically resolved.

Adventure UX may still surface clear player-facing completion goals.

---

# 71. Replay / Return Value

Persistent dungeons should support reasons to return:

- trade;
- restoration;
- changing ownership;
- new archaeological access;
- regional state changes;
- resource extraction;
- later quest stages.

This is preferable to endlessly respawning identical enemies.

---

# 72. Generated Dungeon Variants

Procedural generation may vary:

- layout;
- history;
- state;
- ownership;
- local hazards;
- guardian embodiment;
- resource provenance;
- current purpose.

The five canonical dungeon families remain recognisable despite variation.

---

# 73. Dungeon Validation

Generated dungeons must validate:

- reachable entrance;
- solvable pressure/gravity state;
- survivable progression path;
- no impossible locked route caused by generation error;
- coherent resource provenance;
- appropriate guardian placement.

Danger remains intentional rather than accidental softlock.

---

# 74. Major Encounter Validation

A generated/selected major encounter must validate:

- ecological/historical provenance;
- sufficient space/topology;
- hazard readability;
- aftermath persistence;
- non-duplication of fixed authority identity.

---

# 75. Multiplayer / Group Compatibility Boundary

Future multiplayer systems may scale encounter:

- enemy coordination;
- objectives;
- environmental workload

without multiplying canonical authorities.

Exactly three fixed Realm Authorities remain exactly three.

---

# 76. Universal Death Boundary

Impossible Deep authorities and dungeons do not redefine death.

Universal Leyforge death/respawn remains authoritative.

A major encounter may influence:

- checkpoint/refuge access;
- route safety;
- recovery difficulty

but not create a realm-specific death law.

---

# 77. Deepgate Boundary

No dungeon or boss resolution creates an alternate normal interdimensional exit.

Even if an ancient site contains:

- lift;
- shaft;
- fold;
- Worldroot;
- machine route

normal realm entry/exit remains **Deepgate-based**.

---

# 78. Reward / Progression Boundary

Major rewards may provide:

- capabilities;
- knowledge;
- legal access;
- settlement alliances;
- recipes;
- routes.

They must not bypass global progression through unexplained "boss key" logic unless deliberately authored.

---

# 79. No Mandatory Kill-Key Design

The game should avoid:

> kill named authority → receive arbitrary portal key

as the universal progression model.

If an authority is involved in progression, the required result may be obtained through multiple legitimate outcomes.

---
