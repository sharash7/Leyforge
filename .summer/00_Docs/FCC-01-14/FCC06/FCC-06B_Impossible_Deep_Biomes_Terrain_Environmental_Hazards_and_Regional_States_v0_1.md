# 1. Purpose

FCC-06B defines the authoritative environmental canon for the **Impossible Deep**.

It owns the realm-local definition of:

- the eight canonical top-level biome families;
- biome identity and biome-bound environmental grammar;
- terrain/topology relationships;
- pressure, gravity, Stoneflow and folded-route environmental expression;
- environmental transitions and biome mosaics;
- the twelve principal hazard families;
- the eight major regional-state families;
- compatibility rules for overlapping regional states;
- readability and fairness rules for severe environmental change;
- environmental aftermath requirements;
- environment-facing generation constraints.

FCC-06B does **not** replace or duplicate the detailed ownership of:

- geology, material families and derived forms — FCC-06C;
- flora, fungi, crops and food ecology — FCC-06D;
- creatures and creature ecology — FCC-06E;
- civilisations, settlements and structures — FCC-06F;
- Blocks, Items, processing and recipes — FCC-06G;
- dungeons, guardians and Realm Authorities — FCC-06H;
- corruption, cross-realm interfaces and wider state integration — FCC-06I;
- final registry/art handoff — FCC-06J.

Where this document references a material, plant, creature, settlement or structure, it does so to define **environmental placement and interaction**, not to duplicate that asset's full canonical record.

# 2. Canon Statement

Impossible Deep uses exactly **eight top-level biome families** for current FCC purposes:

1. **Pressure Gardens**
2. **Inverted Caverns**
3. **Seismic Forests**
4. **Blackwater Vault-Seas**
5. **Emberbone Forges**
6. **Gravity Wells**
7. **Fossil Metropolises**
8. **Coreward Chasms**

These are not eight isolated themed rooms.

They are broad environmental families capable of containing:

- sub-biomes;
- local variants;
- mixed transition zones;
- settlements;
- dungeons;
- resource sites;
- ancient infrastructure;
- environmental-state overlays;
- ecological succession;
- persistent world-change aftermath.

The realm may contain an effectively enormous number of individual instances of these biome families without creating new top-level biome categories.

Future content may add deeper sub-biomes or rare specialised environments while preserving these eight as the current canonical production foundation.

---

# 3. Biome Versus Realm Topology

Impossible Deep's macro-topology is broader than its biome list.

The following are **realm-scale topology concepts**, not additional top-level biomes:

- nested world-caverns;
- pressure layers;
- inverted mountains;
- fossil continents;
- Coreward fracture systems;
- Blackwater vaults;
- Gravity Wells;
- folded strata;
- Stoneflow corridors;
- ancient tunnel networks;
- Coreworm route systems.

A biome may occupy one or more of these structures.

Examples:

- a **Pressure Garden** may occupy the floor and walls of a continent-scale world-cavern;
- a **Seismic Forest** may grow across the side of an inverted mountain;
- a **Fossil Metropolis** may be partially submerged in a Blackwater vault;
- an **Emberbone Forge** may form around a colossal fossil embedded within a Coreward fracture;
- a **Gravity Well** may contain islands of several compatible biome families around different local gravity centres.

Generation and authored content must therefore treat **biome family**, **topology**, **pressure profile**, **gravity profile**, **regional state** and **site occupancy** as related but distinct layers.

# 4. Realm-Wide Environmental Principles

## 4.1 Bounded Physical Impossibility

Impossible Deep must feel physically impossible without requiring unrestricted simulation.

The realm expresses impossible behaviour through:

- explicit pressure profiles;
- explicit gravity profiles;
- bounded route folds;
- validated Stoneflow-capable geology;
- authored/deterministic seismic transitions;
- save-persistent regional states.

The realm must not rely on:

- per-voxel arbitrary gravity;
- continuous world-scale fluid dynamics;
- uncontrolled random teleportation;
- unbounded terrain rewriting;
- invisible lethal transitions;
- random settlement deletion.

## 4.2 Environmental Readability

Dangerous environmental states should expose readable cues where practical.

Possible cues include:

- rock cracking;
- debris orientation;
- fluid behaviour;
- changing fungal or plant posture;
- Seismic Crystal reaction;
- Seismic Choir data;
- creature behaviour;
- pressure gauges;
- structural alarms;
- route markers;
- sound or vibration;
- NPC forecasts and warnings;
- historical maps and risk records.

Uncertainty remains part of exploration, but routine danger should be learnable rather than arbitrary.

## 4.3 Persistent Geography

Significant environmental changes leave persistent evidence where appropriate.

Examples include:

- newly exposed tunnels;
- collapsed routes;
- reinforced settlements;
- rerouted lifts;
- altered Blackwater coastlines;
- opened fossil districts;
- new mining faces;
- Stoneflow barriers;
- abandoned or reclaimed refuges;
- updated route maps.

World change is history, not temporary spectacle.

## 4.4 No Automatic Void Attribution

Native Impossible Deep phenomena remain native unless actual Void provenance is established.

The following are not automatically corruption:

- inverted gravity;
- folded distance;
- Stoneflow;
- Blackwater darkness;
- fossil megastructures;
- impossible scale;
- seismic activity;
- pressure anomalies;
- Coreward heat;
- ancient intelligences.

Void corruption is a separate external state family governed by FCC-06I and later global Void canon.

# 5. Biome Family 01 — Pressure Gardens

## 5.1 Core Identity

**Pressure Gardens** are enormous enclosed cavern ecologies where dense atmosphere, mineral-rich water and sustained pressure support giant soft-bodied plants, specialised producer organisms, filter fauna, giant molluscs and pressure-adapted agriculture.

They are among the realm's clearest examples of pressure acting as an ecological foundation rather than only a danger.

## 5.2 Environmental Grammar

Typical Pressure Garden terrain may include:

- broad wet cavern floors;
- mineral pools;
- pressure-fed springs;
- dense biological mats;
- giant soft-bodied plant masses;
- swollen root/bulb structures;
- pressure chambers created by natural constrictions;
- mollusc beds;
- resin-rich growth zones;
- stepped agricultural terraces;
- sealed habitation pockets.

Pressure Gardens are not required to be uniformly wet or uniformly safe.

## 5.3 Pressure Behaviour

Pressure Gardens commonly occupy stable-to-high pressure environments.

Transitions into or out of them may require:

- pressure locks;
- controlled chambers;
- gradual tunnels;
- engineered barriers;
- biological adaptation;
- appropriate equipment.

## 5.4 Canonical Resource/Ecology Hooks

Environmental placement supports:

- Pressure Pearl-producing fauna;
- Pressure Resin flora;
- Pressure Bulbs;
- Dense Reed;
- Vault Molluscs;
- Pressure Crabs;
- specialist native agriculture.

Detailed material and creature definitions are owned by FCC-06C through FCC-06E.

## 5.5 Settlement Affinity

Primary settlement grammars:

- Pressure Garden Community;
- Biological Farm / Pearl Settlement.

Settlement planning commonly prioritises:

- seals;
- pressure stability;
- controlled water;
- aquaculture;
- biological farming;
- refuge chambers.

## 5.6 Typical Hazards

Common hazard families include:

- Pressure Exposure;
- Toxic Atmosphere & Gas;
- Blackwater/Fluid Hazard in wetter variants;
- Isolation & Provision Strain where routes are sealed;
- Structural Collapse where pressure boundaries fail.

# 6. Biome Family 02 — Inverted Caverns

## 6.1 Core Identity

**Inverted Caverns** are chamber systems in which stable local gravity draws matter toward a ceiling, wall or other non-conventional orientation.

They may contain:

- ceiling mountains;
- upside-down settlements;
- upward waterfalls;
- inverted mines;
- ceiling forests;
- orientation bridges;
- dangerous gravity-transition zones.

## 6.2 Environmental Grammar

Generation may use:

- massive ceiling landmasses;
- hanging/inverted valleys;
- suspended rivers or waterfalls that follow local gravity;
- multi-orientation cave networks;
- anchor bridges spanning gravity boundaries;
- debris bands revealing local pull;
- orientation crystals or natural gravity markers.

## 6.3 Gravity Rule

Inverted gravity is an explicit regional profile.

It must not be implemented as arbitrary per-block gravity.

Gravity transition zones should be readable through:

- falling debris;
- plant orientation;
- water direction;
- architecture;
- route markers;
- Orientation Crystal systems;
- movement feedback.

## 6.4 Geology Hook

**Inverted Stone** is not a separate full material family.

It is a gravity-conditioned Pressure Stone facies/state whose fracture, orientation or use may reflect prolonged inverted conditions.

## 6.5 Settlement Affinity

Primary settlement grammars:

- Inverted City;
- Anchor-Bridge Settlement.

## 6.6 Typical Hazards

Common hazards include:

- Gravity Instability & Shear;
- Structural Collapse & Rockfall;
- Folded-Route Instability;
- Darkness & Visibility;
- Seismic Instability.

# 8. Biome Family 04 — Blackwater Vault-Seas

## 8.1 Core Identity

**Blackwater Vault-Seas** are true subterranean oceans occupying enormous sealed or semi-sealed geological vaults under extreme pressure.

They are not merely flooded caves.

They may contain:

- abyssal trenches;
- drowned ruins;
- mineral storms;
- pressure brines;
- submerged fossil structures;
- cliff settlements;
- seabed settlements;
- bioluminescent navigation ecologies;
- enormous migratory fauna.

## 8.2 Fluid Canon

Blackwater is **not** a separate magical base fluid.

It is a regional seawater/brine environment whose gameplay profile may include:

- extreme pressure;
- unusual dissolved minerals;
- low/no light;
- dangerous chemistry;
- powerful currents;
- specialist ecology.

Universal water/fluid systems remain authoritative.

## 8.3 Environmental Grammar

- vent fields;
- drowned districts;

# 10. Biome Family 06 — Gravity Wells

## 10.1 Core Identity

**Gravity Wells** are vast spherical, irregular or multi-centred chambers whose local gravitational structure differs sharply from ordinary cavern space.

They may contain:

- multiple local gravity centres;
- floating or orbit-like debris;
- compressed matter;
- gravity-conditioned geology;
- anomalous route geometry;
- research settlements;
- unusual fauna;
- multi-anchor infrastructure.

## 10.2 Gravity Behaviour

Gravity Wells use explicit regional profiles and transition volumes.

Possible configurations include:

- one dominant central pull;
- multiple stable centres;
- weak neutral zones;
- heavy-gravity pockets;
- orientation corridors;
- bounded drift between known states.

The system must remain readable and deterministic enough for navigation, building and save/load.

## 10.3 Material Hooks

Environmental placement supports:

- Gravity Nodules;
- gravity-conditioned Abyssal Iron deposits;
- Orientation/Compressed Seismic Crystal states;

## 10.4 Settlement Affinity

## 10.5 Typical Hazards

Common hazards include:

# 11. Biome Family 07 — Fossil Metropolises

## 11.1 Core Identity

**Fossil Metropolises** are civilisation-scale environments built into, around or through continent-sized fossil structures and ancient urban remains.

They preserve overlapping layers of:

- extinct ecosystems;
- living settlements;
- ancient technology;
- archaeology;
- language and records;
- political succession;
- buried districts;
- cultural claims.

## 11.2 Origin Diversity Rule

Colossal fossils are not automatically dead gods or one universal titan species.

Possible origins include:

- colossal animals;
- primordial organisms;
- extinct intelligent species;
- world-scale symbiotes;
- beings later worshipped as gods;
- unknown organisms whose identity remains unresolved.

The realm may preserve uncertainty.

## 11.3 Environmental Grammar

Generation may include:

- fossil walls large enough to form districts;
- calcified civic chambers;
- ancient habitation strata;
- archaeological excavation fronts;
- buried streets;
- relic machinery;
- reoccupied neighbourhoods;
- sealed chambers exposed by seismic events;
- subterranean archives.

## 11.4 Settlement Affinity

Primary settlement grammars:

- Fossil Archive City;
- Reoccupied Ancient District.

## 11.5 Dungeon Relationship

**Fossil City** remains the canonical dungeon-family name for adventure-scale lost/contested districts inside or associated with this biome.

A Fossil Metropolis biome is not automatically one dungeon.

## 11.6 Typical Hazards

# 12. Biome Family 08 — Coreward Chasms

## 12.1 Core Identity

**Coreward Chasms** are extreme fracture systems approaching the most severe known combinations of heat, pressure, seismic force, collapsing terrain, deep energy and ancient buried movement.

They represent the realm's most dangerous ordinary progression frontier without requiring a mundane planetary core.

## 12.2 No Mundane-Core Requirement

“Coreward” is directional and metaphysical/geological in realm terms.

It does not require that all routes converge on:

- one spherical molten core;
- one final bottom coordinate;
- one ordinary planetary centre.

Coreward geometry may remain impossible.

# 13. Biome Transitions and Mixed Regions

Impossible Deep uses **transitional environmental mosaics** rather than hard biome walls wherever compatible.

Possible transitions include:

- Pressure Garden → Blackwater wetland shelves;
- Seismic Forest → Inverted Cavern ceiling forest;
- Fossil Metropolis → Emberbone Forge fossil-industrial zone;
- Gravity Well → Inverted Cavern orientation corridor;
- Blackwater Vault-Sea → Coreward thermal trench;
- Seismic Forest → Fossil Metropolis overgrown archaeological district;
- Emberbone Forge → Coreward Chasm high-heat fracture;
- Pressure Garden → Fossil Metropolis cultivated ancient chamber.

Transition regions may inherit compatible environmental properties from both parent families.

They do not automatically become ninth or tenth top-level biome families.

## 13.1 Transition Readability

Transitions should be communicated through:

- changing terrain composition;
- fluid behaviour;
- pressure indicators;
- gravity orientation;
- flora/fauna shift;
- architecture;
- lighting;
- sound/vibration;
- map classification.

# 14. Principal Hazard Families

Impossible Deep has exactly **twelve principal hazard families** for current FCC purposes.

These hazards feed appropriate universal systems rather than a single generic “Deep Exposure” meter.

## 14.1 Pressure Exposure

Covers:

- chamber pressure;
- fluid pressure;
- pressure transition stress;
- incompatible habitat conditions.

Counterplay may include:

- suits;
- seals;
- pressure locks;
- Pressure Sanctuaries;
- compatible structures;
- magic;
- biological adaptation.

## 14.2 Toxic Atmosphere & Gas

Covers:

- oxygen-poor chambers;
- volcanic gas;
- mineral vapour;
- decomposing biological gases;
- toxic vent emissions.

Universal breathing/toxin systems remain authoritative.

## 14.3 Geothermal Heat

Covers:

- magma;
- hot rock;
- vents;
- thermal brines;
- forge environments;
- Coreward heat.

**Geothermal Heat** is an environmental hazard.

**Deepfire Ember** is a resource.

They are not interchangeable.

## 14.4 Darkness & Visibility

Covers:

- low/no ambient light;
- Blackwater darkness;
- obscuring minerals;
- smoke/steam;
- visually deceptive depth.

Counterplay may include:

- equipment;
- native bioluminescence;
- route markers;
- Luminous Market infrastructure;
- mapped knowledge;
- seismic/resonance navigation.

## 14.5 Seismic Instability

Covers:

- tremors;
- quakes;
- fault movement;
- resonance surges;
- ground deformation.

Can alter terrain through bounded state transitions.

## 14.6 Structural Collapse & Rockfall

Covers instability arising from:

- excavation;
- damaged supports;
- pressure change;
- Stoneflow;
- Coreworm tunnelling;
- ancient structural failure;
- seismic damage.

This hazard is distinct from seismic instability because structures and tunnels may remain unsafe after a quake ends.

## 14.7 Gravity Instability & Shear

Covers:

- orientation boundaries;
- multiple-centre pull;
- heavy gravity;
- weak gravity;
- bounded gravity drift;
- transition shear.

Counterplay includes route planning, anchoring, equipment and gravity/orientation technology.

## 14.8 Blackwater Pressure & Fluid Hazard

Covers:

- crushing aquatic pressure;
- deep currents;
- mineral storms;
- dangerous brine chemistry;
- low visibility;
- aquatic navigation risk.

Universal fluid systems remain authoritative.

## 14.9 Stoneflow

Covers active deformation/migration of compatible geology.

Stoneflow may:

- close routes;
- open routes;
- bury sites;
- expose resources;
- threaten foundations;
- create new terrain states.

## 14.10 Folded-Route Instability

Covers changes to known intra-realm topological route relationships.

A fold may:

- shorten;
- lengthen;
- close;
- expose a new branch;
- reconnect through another valid Deep route.

It does not become uncontrolled teleportation or inter-realm travel.

## 14.11 Isolation & Provision Strain

Represents the logistical danger of:

- long Deep routes;
- lost supply access;
- sealed habitats;
- limited native agriculture in some regions;
- damaged transport;
- pressure/gravity incompatibility.

It feeds universal food, storage, settlement-needs, logistics and trade systems rather than creating a new hunger meter.

## 14.12 Void Corruption

Represents genuine external Void provenance.

Void Corruption is not inferred merely because a native Deep phenomenon appears strange, hostile or impossible.

Detailed corruption ownership belongs to FCC-06I.

---

# 15. No Generic Deep Exposure Meter

Impossible Deep must not collapse all environmental danger into a single permanent realm meter.

Examples:

- pressure uses survival/environment systems;
- toxic gas uses breathing/toxin systems;
- heat uses temperature/exposure systems;
- darkness uses visibility/navigation systems;
- gravity uses movement/orientation systems;
- collapse uses terrain/structure hazards;
- isolation uses needs/logistics systems;
- Void corruption uses corruption systems.

This preserves meaningful preparation and allows different regions to be dangerous for different reasons.

# 16. Major Regional State Families

Impossible Deep has exactly **eight major realm-specific regional-state families** for current FCC purposes.

These are environmental overlays, not extra biomes.

## 16.1 Stable Strata

Baseline known environmental condition.

Characteristics may include:

- mapped routes;
- expected pressure;
- stable gravity;
- ordinary local seismic behaviour;
- known settlement access.

Stable does not mean harmless.

## 16.2 Pressure Surge

A regional change in pressure relationships.

Possible consequences:

- formerly safe chambers become hazardous;
- seals become stressed;
- new pressure-compatible routes open;
- biological activity changes;
- Blackwater boundaries move.

## 16.3 Seismic Swarm

Sustained elevated tremor/fault activity.

Possible consequences:

- route change;
- resource exposure;
- creature movement;
- structural risk;
- Stoneflow activation;
- Fossil District Exposure.

## 16.4 Gravity Drift

A bounded change in one or more validated gravity profiles.

Possible consequences:

- route reorientation;
- changed lift operation;
- debris migration;
- settlement access change;
- anomaly-resource exposure.

## 16.5 Stoneflow Cycle

A regional state in which Stoneflow-capable geology becomes active.

Possible consequences:

- gradual terrain migration;
- buried or exposed infrastructure;
- foundation stress;
- new mining opportunity;
- route closure/opening.

## 16.6 Blackwater Upwelling

A major change in Blackwater brine/current/mineral/ecological conditions.

Possible consequences:

- changed coastline or water level;
- mineral concentration;
- fauna migration;
- pressure change;
- new submerged/accessible sites;
- settlement evacuation.

## 16.7 Fold Shift

A known route fold changes between bounded valid topological states.

Possible consequences:

- settlement isolation;
- new shortcut;
- previously hidden district exposure;
- caravan rerouting;
- political change in route control.

## 16.8 Void-Corrupted State

A genuine corruption overlay with actual Void provenance.

It is not a synonym for ordinary Deep instability.

Detailed corruption variants and cleansing/restoration rules belong to FCC-06I.

---

# 17. Regional-State Combination Rules

Compatible regional states may overlap through explicit validated combinations.

Examples:

- **Seismic Swarm + Stoneflow Cycle** → major geological instability;
- **Pressure Surge + Blackwater Upwelling** → dangerous sea/pressure migration;
- **Gravity Drift + Fold Shift** → severe route-navigation change;
- **Seismic Swarm + Fossil Metropolis site state** → archaeological exposure or structural crisis.

Not every theoretical combination must exist.

State combinations must be:

- authored or validated;
- deterministic enough for persistence;
- save-safe;
- readable;
- bounded in effect.

Incompatible combinations should be rejected, prioritised or resolved explicitly rather than producing contradictory world state.

# 18. Realm Event Families

FCC-06 defines twelve major realm-event families that drive environmental and civilisation-scale change.

Detailed event integration, authorities and corruption ownership are cross-referenced by FCC-06H and FCC-06I.

## 18.1 Pressure Basin Shift

Pressure-safe and hazardous regions change within validated topology.

## 18.2 Seismic Swarm

Sustained tectonic activity affects routes, structures and ecology.

## 18.3 Stoneflow Advance

Active Stoneflow moves through a known region.

## 18.4 Gravity Reorientation

A Gravity Well or other gravity-defined region changes configuration.

## 18.5 Fold Route Shift

A known folded route opens, closes, shortens, lengthens or reconnects.

## 18.6 Blackwater Mineral Storm

Extreme currents, suspended minerals, chemistry and pressure disturbances move through a Vault-Sea.

## 18.7 Coreworm Migration

One or more major Coreworms carve new routes and disturb existing geology or infrastructure.

## 18.8 Seismic Forest Resonance

Large-scale vibration triggers coordinated growth, fruiting, defence, migration or ecological disturbance.

## 18.9 Fossil District Exposure

Geological change reveals a buried district, fossil organism, archive or archaeological site.

## 18.10 Deepfire Vent Eruption

Exceptional thermal/Deepfire activity creates hazards, industrial opportunity and resource exposure.

## 18.11 Deep Settlement Crisis

A civilisation-scale emergency caused by one or more of:

- seal failure;
- route collapse;
- pressure migration;
- food shortage;
- evacuation;
- isolation;
- political response to environmental failure.

## 18.12 Void Fracture

A genuine Void-corruption breach affecting native Deep systems.

This is not an ordinary geological event.

# 19. Event Predictability and Precursors

Major physical events generally expose appropriate warnings or precursor states where practical.

Potential sources include:

- Seismic Crystals;
- Seismic Choirs;
- pressure gauges;
- fluid changes;
- creature migration;
- Resonance Tree behaviour;
- geological sounds;
- repeated micro-tremors;
- NPC observers;
- historical risk maps;
- settlement alarm systems.

Predictions may be incomplete.

The purpose is not to remove danger but to ensure major environmental systems can be learned, prepared for and engineered around.

---

# 20. Event Persistence Contract

Realm events must use bounded, save-persistent state transitions.

An event record may preserve:

- event family;
- cause;
- start state;
- affected region;
- warning/precursor state;
- player intervention;
- affected structures;
- route changes;
- ecology changes;
- resource exposure;
- settlement/faction response;
- resulting state;
- recovery/restoration state;
- long-term aftermath.

Events must not casually regenerate an entire region from scratch in a way that invalidates persistent player/world history.

---

# 21. Authority-Driven Environmental Change

Fixed Realm Authorities and variable titan/world-scale encounters may instantiate or modify existing event families rather than requiring separate realm-wide simulation systems.

Examples:

- **Pressure Titan** movement may drive a Pressure Basin Shift;
- **Blackwater Matriarch** succession or ecological action may interact with Blackwater Upwelling or Deep Settlement Crisis;
- **Fossil Crown** awakening may contribute to Seismic Swarm or Fossil District Exposure;

# 22. Environmental Settlement Safety Rules

Settlements built in Impossible Deep must evaluate relevant environmental conditions rather than assuming universal Overworld-safe terrain.

Possible planning factors include:

- pressure class;
- seal integrity;
- structural support;
- seismic risk;
- Stoneflow risk;
- gravity orientation;
- heat;
- toxic atmosphere;
- water/fluid pressure;
- refuge capacity;
- route redundancy;
- lift dependency;
- food/habitat compatibility.

The planner must not require every settlement to solve every hazard.

It must require settlements to solve the hazards actually present at their site.

Detailed settlement grammar belongs to FCC-06F.

A valid Impossible Deep generation system should support:

1. all eight canonical biome families;

3. mixed transitions and mosaics;

# 25. Effectively Vast / Infinite-Realm Compatibility Rule

FCC-06B is deliberately compatible with the Impossible Deep being experienced as an **effectively infinite giant underground dimension**.

Current FCC completeness does not require:

- a final mapped boundary;
- a final deepest cavern;
- exhaustive enumeration of every region;
- one canonical ultimate bottom;
- a finite number of cities or world-caverns.

The eight biome families act as production/generation foundations capable of supporting additional procedural or authored depth later.

Future exploration may add:

- new sub-biomes;
- rarer transitions;
- distant civilisations;
- unknown ecological zones;
- deeper historical layers;
- extreme geological phenomena;
- further Coreward content;

provided those additions respect FCC ownership and do not casually create contradictory new top-level realm laws.

# 26. Implementation Feasibility Rule

The canon defines intended player-facing behaviour, not sacred low-level simulation technique.

If a literal implementation of:

- pressure;
- gravity;
- Stoneflow;
- folded distance;
- cavern scale;
- seismic terrain change;
- Blackwater fluid behaviour

would be too expensive, brittle or incompatible with the Godot/Summer Engine production architecture, implementation may simplify the mechanism while preserving:

- readable cause/effect;
- player counterplay;
- deterministic state;
- save persistence;
- biome identity;
- settlement consequences;
- progression meaning.

The implementation may not solve feasibility by deleting the core environmental identity of the realm.
