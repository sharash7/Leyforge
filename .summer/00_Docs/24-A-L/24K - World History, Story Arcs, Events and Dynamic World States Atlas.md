# **Document Purpose**

This document is the history, narrative, event, and world-state volume of Document Set 24, the World Content Atlas. It turns the places, peoples, ecologies, dungeons, bosses, structures, resources, routes, and realms defined by Documents 24A-24J into generated pasts, present tensions, future event possibilities, story-arc relationships, and persistent consequences.

The Atlas owns authored history foundations, story grammars, event families, state profiles, rumours, evidence types, aftermath modules, and cross-content selection rules. It does not replace the Quest and Event System, NPC simulation, combat, world generation, economy, magic, or technical documents. Those systems execute and present runtime activity; 24K defines the world-content relationships they select and preserve.

This volume formally supersedes all POC-only narrative arrangements. Reusable mechanics such as request generation, event escalation, consequence tracking, quest negotiation, raid warning, environmental evidence, and persistent aftermath remain valid. Any named POC villager, fixed watchtower sequence, guaranteed goblin attack, scripted mana discovery, or demonstration-only chronology is archived and excluded from normal production generation.

## **1.1 What This Atlas Is Not**

- A fixed main quest that always uses the same people, settlement, villain, dungeon, or realm order.

- A procedural text generator allowed to invent participants, locations, resources, or outcomes not present in the world.

- A calendar of random pop-ups disconnected from simulation conditions.

- A universal morality meter that declares one outcome correct without culture, law, consent, ecology, and consequence.

- A reset system that erases wars, disasters, deaths, restorations, ownership changes, or ecological aftermath for convenience.

- A requirement that every historical claim has one immediately discoverable objective answer.

- A pressure system that punishes players for building, exploring, resting, or ignoring content without configurable pacing and warnings.

# **5. World Chronology and Relationship Model**

The chronology is a graph of incidents and consequences rather than a decorative list of dates. Each world begins with seed-derived physical and realm conditions, then resolves compatible historical layers whose outputs become inputs for current regions, settlements, factions, ruins, resources, languages, routes, and unresolved stories.

| **Layer**                   | **Required Content**                                                                                    | **Purpose**                                                  |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Foundational Conditions** | World shape, climate, realm network, leylines, primordial ecologies, ancient structures.                | Constrains what histories can occur.                         |
| **Historical Actors**       | Cultures, polities, factions, institutions, creatures, bosses, realm powers, natural forces.            | Must reference generated or authored identities.             |
| **Incident Chain**          | Cause -\> action -\> resistance -\> resolution -\> aftermath -\> later reinterpretation.                | Supports multi-step causality without a fixed novel plot.    |
| **Spatial Scope**           | Site, settlement, route, region, continent, Overworld, realm, cross-realm.                              | Controls propagation, evidence density, and simulation cost. |
| **Temporal Scope**          | Moment, season, generation, era, ancient age, ongoing cycle.                                            | Controls dating precision and living memory.                 |
| **Present-Day Outputs**     | Claims, borders, ruins, traditions, taboos, technologies, shortages, hostilities, alliances, mysteries. | Feeds world generation and active story selection.           |

# **6. Cosmology, Origins, Objective Truth and Cultural Belief**

The Atlas distinguishes developer-level world truth from in-world belief. Some physical and historical facts may be objectively stored; their causes, meanings, divine interpretations, and moral legitimacy may remain disputed. A realm can have measurable laws while cultures disagree about who created it or what those laws demand.

| **Knowledge Class**                  | **Storage Rule**                                                             | **Example**                                                      |
|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Objective World Fact**             | Engine-confirmed state needed for coherent simulation.                       | A portal opened in a recorded era; a city lies beneath the lake. |
| **Best-Supported History**           | High-confidence reconstruction from several independent evidence sources.    | Trade records, matching ruins, and dated materials agree.        |
| **Cultural Tradition**               | Community memory expressing identity, meaning, warning, or legitimacy.       | Founding song, ancestor account, sacred migration.               |
| **Faction Claim**                    | Interested interpretation supporting territory, law, authority, or conflict. | Dynastic right, heroic victory, enemy betrayal.                  |
| **Religious / Philosophical Belief** | Meaning applied to world events without requiring system confirmation.       | Storm as judgement, realm breach as pilgrimage sign.             |
| **Rumour / Folklore**                | Low-certainty claim that may contain clues, distortions, or fiction.         | Sleeping titan below a hill; cursed caravan road.                |
| **Deliberate Fabrication**           | Known or suspected misinformation created by an actor.                       | Forged charter, staged prophecy, false casualty report.          |

# **16. Story Architecture and Narrative Relationship Graph**

A story arc is an authored causal grammar bound to generated content. It stores roles, stakes, hidden information, escalation, discovery, possible objectives, branch conditions, resolutions, aftermath, and replacement rules. It must never assume a participant or location that the seed did not create.

# **19. Branching, Convergence, Replacement, Fallback and Continuity**

| **Rule**             | **Production Requirement**                                                                                                          |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Branch**           | Meaningful choice changes beneficiary, method, risk, knowledge, timing, ownership, or world outcome.                                |
| **Convergence**      | Branches may share later structural needs without erasing earlier consequences or pretending choices were identical.                |
| **Replacement**      | If an NPC dies, faction dissolves, site is destroyed, or route closes, the arc may transfer to a successor or institutional record. |
| **Closure**          | Some arcs become permanently impossible; the journal records why instead of silently respawning content.                            |
| **Fallback**         | Critical capability guarantees may generate alternate access, but never force the original story or resurrect invalid actors.       |
| **Continuity Audit** | Every transition verifies referenced entity state, ownership, location, role, and saved history before advancement.                 |

# **51. Balancing, Ethics, Anti-Manipulation and Quality Rules**

| **Quality Rule**             | **Requirement**                                                                                                                       |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **No Ancestry Determinism**  | No ancestry is automatically villainous, primitive, warlike, magical, criminal, or historically guilty.                               |
| **Civilian Consequence**     | Wars, sieges, disasters, extraction, conquest, and boss events include affected non-combatants and recovery.                          |
| **No Fake Urgency**          | Timers and warnings reflect actual event rules; the UI does not manipulate the player with false deadlines.                           |
| **Respectful History**       | Genocide, enslavement, persecution, disease, trauma, colonisation, and cultural erasure require deliberate review and are not filler. |
| **Consent and Personhood**   | Spirits, constructs, undead persons, intelligent creatures, and realm peoples retain agency and legal/moral treatment.                |
| **Configurable Consequence** | Players control event frequency, severity, permanent loss, NPC death, settlement destruction, and recovery assistance.                |
| **Readable Causality**       | Major state changes identify causes, contributors, knowledge limits, and future effects.                                              |
| **No Infinite Crisis Loop**  | Cooldowns, density budgets, recovery states, relief systems, and protected windows prevent permanent exhaustion.                      |

# **Appendix F. Production Acceptance Criteria**

| **Godot/Summer Alignment** | No Unreal-only requirement remains; registries, resources, tools, runtime records, and validation match the current engine direction. |
| **Content Quality**        | Sensitive themes are reviewed; ancestry determinism, arbitrary punishment, fake urgency, and endless crisis loops are prohibited.     |
